# Running a Lodestar that other people can reach

This page is for the person who makes a Lodestar instance reachable by others: through its web interface, its API, a Torznab feed or an integration. Publishing the software and running a service are different things. The makers do the first; if you do the second, the duties below may fall on you. **This is guidance, not legal advice.** What applies to you depends on where you and your users are and on what your service does; ask a lawyer before you offer an index to others.

## 1. Private is not the same as exempt
Lodestar is designed for a personal instance behind a password, reached over a LAN or a VPN (user guide, chapter 3). Authentication and private networking reduce exposure. Which duties apply depends on who uses the instance, what it does, what data it processes and the law that applies. A genuinely personal or household installation may qualify for exemptions that don't apply to a service offered to others, whether customers, colleagues or members, even when they need a password to get in.

## 2. Keep a working contact
Give the people who use or are affected by your service a way to reach you that you read: an email address or a form, on the page itself or in the feed's description. Monitor it. Failing to maintain the contact and notice procedures a law requires can cost you protections that would otherwise be available. Public issues on the Lodestar project don't reach you and aren't a substitute.

## 3. Handle complaints about indexed items
- **Removing an item.** Use **Block this…** on the item, in the search results or on its detail page, and choose the narrowest rule, which is the torrent's hash (§20.2). Know what each action does:
  - **Hide** (the default) applies to the stored record and to any future copy: it leaves ordinary results, feeds and exports, but the record stays in the database and an authenticated user can still retrieve it, for example with a search that asks for hidden items.
  - **Drop** only prevents the torrent from being stored or fetched again. **It leaves a record that is already stored as it is.** Use it together with Hide, not instead of it.
  - **Report abuse** is for child-exploitation material only: it deletes the stored record at once and adds the hash to the locked floor.
  - There is no general delete-now action for a stored record yet **(planned)**. For a takedown today, Hide the hash, verify that nothing you offer still serves it (web UI, API keys you issued, feeds), and keep anything you are legally required to retain.
- **Backups undo takedowns.** Restoring a backup restores the filters as they were when it was taken (user guide §3.7). After a restore, reapply every takedown made since, and keep your own list of them outside the data folder.
- **Copyright notices (United States).** An index is an "information location tool" under 17 U.S.C. §512(d). An operator **may qualify** for its safe harbour, which limits monetary liability for linking to infringing material. Among other conditions: lacking actual knowledge or awareness of apparent infringement, or acting expeditiously to remove or disable access on obtaining that knowledge or awareness (a valid notice is one trigger, not the only one); no financial benefit from infringement the operator has the right and ability to control; a designated agent registered with the US Copyright Office (online, $6 per filing, renewed every three years) whose contact is published; a reasonably implemented repeat-infringer policy; and accommodating, and not interfering with, "standard technical measures" as §512(i) defines them, which does not mean installing every filter a complainant asks for. Losing the safe harbour doesn't by itself make you liable, and a disclaimer gives none of its protection. See https://www.copyright.gov/512/ and https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/.
- **Keep a record** of notices received and what you did, without storing the complained-of content.

## 4. Child-exploitation material
Lodestar's child-safety core runs on every source and its floor can't be switched off, but no filter is complete.
- **Anyone:** if you learn of suspected material, don't obtain it to investigate, don't share it, and don't send it to Lodestar's maintainers or post it in an issue. Block the hash, and report it to the reporting authority: in the United States the NCMEC CyberTipline (https://report.cybertip.org), elsewhere your national hotline (https://www.inhope.org lists them). Follow its instructions. Lodestar's maintainers can't act on it: they don't operate your instance and can't tell who published the material.
- **Covered providers:** if your service is a "provider" of an electronic communication or remote computing service under 18 U.S.C. §2258E, then under §2258A you must report apparent violations **to the CyberTipline specifically**, as soon as reasonably possible after you gain actual knowledge, and you must **preserve** the contents of each report for one year, including reasonably accessible contextual material, handled securely. A report to the police alone doesn't satisfy this. The statute does not require you to monitor or scan proactively. Disabling public access or dropping a record must not destroy material you're obliged to preserve. Whether a public Lodestar is a provider depends on what it offers; find out before you go public.
- **Intimate images.** The TAKE IT DOWN Act obliges a "covered platform" to offer a notice-and-removal process and, within 48 hours of a valid request, to remove non-consensual intimate imagery **and make reasonable efforts to remove known identical copies**. An index of metadata may or may not be a covered platform; check against the FTC's guidance (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act).

## 5. Personal data
A public instance processes personal data: visitors' IP addresses in access logs, searches if you turn the query log on, API keys, and any account you create for others.
- Keep the query log **off** unless you need it, and clear it when you don't.
- Decide how long logs live, and write it down.
- **EU and UK.** If the EU or UK GDPR applies to you, an operator who decides why and how personal data is processed will generally be a controller, with the duties that follow. Scope, the household exemption and processor relationships all matter; the ICO's guidance on who the UK GDPR applies to is a good start (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/who-does-the-uk-gdpr-apply-to/).
- **Colorado.** The Colorado Privacy Act applies to covered entities, including nonprofits, with a Colorado nexus that process the personal data of 100,000 or more Colorado consumers a year, or of 25,000 or more while earning revenue or discounts from selling personal data, subject to the Act's exclusions (https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/). Colorado's security, disposal and breach-notification laws may apply even when those thresholds aren't met; they have their own definitions of covered entities and information, and an exposure of IP addresses or search logs alone doesn't automatically trigger them. A covered breach requires notice to affected residents without unreasonable delay and generally within 30 days of determining that it occurred, subject to statutory exceptions, and notice to the Attorney General when 500 or more Colorado residents are affected (https://coag.gov/resources/data-protection-laws/). Other states have their own rules.
- **Minors.** Lodestar is for adults, but saying so settles nothing by itself. Colorado's protections for minors' online data (SB24-041, in force since 2025-10-01) turn on actual knowledge or wilful disregard that minors use the service. COPPA concerns commercial services directed to children under 13, or actual knowledge of collecting personal information from them (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). Hypothetical access by a minor isn't the whole test; knowing about it changes your position.

## 6. If you changed the code: AGPL section 13
If you modify Lodestar and let users interact with your modified version over a network, the AGPL's section 13 requires you to prominently offer those users access to its Corresponding Source, including your modifications, without charge. A link to the upstream repository isn't enough, because it doesn't contain your changes. Publish your modified source and link it from your instance.

## 7. Private sources stay private
Items found only through private trackers, Usenet or your own download clients are flagged `private_source` and are never served over Torznab, included in shareable exports or sent into public peer finding. Don't work around this: the trackers' rules usually forbid redistribution, and a public lookup leaks the hash.

## 8. Describe the service accurately
Describe the service and its lawful uses accurately. Don't promote, demonstrate or support it as a way to obtain unauthorised copies of anything. Keep the rule packs on, in particular the unauthorised-copy pack: on a public instance they remove the most obvious unauthorised copies before anyone sees them, which also lowers your red-flag exposure under §512(d). A lawful-use notice doesn't neutralise conduct that encourages infringement; that conduct is what decided the cases against isoHunt and LimeWire, and it is the line the Supreme Court drew again in *Cox v. Sony* (2026).
