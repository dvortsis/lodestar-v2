# WK-7 decisions (store-free worker stages, claim batching, bounded outbound bytes)

Plan `docs/plan/2026-11-06-v06-workers.md` §4.3 WK-7 and Q4/Q5; architecture §19.2, §19.4; `docs/owner-decisions.md` §1.6.5 (a worker uses ≤ 256 MB of RAM). Builds on WK-0 (`docs/decisions/2026-11-06-WK-0.md`).

## The builder
- **`lodestar_pipeline::stages::WorkerStages` builds the fetch pool, the prepare stage and the worker's funnel with no `Store`.** It takes a `Preparer`, a filter `Handle`, a `Reports` handle for terminal losses, the worker's `WorkerLeases` and its counters, and spawns the stages into any `Outbox<Results>`. `Pipeline::start_with` builds the built-in worker's stages with it too (`Queues::local()`, `WorkerId::LOCAL`, the `Coordinator` as `Reports`). The intake stays in `lib.rs`: on a single machine it claims straight from the lease authority, and a remote worker's intake (claims over the session) is WK-10's.
- **`Preparer::from_dict(dict, level, archive_raw)`** (the one shared edit in `crates/store/src/lib.rs`) builds a preparer from one dictionary's id and bytes. It doesn't care where they came from: the store on the coordinator, WK-8's snapshot on a worker. With the store's active dictionary, level and `archive_raw`, it prepares exactly the bytes `Store::preparer()` does (`a_preparer_from_the_dictionary_matches_the_stores`, with and without a dictionary and with archiving on). `Preparer::dict_id()` says which dictionary its blobs name, for WK-8's "every blob names its dictionary id" check. The built-in worker still uses `Store::preparer()`, so nothing changes on a single machine.
- **`Reports` is the claim/report interface's report half:** `lost(key, token, loss) -> bool`. The `Coordinator` implements it with the direct call it made before (plan Q4). A remote worker queues an `Outcome::Lost` for its session; it can't know yet whether the token was current, so it says true and the coordinator fences it (WK-0). The prepare stage's and the funnel's calls go through `Arc<dyn Reports>`: one indirect call per lost key.
- **The claim half is `next_claims`:** it waits for a claim and gathers more for the same batch, at most 512 keys (`MAX_CLAIM_BATCH`) and at most 250 ms after the first (§19.2). It's generic over the item, so WK-10 can batch `ClaimItem`s for `WorkerSession::claim`. The built-in worker keeps claiming key by key (plan Q4: the authority is a function call away, so batching would only add latency).
- **`Results` and `WorkerLeases` are public** (re-exported from `stages`), and `Results.worker` is the builder's worker id instead of always `LOCAL`. WK-6's codec encodes what a remote worker sends; the writer still gets `Results` in-process.

## Bounded bytes on a worker (owner §1.6.5)
- **`byte_queue(bytes, count)`** is a queue capped by the bytes its items hold (`Weigh`, an upper estimate with the heap) as well as by count. A sender waits for room, so a full queue pauses whoever feeds it. An item keeps its bytes until the `Held` the receiver hands out is dropped, so what the consumer is still working on counts too. An item larger than the cap waits for the queue to empty, then passes alone (as WK-0's queues do). Dropping the receiver closes it for waiting senders.
- **Outbound:** a remote worker's prepare stage sends into a `byte_queue` of `OUTBOUND_BYTES` (8 MiB, plan §4.3) that the session task drains (WK-10). When the link stalls, the queue fills, the prepare stage waits, the fetched queue fills, and the pool's fetches wait on their output: **fetching pauses instead of memory growing**. `a_stalled_link_pauses_fetching` shows it: with the queue unread, it stays within its cap and the pool stops at what the two capped queues and its sockets can hold (28 fetches out of 400 found, against a bound of 40; with the fetched queue uncapped, 130); once the link moves, all 400 arrive once each.
- **Fetched info dicts are capped too on a worker (`Queues::worker()`):** the pool's 64-slot channel holds up to 64 dicts of up to 16 MiB each (`FetchOptions::max_metadata`), 1 GiB in the worst case. A worker's pool channel holds one, and a forwarder moves each into a `byte_queue` of `FETCHED_BYTES` (4 MiB). An item keeps its bytes while it's prepared and until its result is sent, so the prepare batch is inside the cap. The built-in worker keeps today's 64-slot channel (`Queues::local()`), as the coordinator has no such budget.
- **What the stages add to plan Q5's estimate, against the owner's 256 MB** (§1.6.5, read as 10⁶ bytes, the stricter reading). Q5 already counts the outbound queue (8 MiB) and "prepare and filter snapshot ≤ 40 MiB", which takes the prepared rows: at most about 3× their dicts (each file becomes a `FileInfo` with its own strings), 12 MiB for a 4 MiB fetched queue. The stages add the fetched queue itself. Q5 with a have-filter swap is 223 MiB; with the fetched queue, 227 MiB, about 238 MB. `the_worker_queues_fit_the_budget` checks the sum.
- **The estimate assumes no dict is larger than `FETCHED_BYTES` (4 MiB).** A `byte_queue` lets an item over its cap through alone, so the fetched queue's real cap is `max(FETCHED_BYTES, max_metadata)`. With today's 16 MiB `max_metadata`, one oversized dict passes the fetched queue, its prepared rows (about 48 MiB) wait in prepare, and its result passes the outbound queue alone: about 64–112 MiB above the estimate. **WK-10 caps it:** a worker `max_metadata` of at most `FETCHED_BYTES`, or a budget that covers the oversized case. The budget test pins the assumption.
- **Not bounded by WK-7, and capped by its own slice, `X-fetch-pool-bytes` (runner, 2026-11-06):** dicts the pool holds outside the stages. One in the pool's channel, one in the forwarder's hand, and the dict of every fetch that finished while the pool waits on its output (up to `max_sockets`, each up to `max_metadata`). At today's defaults (400 sockets, 16 MiB) that's about 6.3 GiB in the worst case, against about 18 MB of headroom, so a count cap isn't enough: the pool needs a cap on the bytes it holds in finished fetches. The same unbounded hold affects single-machine installs today, so the runner made it its own slice rather than WK-10's. Measured RSS goes to the owner under `ext:owner-wkq3` if it's over.

## For WK-10
- **One outbound queue for the worker's life.** Once `out` closes, the prepare stage ends and keys fetched after that keep their leases until the coordinator expires them. The session task owns one long-lived `ByteReceiver` across reconnects (or hands to the spool, WK-11), never one per session.
- **The remote `Reports` queues every loss, unbounded but never dropped** (exactly-once, §19.3). Its length is bounded by the leases the worker holds, which its pool cap bounds (Q5's 50,000 keys).
- **Encode with WK-6's codec in the session task:** `Results::into_record` maps a result to WK-6's `ResultRecord` (only the verdict's action and rule version travel; the coordinator re-judges at commit), and a test round-trips it through `ResultsBatch`. The session encodes batches up to 512 KiB (`MAX_OPAQUE`) and calls `WorkerSession::committed` on `Accepted { accepted: true }`.
- **`next_claims`, `next_batch` and the prepare stage's `collect` aren't cancel-safe:** dropped mid-window, they lose the items gathered so far. Await them to the end, never in a `select!` with shutdown; to stop, close the channel and drain it.
- **Cap the oversized dict** (above), set the worker's pool cap (Q5), and build on `X-fetch-pool-bytes` for the pool's held fetch bytes.

## Checks
- **No fixed sleeps that assume work has finished.** The batching and queue unit tests run on paused time (`start_paused`): their sleeps advance the paused clock to prove a sender is still waiting or a window has passed. The integration tests poll conditions with a 10 s deadline. 40 runs of `tests/stages.rs` in a row: no failure.
- **Parity** (`bench/examples/mem-ingest`, release build, no search, one 4-core machine, `main` at `f907139d` against this branch before the merge of `main`, which brought only WK-6's unused codecs):
  - **Throughput, 60,000 torrents with the source unthrottled, alternating, the order swapped every other pair:** wall time to store all of them was 54.0, 51.7, 48.5 and 63.5 s on `main` (mean 54.4) and 54.5, 53.6, 53.2 and 58.5 s here (mean 54.9). Within noise.
  - **Anonymous memory at the end:** 291–432 MiB on `main` and 345–391 MiB here. That's the spread between runs of one binary.
  - **20,000 torrents at 4,000/s, six alternating pairs:** both stored all 20,000 every time. Stored at 9 s was 13,803–15,141 on `main` and 11,606–15,235 here. These samples are one second apart, so they're too coarse to separate the two, and the throughput runs above decide.
  - The review fixes after these runs touch only the remote worker's path (`Queues::worker`, the forwarder, `Weigh`, `Held`). The built-in worker's code is the same.
