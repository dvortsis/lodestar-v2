# F-rollback-holds decisions

Builds on `2026-10-30-F-rollback-journal.md` (#241) and closes the gap it left under "Not covered: holds", plus O1 and O2 from its review. It refines BB-1, the deletion guarantee (owner decisions §2.16, §2.17, §2.21), and the content-safety holds (§20.3, §20.5.2) for `lodestar rollback` (architecture §12). Nothing here loosens a hold or BB-1, or changes an on-disk format: the rows are written to the existing V4 tables as they are. **Flagged to the owner (§2.19) as content safety.**

- **A rollback keeps the holds recorded after the snapshot.** In the same transaction as #241's purge, `snapshots::carry_holds` merges the current store's `cs_hashes`, `tombstones` and `quarantine` into the copy's, wherever both schemas have them (V4 adds all three; a copy before V4 has no hold tables, and nothing is added to its schema). Before this, the held torrents left the copy but the restored store no longer refused them, so a later crawl could fetch and index again what the safety core blocked after the snapshot.
- **Imported CS-1 entries are carried too, not only your own.** The brief expected imports to come back through re-import, and config-file entries do: `blocklist.files` are synced into the store at every start (`lodestar::floor::sync_files`). But an entry a floor-marked list dropped and you chose to keep (`resolve_pending_floor` without `remove`) is stored as an import that no source writes again. Carrying all of them is stricter, never laxer. An imported entry the copy's floor manifest doesn't know stays blocked, and can be removed in the app with Remove CS-1 as any entry.
- **Conflicts keep the stronger hold, then the later one.**
  - A CS-1 entry is merged as `add_cs_hashes` merges it: your own confirmation or report outranks an import, and its time stays.
  - A tombstone or quarantine row keeps the floor's rule (`CS-1a`, `CS-1b`, `CS-8a`, which can't be released) over an adult hold, and an adult hold (the one filtering can't rebuild) over any other. Between equals, the later `at` wins, and the current store's on a tie. The whole row comes from the winner.
  - A hold only the copy has stays: **a release made after the snapshot is undone by the rollback**; release it again.
  - A hash can end up both tombstoned and quarantined, as it can in a running store.
- **Quarantine stays sealed.** Rows are copied byte for byte; nothing is unsealed. The copy's quarantine purge then runs over the merged rows, as the store's own tick would: an expired adult hold becomes a tombstone, other expired holds go, and a sealed name the current store no longer holds is cleared.
- **The whole journal and every hold apply to a rollback's copy (O1).** `purge_for_restore` now matches every journal and hold row by hash, whenever it was recorded (`since = i64::MIN`), not only those from a day before the snapshot's modification time. A snapshot copied without keeping its time (`cp` without `-p`) looks taken after deletions it still holds, so the window would let a deleted torrent back. The cost: a torrent deleted and then legitimately fetched again since is removed from the copy too, and can be fetched again. The hourly snapshot purge keeps its window (`2026-10-23-S-snapshots.md`): it only scrubs, it never makes a snapshot the store.
- **Rolling forward from a store before V12 can bring back what it deleted (O2).** A store before V12 keeps no deletion journal, so rolling it forward (undoing a rollback to a schema before V12, say) has no record of what it deleted since the copy it restores was taken. Its holds still apply and are carried. This is inherent: there's nothing to read.
- **Tests:** a rollback with holds recorded after the snapshot keeps every one, and the restored store's filter state (which intake refuses from at start) refuses the reported hash and holds the tombstoned and quarantined ones; a copy dated a month after its deletions still loses them; the journal row is merged when the store doing the rollback is older; and dropping any one table's merge fails a test.
