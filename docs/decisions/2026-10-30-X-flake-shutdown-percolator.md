## `the_task_turns_commits_into_alerts_quickly` (B9)
- **The cause: the test timed more than the percolator.** B9's test asserted store-to-alert under 5 s. That span includes the live indexer's poll (500 ms), its commit interval (2 s) and the commit and reader reload. Those are the indexer's share of "new torrent → searchable" (§1.6.3), which `search-bench --gate` already holds to 5 s (`NRT_MAX`) on a quiet machine. The percolator's own share, from the commit being reported to the alerts being stored, is a small fraction of the total.
- **Measured on Linux:** store-to-commit is 2.04 to 2.29 s and commit-to-alert is 42 to 216 ms, over 30 runs with 8 busy loops on 4 cores. On a quiet machine they're 2.08 s and 37 ms. The Windows run printed only its total, so its split isn't measured. Its extra ~3.5 s over Linux is most likely in the commit (Tantivy's commit and reader reload, with their `fsync`s, on the runner's disk), which is the indexer's share, not the percolator's.
- **Timed from the commit, as the brief prefers (§2.19).** The test adds its own sink to the commit hook ahead of the percolator's, so it runs first. It asserts commit-to-alert under 5 s, timed from the commit that carries the new torrents, the moment the percolator hears of it. The 5 s number is unchanged. Store-to-commit is still printed, and a failure's message gives both parts, so a slow Windows run shows which part grew.
