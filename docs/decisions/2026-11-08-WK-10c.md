It adds the worker's sharded DHT, live shard and intensity changes, the per-worker budget and intensity, the heartbeat's stats, Hello's capabilities, the metrics.md target-prefix text and the measured RSS breakdown (plan Q6). The floor-drop tombstones, the drain fencing and WK-10b's follow-up list go to WK-10d (runner, 2026-11-08T01:54Z, a §2.26 call).

## The worker's DHT (`crates/lodestar/src/worker/dht.rs`)
- **One node on the shard `Assign` names.** `lodestar worker` builds it from the `[dht]` settings (`Config::dht_config`), its state in the worker's own folder (`dht-state.bin`). It runs with `dht.enabled` on; the shard and intensity come from `Assign`. The full shard is no shard (WK-4), so a worker that's alone behind its address runs exactly as the built-in DHT.
- **A new shard restarts the node, not the process** (WK-4: a shard is fixed for a node's lifetime). The old node is shut down, saving its state within 10 s, and a new one binds the same addresses. The stages, the session, the leases and the queues carry on (the gate test checks the prepare task is the same task before and after). A new intensity alone is applied to the running node (`set_intensity`). An assignment without a DHT stops it.
- **When it runs:** a session is up and its first `Assign` is in, the stages run (the snapshots are in), nothing drains, and the assignment names a shard. Before the stages start, nothing could be claimed, so the DHT stays off.
- **A lost session pauses it at once, and a minute without one stops it** (`LINGER`). WK-5 says a worker that loses its session must stop its DHT at once, since the coordinator re-plans without it. Paused, the node sends no samples, starts no lookups and reports no harvests (`NodeHandle::set_paused`), which is the traffic the per-address plan divides. It still answers other nodes' queries and keeps its routing table. A session back within the minute whose first `Assign` names the same shard resumes it with no restart. One naming another shard restarts it. **Why not stop at once:** a node started without the restart proof (`dht.safe_restart`, off by default) suppresses sampling for six hours plus its query timeout, so stopping on every dropped connection would leave a worker on a flaky link sampling nothing. This refines WK-5's rule; the runner accepted it (2026-11-08T02:21Z) and flags it to the owner (§2.19). While paused, the node still holds its BEP 42 ids for up to a minute, which a re-plan may by then have given to another member behind the same address. Two nodes then answer with ids in the same slot for that minute; neither samples with them meanwhile.
- **The pool pauses with it.** WK-5 also says a worker without a session stops opening connections at once. The pool is paused when the session goes and resumed when one is back, beside WK-10b's pause while a result waits for a sealable key. One function works out the pause from the state now (a result waiting, or no session up) and tells the pool under a lock, so pauses and resumes arrive in the order they're decided, whatever thread asks. Fetches under way finish (WK-5 allows it). Before this, a worker kept fetching its held keys with no session.
- **The pool's peer finder.** The pool asks the DHT for peers through one channel that outlives the nodes. A request is handed to whichever node runs, and dropped with none, which the pool reads as a refusal. The worker's pool doesn't stream (`streaming_peers` off), so a session admission, if one ever came, is refused and the request answered as before.
- **Harvests** become finds for the claims (WK-10b): `Sample`, `GetPeers` and `Announce`, with the DHT's strategy codes. `Lookup` harvests answer `Node::get_peers`, which the worker never calls; the pool's own lookups answer through their admissions. A full claims queue drops the key and counts it (`DhtCounts::dropped`): it'll be found again.
- **A node that fails to bind** is retried after a back-off, 1 s doubling to 60 s, and logged (`worker.dht_start_failed`). One that stops by itself is started again likewise. Each start is logged (`worker.dht_started`, with the shard's prefixes and slot count).
- **Stopping the runtime** stops the node and waits for its state save (within the 10 s stop deadline) before the tasks are aborted. Dropping the runtime drops the node, which stops its actor without the save.
- **Not in this PR:** the IP blocklists (`dht.deny`, §20.13) aren't wired on a worker, since it subscribes to no lists. CR-4's uTP port isn't shared with the worker's fetches either; the worker fetches over TCP.

## Budget and intensity (`crates/lodestar/src/worker/budget.rs`)
§18.4's scheduler v1 sets the global budget from the intensity preset: outbound DHT queries (the DHT's preset) and TCP sockets (the fetch pool). A worker does the same on its own NIC and router, from its `Assign`. The budget's fields (WK-5) narrow it, and 0 means no cap beyond the defaults:
- **Sockets:** `Low` opens at most 100, `Balanced` and `Aggressive` the worker's 200 (`MAX_SOCKETS`). §6's 400 and 1,000 don't fit a worker's 4 MiB held budget.
- **`ram_mb`** caps the pool's keys after 162 MiB for everything else (plan Q5's 208 MiB steady less the pool's 46). The pool takes at most about 973 B a key, Q5's observed upper bound. It never goes below 1,000 keys and never above the coordinator's `pool_cap`. At 256 MB the 50,000-key default stands.
- **`cpu_permille`** lowers the intensity: at most `Balanced` at 500 ‰ or less, `Low` at 250 ‰ or less. It never raises it.
- **The upload and download caps (`up_kbps`, `down_kbps`) do nothing yet.** Neither the DHT nor the pool has a byte-rate limiter, so a worker's bandwidth is bounded only by its intensity. **No UI sets them today.** The workers API does: `PATCH /workers/{id}` accepts `budget.up_kbps` and `budget.down_kbps` (WK-14, `crates/api/src/workers.rs`), stores them and sends them in `Assign`, and the worker ignores them. The runner accepted this for WK-10c (2026-11-08T02:21Z), flags it to the owner (§2.19) and logs the limiter as X-worker-bandwidth-limit.
- **When they apply:** the intensity on every `Assign`; the pool's cap and sockets when the stages start, since the pool has no setters. A later change to either reaches the pool at the worker's next start. A live setter is a `crates/sources/src/pool.rs` change, proposed for WK-10d.

## Hello's capabilities (runner-approved, 2026-11-08T02:21Z)
WK-2 reported only the core count. `Work::capabilities` (a default method, so WK-2's idle runtime is unchanged) is asked before each connect, and the session adds the cores:
- **`ram_mb`:** the memory this process may use. That's the cgroup's limit when one is set (a container's, v2 `memory.max` or v1 `memory.limit_in_bytes`), else the machine's `MemTotal`, whichever is smaller. It reads 0 off Linux.
- **`external_ips`:** the public address the worker's DHT last voted. It's seeded from the DHT's saved state at start, so the first `Hello` after a restart carries it, and refreshed from the running node every minute. **Only a public address is reported** (`network_policy::allows` with private addresses refused): never a private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT (100.64.0.0/10) or documentation address. The planner groups members by these addresses (WK-5), so an unknown one is planned as polite.
- **A changed address reaches the coordinator at the next connect.** Reconnecting as soon as it changes goes to WK-10d, with `set_coordinator_ips`.
- Companions and egress stay 0 until their slices.

## Heartbeat stats
Every 5 s the runtime hands the session the stats each heartbeat carries: claimed (keys claimed), fetched (results sent), processed (results the coordinator accepted), CPU and resident memory. **CPU** is the process's share of the whole machine since the last sample, in ‰, from `/proc/self/stat` (in `USER_HZ`, which Linux fixes at 100). **Memory** is `VmRSS` from `/proc/self/status`. Elsewhere both read 0, since the workspace forbids `unsafe` and the dependencies have no portable reader. Windows workers report them in a later slice. **The spool's bytes** are WK-11's, so 0 for now.

## Measured memory (plan Q6)
**Method:** one test process (`rss_breakdown`, ignored in normal runs) builds a worker runtime and fills each part in turn, reading `VmRSS` from `/proc/self/status` before and after each step. The parts:
- an idle runtime with a session;
- the filter snapshot and the stages, with a DHT node bound on loopback;
- a have-filter header and chunks sized by the coordinator's own `worker_params(10,000,000)`, then a second generation all but its last chunk, so both are held;
- 50,000 granted keys, against a fetcher that never answers, so they stay in the pool.

Measured on Linux (glibc, the test profile, which is optimized), three runs, with `cargo test -p lodestar --bin lodestar -- --ignored --exact --nocapture worker::runtime::tests::rss_breakdown`. Each row is the rise in resident memory as that part fills to its default size, with a have-filter sized for 10M stored swarm hashes:

| Part | Measured (MiB) | Q5's estimate (MiB) |
|---|---|---|
| Process and Tokio (the test binary, before the runtime) | 18.6–19.0 | runtime ≤ 30 |
| The runtime and its session, idle | 7.5–9.2 | (in the runtime's 30) |
| Filter snapshot, the stages, a DHT node with no peers | 19.9–21.8 | prepare and filter snapshot ≤ 40 |
| Have-filter, 10M stored | 16.0–18.0 | ~16 |
| A have-filter swap (both held) | 16.0 | +15 (~31 during a swap) |
| The pool at 50,000 keys, with their leases | 32.1–44.1 | ≤ 46 |
| **Total measured** | **113.5–124.0** | |

**Bounded by construction, not measured here:**
- the DHT's routing tables on the live network (Q5: ≤ 60);
- the pool's held bytes (4 MiB);
- the inbound event queue (Q5: 8);
- the outbound queue and the batches in flight (8 MiB, plus up to 8 more for the encoded copies, `2026-11-07-WK-10b.md`).

**The total fits:** 124 + 60 + 4 + 8 + 16 ≈ 212 MiB at the worst run, during a swap, inside the 256 MB budget (owner §1.6.5). That's close to Q5's 223 MiB. **No cap is raised:** the pool stays at 50,000 keys and the sockets at 200. The headroom (about 44 MiB) is less than another 50,000 keys would take at the upper bound.

The measurement doesn't cover the musl release build's mimalloc, a DHT with a full routing table, or a prepare stage mid-batch. WK-20's chaos harness and the soak can measure those in a real process.

## Tests
- `a_live_shard_change_restarts_only_the_dht` (the gate): a new shard restarts the node and nothing else; a new intensity is applied without a restart; an assignment without a DHT stops it.
- `a_lost_session_pauses_the_dht_and_a_quick_return_resumes_it`: paused at once, and kept paused until the new session's first `Assign`, then resumed with no restart. Stopped, and saved, at shutdown.
- `a_session_gone_past_the_linger_stops_the_dht`.
- `a_lost_session_pauses_the_pool`: paused on a lost session and resumed on a new one (it fails without the pause).
- WK-10b's tests that fetched with no session now open one, since fetching needs a session up. The stalled-session gate uses a link of one frame that the coordinator never reads, a real stalled session instead of none.
- `dht::tests::a_request_with_no_node_is_refused`: the pool's request is refused at once (its admission closes), and the task ends with its sender.
- `budget::tests`: the budget's mapping, and the `/proc` and cgroup parsers.
- `hello_reports_the_saved_public_address_and_the_memory`: a saved public address is reported, a private one isn't.
- `rss_breakdown` (ignored, a measurement): the table above.
