# X-fetch-first-window decisions

This builds on X-fetch-room-fix (`2026-11-14-X-fetch-room-fix.md`). Architecture §6 (hostile-peer limits) and §18.4 (the fetch pool). This file says only what the code now does. The numbers are the runner's calls under `docs/owner-decisions.md` §2.26. No owner-set number or memory budget moves.

## Calls
- **An unproven claim's announced size doesn't count toward admission** (`pool.rs`, `Claim::proven`). A large dict's partial claim is proven once its first window is in, which is when it asks to grow. Until then, the room its announced size would need doesn't count in the largest need that a new first window is checked against. Its first window's bytes count as before, within its network's share.
  - **Why liveness still holds:** every admission keeps the partial claims' bytes plus the largest *proven* need beside the floor. So once grown claims and finished results drain, which the pace and the deadlines bound, the proven claim with the largest need can grow.
  - **Claims proving together need a pass rule** (`grant`, the growth branch). With unproven sizes out of admission, two large claims can both have their windows in when only one of their growths fits beside the other's window. The front of the growth queue would then wait for the window of a claim queued behind it, which waits for the front. So a growth queued behind the front may go first when only the first windows of the growths queued behind the front keep the front from growing: the front's shortfall, beside the floor or in the budget, is no more than those windows' bytes. The first of them that fits grows. The front keeps its turn against everything else: a new first window comes in only beside its growth (below), and a front held back by the grown claims' cap isn't passed.
  - **A front passed by its own network keeps its place.** A network has one grown claim at a time, so once a claim of the front's own network has passed it and grown, the front can't grow until that claim ends. It stays at the head of the growth queue meanwhile, so no growth that asked later goes ahead of it. (Elsewhere, a growth held back by its network's grown claim waits apart and holds up nothing; while a front waits nothing grows but the claims that pass it, so at the front that case arises only this way.)
  - **First windows while a growth waits** (runner decision, refining "new first windows stay out while a growth waits"). A first window is let in while a growth waits only if the front's growth would still fit beside the floor and in the budget with it counted, along with every window let in during the wait. A window let in during the wait never passes that front. As the gate stands, it lets a window in only when the front's shortfall is nil, and a pass needs a shortfall, so this rule is defence in depth: the debug assertion on the front's passes is what guards it if the gate ever changes. That guard (`admitted_during_wait`) is untested for the same reason: no test can reach it while the gate stands. So under steady large-dict traffic, first windows keep coming in beside a waiting growth instead of only in the moments when none waits, and the front's room is never taken.
  - **The bound:** the claims that can pass the front are the ones whose window was in when it began to wait, each once (a claim grows once). Each that grows takes more room than its window held, so the front then waits for it to end. An honest large dict therefore waits at most for the growths queued ahead of it and the ones that pass it, each bounded by the pace rule and its deadline. The budget keeps, for the waiting front, the partial claims held when it began to wait and the passes it has had, and checks the second never exceeds the first (a debug assertion, and an invariant of the budget model).
  - **What's left:** under sustained large-dict growth, a first window that would take the front's room waits for it, and a front that can't fit beside the grown claims and finished results waits for those to end, as any growth does. Both are bounded by the deadlines. Claims whose needs can never fit beside each other are served one after another, never together.
- **A large dict's first window must arrive within 4 s** (`FIRST_WINDOW_DEADLINE`, twice `PACE_FREE`). The window's four requests go out together, so a slow peer isn't held to a round trip a piece.
  - Past the bound the fetch ends with `Error::FirstWindow`, a timed-out failure of that peer; the key keeps its other peers.
  - A dict of one window or less is left to the fetch's deadline, as before.
- **Counted** as `fetch_first_window_timeouts_total{transport}` (`docs/metrics.md`), so the bound can be tuned from what honest peers do.

## Tests
- **Pool** (`pool.rs`, `mod budget`, paused clock): an unproven claim's size doesn't count toward another network's admission, and its need counts once it asks to grow, until it has grown; a growth passes a front its own window blocks, and the front grows once it has ended; a passed front grows before a growth that asked later; a front passed by its own network keeps its place; a front held back by the cap isn't passed; a front with room isn't passed in the moment before it asks again; a window let in during a wait doesn't delay the front or pass it; a window that would take the front's room stays out; a passable front isn't starved by claims that keep coming. #886's admission and liveness tests pass unchanged.
- **Peer wire** (`fetch.rs`, paused clock): a large dict's first window that stops ends when due, as `FirstWindow`, which counts as a timeout; a slow but steady honest peer, its four pieces 900 ms apart, finishes; a first-window timeout is counted by transport and shows in the metrics, and a real fetch over loopback TCP counts its own under `tcp`.
- **The budget model** (`pool_budget_model.rs`) gains claims whose first window never completes, announcing up to the largest dict, and checks the waiting front's passes against the windows in when it began to wait; its other bounds, and its 30 s bound on every wait, are unchanged.
- Each test fails without its part.
