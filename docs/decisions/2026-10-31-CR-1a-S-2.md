# CR-1a-S decisions, part 2

The CR-SIM switch and screen, and the metrics export, for streaming peer batches (`dht.streaming_peers`). Part 1 (#251, `docs/decisions/2026-10-31-CR-1a-S.md`) built the boundary, the actor, the pool's ownership and the regression cases. This part changes no behaviour of the crawler itself, apart from one new counter in the DHT.

## CR-SIM

- **`Switches { streaming_peers }`**, which `apply` writes into `PoolConfig` as the config does, and `describe` names in the report header.
- **The sim's glue forwards sessions as the pipeline does.** CR-SIM doesn't depend on `lodestar-pipeline`, so `run.rs` repeats its small adapter (`SimSession`, `SimEnd`). Without it the pool would accept the DHT's final answer instead (part 1's non-streaming-finder fallback), and the screen's "on" arm would quietly run the old path. `streaming_peers_stream_batches_deterministically` (runs in CI) asserts that the on arm streams (DHT batches, pool batches, terminals, first fetches all > 0), the off arm counts none, and the same seed is byte-identical.
- **The goldens are byte-identical.** The new report lines are one counter each and read 0 with the switch off, which the golden comparison tolerates as new zero counters.

## A new counter for the screen

- **`dht_peer_stream_first_batch_seconds_total`** and its count: time from a streaming request's start to its first batch. Without it, "time to first fetch" couldn't be compared across arms: off, peers arrive only when the lookup ends; on, they arrive with the first batch. It is measured from the request's start, as `dht_peer_lookup_seconds_total` is.

## Metrics

`docs/metrics.md` gains "Streaming peers (CR-1a-S, 2026-10-31)": `dht_peer_stream_*` (batches, backpressure, seen-full, terminals by kind, first-batch time and count) and `fetch_stream_*` (batches read, peers capped, terminals by kind including `source_failed`, first-batch-to-fetch time and count). They are always exported, 0 with the switch off, with no infohash or endpoint label (`streaming_families_render_each_label_with_its_own_value`). `crates/lodestar/src/metrics.rs` is the shared-file edit the brief names.

## CR-SIM screen (off against on, `SCREEN_SEEDS` 1–8)

`cargo test --release -p lodestar-crawler-sim --test sim streaming_peers_screen -- --ignored --nocapture`. Steady stored per minute after the 3-minute warmup. "Spread" is the off arm's range over the eight seeds, the measured noise floor. Conversion is stored per admitted lookup. Time to first fetch is, off, the mean lookup run time (peers arrive at its end) and, on, the mean time to the first batch plus the pool's mean wait from that batch to the fetch.

| Scenario                  | Off mean | On mean | Change | Off spread | Sign test            | Conversion off → on | First fetch ms off → on |
| ------------------------- | -------- | ------- | ------ | ---------- | -------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| `healthy`                 | 76.0     | 81.8    | +7.5%  | 10.9%      | Better (8/8)         | 0.298 → 0.298       | 5,625 → 386             |
| `high-latency` (H1)       | 33.1     | 35.6    | +7.7%  | 15.1%      | Inconclusive (6+/2−) | 0.303 → 0.300       | 16,446 → 3,582          |
| `admission-pressure` (H2) | 29.8     | 30.6    | +2.9%  | 19.7%      | Inconclusive (5+/3−) | 0.097 → 0.099       | 5,461 → 341             |

- **Fetches start much earlier: 14–16× sooner on `healthy` and `admission-pressure`, 4.6× on `high-latency`.** That is the overlap the brief expected.
- **Stored per minute rises a little, but no change clears its noise spread.** On `healthy` every seed agrees (a 1-in-256 chance under no effect), but +7.5% is inside the 10.9% spread, so by CR-ADM's bar (a change larger than the spread, and every seed agreeing) it isn't a clear win. The other two are inconclusive. Conversion doesn't move. As the brief said, the slot isn't freed, so streaming brings fetches forward without letting more lookups run.
- **Backpressure, entry-cap rejections and source failures were 0 on every seed.** The sim's peers come a handful per reply, so the bounds were never reached. The bounds are exercised in the part-1 tests, not here.
- **Admitted lookups rise and completed ones fall on the on arm.** A verified fetch cancels its lookup, so lookups that found a working peer early end as cancelled rather than completed, and their slot frees sooner, which likely explains the small rise in admissions.
- **What this doesn't show:** the sim's limits (CR-SIM README): IPv4 only, approximated routing, no NAT, and "stored" means "reached the writer". This is a screen; the live A/B decides. A null or small result is a valid result.

## From #251's review (optional findings, folded in here)

- **A stream whose entry has moved on is released at its deadline**, and now has a test: `a_silent_stream_after_a_failed_fetch_is_released_at_its_deadline` (a batch, a failed fetch, a silent finder; the slot and session are released at `lookup_wait`). Without that branch, the request would hold its slot until shutdown; the test fails with the branch removed.
- **A held batch costs no allocation on a full channel:** `Stream::flush` reserves channel room before replacing the pending vector.
- **A back-off clears the key's first-batch clock**, so a retry's time to first fetch is measured from its own first batch.
