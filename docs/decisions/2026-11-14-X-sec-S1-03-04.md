# X-sec-S1-03-04 decisions (#PR)

Security fixes S1-03 (blocking) and S1-04 (important) in the DHT node (`crates/dht`). This file says what the code now does (`docs/security/v1/README.md` §5).

## S1-03: intake is bounded per reply and per source
- **A reply offers at most 16 contacts to sampling, and a ping's reply offers none.** An honest reply holds 8 per family. Lookups still use the contacts a reply carries, as before.
- **The sampler gives each IP at most 8 addresses, and each /24 (IPv6 /64) at most 64, across its frontier and its table.**
  - 8 matches the fleet's DHT nodes per IP (`MAX_DHT_PER_IP`), so a host running several nodes still has each sampled.
  - An address counts once whether it's queued, in flight or cooling. Its place comes back when its record leaves the table.
  - Records restored by the restart proof are never refused for their shares, as they're never refused for capacity, but they count.
  - Loopback has no share. The public policy refuses it, and local test networks run many nodes on it.
  - The 20,000 frontier and 200,000 table bounds are unchanged, and records are still kept per address, so the restart snapshot's format is unchanged.
- **A timed-out sample still cools for six hours.** The runner suggested a short cooldown after timeouts. It was left out because CR-0 (`2026-10-25-CR-0.md`) sets six hours for timeouts, the CR-0d restart proof bounds its obligations on that rule, and the crawler-sim restart harness checks it. The table's use by any one block is bounded by the shares above instead. Changing the timeout rule belongs with a change to CR-0 and that harness.
- **One `sample_infohashes` reply hands the pipeline at most 100 hashes.** A full honest reply in one datagram holds about 70.
- **Each /24 (IPv6 /64) has a harvest share:** 4 hashes a second, up to 400 at once. Samples a block's nodes answer and get_peers or announces its hosts send all draw on it. That share also bounds how often the seen-filter turns over, and how many responder hints one block can leave.

## S1-04: replies have a per-source share
- **Each block has a share of the reply rate, taken before the global bucket:** a thirty-second of the global rate (at least 2 a second), with a burst of twice that (at least 8). One block can't empty the global bucket for everyone else.
- **Passive harvest has its own limits instead of sitting behind the reply limiter:** the query rate overall, plus the block's harvest share. An announce with a valid token is stored, and its hash harvested, whether or not a reply could be sent.
- **A get_peers reply lists at most 16 peers.**
- **BEP 33 filters, and nodes of the family the query didn't arrive on, go only to addresses that have answered a query of ours.**
  - Such a reply carried a transaction id only the asked address could know (S1-01), so the address isn't forged.
  - Other queriers get their own family's nodes, and peers without filters.
  - The answered set remembers up to 65,536 IPs for 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Per-source tables are bounded.** Each holds about 4,096 blocks in two generations. A block that drops out comes back with a full bucket, so forgetting can only hand out a burst.

## Other
- The actor-level transaction-id test now checks the ids of real queries on the wire, so it fails if queries stop using the keyed ids (polish from the #868 review).
- No crawler-sim golden changed. The seed-spread table is in the PR description.
- Tests state the invariants in general terms. Property tests cover:
  - contacts offered per reply;
  - hashes per reply and per block;
  - each block's reply share under any interleaving;
  - the sampler's share counts under any sequence of operations;
  - the per-source tables' bounds and per-key rate.
