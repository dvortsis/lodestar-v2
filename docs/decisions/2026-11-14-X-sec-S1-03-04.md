# X-sec-S1-03-04 decisions (#PR)

Security fixes S1-03 (blocking) and S1-04 (important) in the DHT node (`crates/dht`). This file says what the code now does (`docs/security/v1/README.md` §5).

## S1-03: intake is bounded per reply and per source
- **A reply offers at most 16 contacts to sampling, and a ping's reply offers none.** An honest reply holds 8 per family. Lookups still use the contacts a reply carries, as before.
- **The sampler gives each IP, block and IPv6 allocation a share of its frontier and table together:**
  - each IP: at most 8 addresses, matching the fleet's DHT nodes per IP (`MAX_DHT_PER_IP`), so a host running several nodes still has each sampled;
  - each IPv4 /24: at most 64;
  - each IPv6 /64: at most 16;
  - each IPv6 /48, an ordinary site allocation: at most 64 across all its /64s (the runner's requirement, matching the /48 unit #868 uses for the address vote).
  - So the table holds at most 64 addresses per IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /48, the same on both families.
  - An address counts once whether it's queued, in flight or cooling. Its place comes back when its record leaves the table.
  - Records restored by the restart proof are never refused for their shares, as they're never refused for capacity, but they count.
  - Loopback has no share, here or in the per-source buckets below. The public policy refuses it, no packet from off the host carries it, and local test networks run many nodes on it.
  - The counts are 32-bit, so restored records (uncapped, up to 200,000) can't overflow them.
  - The 20,000 frontier and 200,000 table bounds are unchanged, and records are still kept per address, so the restart snapshot's format is unchanged.
- **A timed-out sample still cools for six hours**, as CR-0 (`2026-10-25-CR-0.md`) sets. The CR-0d restart proof bounds its obligations on that rule, and the crawler-sim restart harness checks it. The runner confirmed keeping it here.
- **One `sample_infohashes` reply hands the pipeline at most 100 hashes.** A full honest reply in one datagram holds about 70.
- **Each /24 (IPv6 /64) has a sample share:** 4 hashes a second, up to 400 at once, from samples its nodes answer. Responder hints are only taken for hashes within it.

## S1-04: replies have a per-source share
- **Each block has a share of the reply rate:** a thirty-second of the global rate (at least 2 a second), with a burst of twice that (at least 8). One block can't empty the global bucket for everyone else. A global token taken for a block that has used its share goes back.
- **Passive harvest and announce storage have their own limits instead of sitting behind the reply limiter.**
  - Each block has a passive share, apart from its sample share: 4 a second, up to 100 at once. Get_peers it sends and announces it makes draw on it.
  - What reaches the pipeline also draws on a passive rate overall, equal to the query rate.
  - An announce with a valid token is stored, within the block's passive share, whether or not a reply could be sent.
- **A get_peers reply lists at most 16 peers.**
- **BEP 33 filters, and nodes of the family the query didn't arrive on, go only to addresses that have answered a query of ours.**
  - Such a reply carried a transaction id only the asked address could know (S1-01), so the address isn't forged.
  - Other queriers get their own family's nodes, and peers without filters.
  - The answered set holds up to 65,536 IPs, each for up to about an hour, less when many addresses answer.
- **Per-source tables are bounded.** The reply, sample and passive tables each hold about 4,096 blocks in two generations. A block that drops out comes back with a full bucket, so forgetting can only hand out a burst.

## What this gives up
- **Timed-out samples keep CR-0's six-hour cooldown,** within their IP's, block's and allocation's shares. A shorter one is the follow-up `X-dht-timeout-cooldown`, because it changes CR-0's rule, the restart proof's bound and the sim harness together.
- **A /24 or /48 with many honest DHT nodes has only 64 of them in the frontier and table at once.** The rest wait until a place comes back.
- **An unproven querier gets no BEP 33 filters and only its own family's nodes,** until it has answered one of our queries.

## Other
- The actor-level transaction-id test now checks the ids of real queries on the wire, so it fails if queries stop using the keyed ids (polish from the #868 review).
- No crawler-sim golden changed. The seed-spread table is in the PR description.
- Tests state the invariants in general terms. Property tests cover:
  - contacts offered per reply;
  - hashes per reply and per block;
  - each block's reply share under any interleaving;
  - the sampler's share counts under any sequence of operations, and each family's caps (IPv4 /24, IPv6 /64, IPv6 /48 across many /64s);
  - each block's passive share, and a valid announce stored while its reply is dropped;
  - BEP 32 nodes of both families only for a proven querier;
  - the per-source tables' bounds and per-key rate.
