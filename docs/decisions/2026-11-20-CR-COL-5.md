# CR-COL-5 decisions

Slice session, 2026-11-20. It implements r5.1 (`docs/decisions/2026-11-20-path-to-100-cr-spec.md`, the owner's A1 and the A2 design adopted under the owner's instruction) in the collector and analyzer (`docs/soak/tools/`). CR-SPEC's rules don't change except as r5.1 states: the fit, the +5 % margin, validity, no peeking (rule 1.3) and two blocks per outcome are as they were.

- **Stage 1's verdicts are what they were.** `fixtures/cr_col5_stage1_golden.json` holds the verdict, reasons, every check and its effect, the window mean and the Markdown verdict line for nine stage-1 segments (each treatment on and off, a stray switch, a void uTP arm, a wrong preset, CR-COMB, the comparator) and four effects (win, loss, inconclusive, one block). They were captured from the analyzer on `main` before this change. The regression test compares them with today's output twice: once with the collector recording the new switches as off, and once with a CR-COL-3 collector's run.json that predates them. Both are identical. Stage 1 never passes `--winners`, and the analyzer refuses `--winners` for a stage-1 treatment. Stage-1 reports only gain fields: `ledger` and two Markdown sections ("Switches", "Ledger") per segment, and `control_switches` and `ledger_by_arm` per effect.
- **A stacked control is named, not inferred (A2).** `segment` and `effect` take `--winners FILE` (`{"winners": [...]}`, possibly empty) for a stage-2+ treatment and refuse to run without it. A segment is invalid unless the switches it has on, other than the treatment's own, are exactly the winners (`control_is_the_stacked_winners`). A block is invalid if its control isn't the winners, or if a segment was judged against other winners. **Why a file and not the earlier effect reports:** a winner's identity is a decision the runner records (a skip, or CR-LOOKUP-END alone after an inconclusive pair, changes it), so the analyzer checks against what's recorded rather than re-deriving it.
- **The control's switch set is printed beside each result.** Each block carries `control_switches` (every switch on in its A arms, `safe_restart` included), and the effect report carries one `control_switches` beside `outcome`. If valid blocks' control sets differ, the outcome is `invalid` with the reason "the control's switch set differs between blocks of this treatment (r5.1 A2)". The segment Markdown gains a "Switches" line, and a "Stacked control" line from stage 2. **It's a backstop:** for a stacked treatment every valid block's control already equals the winners, and for stage 1 rule 6.1's `other_switches_off` already holds every control at the shipped defaults, so it can't fire on blocks that are valid. `invalid` isn't a final outcome for Checkpoint 1, which treats it as pending.
- **The pair is one treatment (A1): `--treatment lc`** turns on `lookup_end` and `preset_slots` together. Within a segment, half a pair is invalid (`treatment_switches_together`). In a block, a segment with half a pair is neither arm (`treatment_switches_split`). The switches within a block are now compared as the set switched on, so a switch an older collector didn't record reads as off rather than as a difference.
- **A1's mechanism check, treatment arm only, void on failure (rule 5):**
  - lookups ending on values are above 0: the window's delta of lookups ended with a values reason;
  - occupied slots exceed 32 in at least one window scrape (the gauge is read at each 60 s scrape, so "at least once" means at least one scrape);
  - the query rate stays within the preset: in every scrape interval, `dht_queries_out_total`'s delta is at most the preset's rate × 1.05 × (interval + 2 s). The rate is `Intensity::queries_per_sec` (Balanced 200). The 1.05 is pings to queriers, which go out from their own 5 % share (`VERIFY_SHARE`) and count in the same total. The 2 s is one second of bucket burst and a second of scrape timing. An interval it can't read counts as over. Each interval is judged on its own length, so a 120 s interval after a missed scrape is judged on its mean rate.

  The report also carries `lodestar_dht_lookup_slots_capacity` beside the peak occupancy. The control arm isn't checked: there the pair is off, and nothing says it must not act. `dht_queries_out_total` is used rather than the ledger's per-purpose sum, because CR-LEDGER's brief makes the two equal and the total is already in every snapshot.
- **The ledger is required only for `lc`.** Its series are required in both arms of a `lc` segment (rule 6.10, missing evidence when absent). Every other run reports them when present and lists them under `absent` when not: never a crash, never a verdict change.
- **`W`, `q` and `y`** come from CR-LEDGER's identities, per segment window and per hour (`ledger`): `W` = residence seconds ÷ lookups ended, `q` = peer-lookup queries ÷ lookups ended, and `y` = `metadata_new_verified_total{origin="network"}` ÷ lookups ended (the stored rate the analyzer already reports). `dht_peer_lookup_seconds_total` and `dht_peer_lookups_answered_total` are kept, and their ratio is reported as `seconds_per_answered`, a second reading of residence from admission to answer. `effect` reports each valid segment's `W`, `q` and `y` per arm, with each arm's plain mean (`ledger_by_arm`). They're reported, not fitted or judged: the gain is still decided only on the numerator (rule 6.2).
- **The collector keeps the ledger.** Its series join the snapshot allowlist with fixed label enums. Histogram edges are CR-LEDGER's own lists, so a bucket edge it adds later is dropped until it's added here, as with any other label value. The analyzer's label parser now accepts `.`, for the `le="0.5"` edge.
- **Names (the runner, 2026-11-20):** CR-LEDGER's series and label values as the runner relayed them (docs/metrics.md, "Lookup ledger (CR-LEDGER, 2026-11-20)"), and four switches in `SWITCH_ENV`: `lookup_end`, `preset_slots`, `hint_first` and `sampler_cooldown_split` (`LODESTAR_DHT_<KEY>`), so later stages need no collector change.
- **An unknown switch variable isn't read as off.** The collector records the names (never the values) of `LODESTAR_DHT_*` variables that are neither a known switch nor a known setting, as `unknown_switch_env`, only when there are any, so a stage-1 run.json is unchanged. From stage 2 a segment with one is missing identity (`no_unrecognized_switch_variable`): a misspelt or renamed switch would otherwise record the pair as off without an error.
- **`q` is queries per lookup,** as build-plan §0.5 and CR-LEDGER's brief define it (`peer_lookup` queries ÷ lookups ended), not an unanswered share.

## Rehearsal

`test_analyze_cr_col5.RehearsalTests` runs a synthetic stage 2 end to end. It writes eight segments as the collector writes them (two blocks, ABBA and then BAAB about 12 h out of phase, 5 minutes between arms). The control has CR-ADM on as the stage-1 winner, and B adds the pair. Each segment goes through `analyze_cr.py segment --winners`, then through `effect --treatment lc --winners`. In the run for this PR, all eight segments were valid and both blocks were valid. The outcome was `win`, effect +0.112 (95 % CI +0.101 to +0.122, df 3), with `control_switches` = coordinated_admission, safe_restart. `W` read 23.00 s in A and 6.00 s in B, and `q` read 45.00 and 12.00, as written. It's synthetic data: it shows the tooling works end to end, not any effect.
