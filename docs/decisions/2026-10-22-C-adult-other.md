# C-adult-other decisions (#105)

Fixes a content-safety false negative found in the R-relevance review (#94): a torrent typed Other (no known extension, a lone archive, documents) or Ebook was never called adult, even with the `XXX` tag at score 90, so it showed in default search while Adult was hidden.

- **Other and Ebook get an adult kind from detection** (§20.9 "As built"; supersedes its "Ebook and Other keep their type"). At or above the threshold, Other becomes **Scene** and Ebook **Comics**, the nearest kinds an Is adult correction already gives (`forced_kind`). VR words turn Other's Scene into VR, as they do for Video and TV; Ebook stays Comics. *Why:* hiding Adult by default is the owner's rule (§1.8.12), and a full-score adult name that slips past it is a miss, not caution.
- **The threshold is the same 60 for every type, Ebook included.** *Why:* the signals, the counters and the season rule are the same whatever the type, and TV, Comic and Movie already work this way. Every mainstream control in the fixtures stays out: a novel called *Porno*, *The Joy of Sex*, lecture notes with two adult terms (the counter wins), a documentary with two terms, an episodic *Sex Explained*, an `nsfw` model archive, a plain backup archive, the *xXx* soundtrack. A stricter rule would have lost term-only names like `JAV.Uncensored.Pack.rar`, which are adult when typed Video.
- **Known Ebook and Other false positives at 60** (left for the owner's call under §2.19; corrections and class rules fix them per torrent):
  - `The Hentai Prince and the Stony Cat Vol 1 [Light Novel].epub`: Adult 60, Comics (`hentai` alone is strong).
  - `The Porn Trap - Uncensored Edition.epub`: Adult 70, Comics (two terms).
  - `Porn Studies Journal Vol 1 Uncensored` (PDF, typed Other): Adult 70, Scene (two terms).
  - `After.Porn.Ends.2012.Uncensored` with no extension: Adult 70, Scene. The same documentary as `.mkv` was already Adult (Movie) before this change.
- **`hentai-free` isn't the hentai signal.** `Anime.Collection [hentai-free]` stays Other. Only the hyphenated form is dropped, before the name is split into words, so `[Hentai] Free Download` still counts. It isn't a counter-signal: it doesn't weaken an `XXX` tag or a site name.
- **Only the torrent name and the largest file's name are read, not its folders** (unchanged from classifier v2). A folder scan was tried and dropped in review: counter words on a folder (`Massage Therapy/`, `Medical Exam/`, `Anatomy Lesson/`) pulled tagged torrents out of Adult, and `XXX` as a folder's first word added mainstream false positives (*xXx* in a film collection, the album *XXX*). Fixtures pin both. A hash-named torrent whose only adult signal is on a folder is still missed; the runner carries it forward.
- **`CLASSIFY_VERSION` is 3.** Stored torrents pick up the change through background enrichment: every torrent whose `torrent_parsed.parser_ver` isn't the new `ENRICHMENT_VERSION` is classified again, its `content_type` rewritten and a `reindex_log` entry added, so the search index follows. A torrent that becomes adult this way is judged as adult by the filter engine (§20.7), so the stricter Adult-only checks apply to it. No migration.
- **Refinement under §2.19.** This refines "detection is cautious, so mainstream titles stay out of Adult" (§1.8.12) without loosening it. The scores, the threshold and the counter-signals are unchanged. The only signal change is that `hentai-free` no longer counts as `hentai`, which can only keep a torrent out of Adult. Mainstream controls are pinned by fixtures, and the known false positives are listed above. The change stops a full-confidence adult signal being dropped for two types. The runner flags it to the owner.
