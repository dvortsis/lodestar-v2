# X-dht01-fix decisions

- **DHT-01 (blocking, network; the DHT node) fixed.** Its detail is held under `docs/security/v1/README.md` §5 until a release with the fix is published. This file says only what the code now does.

## Calls
- **Pings to queriers have a budget of their own** (architecture §5). A node that queries us isn't put in the routing table until it answers a ping of ours, since its address may be spoofed. Those pings now draw on their own bucket first, then on the query rate as every query does:
  - **rate:** 5% of the query rate (`VERIFY_SHARE`): 2.5, 10 and 50 a second at Low, Balanced and Aggressive, with a burst of one second's worth;
  - **outstanding:** at most 1/16 of the outstanding-query cap awaiting an answer at once (`VERIFY_OUTSTANDING_DIV`): 16, 64 and 256.
- **A ping the shared limits refuse costs nothing from the share:** when the family check, the query rate or the outstanding cap refuses it, the share's token goes back. Once the query rate has given its token, the share's is spent too, whether or not the packet then leaves.
- **Past either, the querier just isn't verified this time.** It is still answered, within the reply rate as before; a later query from it gets another chance. The refusal counts in `dht_throttled_total`, as any query the budget refuses does, so no metric is added.
- **The total stays inside the query rate.** The new bucket is taken before the shared one, never in place of it, so the node sends no more than it did: the politeness caps of architecture §5 are unchanged.
- **The policy check runs before the budget.** An address the network policy or the blocklist refuses costs nothing from either bucket.
- **Both follow the intensity,** and a change of intensity starts the new bucket full, as it does the others.
- **Not behind a switch.** The crawler rule (new behaviour off by default until a live A/B shows it wins) is for changes meant to raise the yield. This one closes a blocking finding and only lowers what the node sends, so it is on.

## What it may change in a crawl
- At Balanced the node verifies at most 10 queriers a second. On a quiet table that is far more than the table can take (8 a bucket); on a busy node the table is mostly full and `would_accept` already declines most queriers. So no change in the 24 h mean is expected, but it isn't measured.
- **Follow-up (runner):** compare `dht_throttled_total` and the routing-table size against the last soak in the next one, before the crawler gate's 24 h run. `dht_throttled_total` counts these refusals together with the crawl's own throttled queries, so a rise there can be either.

## Tests (`crates/dht/src/node/verify_tests.rs`)
- `queriers_spend_only_their_share_of_the_query_rate`: at most the share is sent, whatever arrives; the rest of the query rate is untouched, and a lookup's query still goes out.
- `queriers_hold_only_their_share_of_the_outstanding_cap`: with no rate limit at all, at most the share is outstanding; an answered ping gives its place back, through the node's own handling of the answer.
- `an_unsent_ping_keeps_its_token`: with the shared rate used up, nothing is sent and the share isn't spent.
- `a_ping_that_fails_to_send_still_spends_the_share`: when every send fails after the shared rate let it through, the tries stay within the share.
- `a_ping_that_times_out_gives_its_place_back`, through the node's own timeout sweep.
- `the_share_follows_the_intensity`, through the intensity command: the new bucket starts full.
- The first two fail with the budget check removed.
