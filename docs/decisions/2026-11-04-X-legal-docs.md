# X-legal-docs decisions (#447)

**Legal notice and lawful-use notices (owner request, 2026-11-04, revised the same day).**

The owner asked, while reviewing the consent drafts in `docs/decisions/2026-11-02-SRC-4.md`, for language that makes clear Lodestar is for lawful use only, explains the risk, and has the user accept responsibility, so that the owner isn't held liable for what users do. A first version was a fourteen-section Terms of Use. A second review the same day (Codex, at the owner's request) found that a terms-of-use conflicts with the AGPL, and the owner asked for the model BitTorrent clients use. **The result is a notice, not a contract:** `docs/legal/legal-notice.md` (shown once before first use, confirmed as read), `docs/legal/operator-guidance.md` (for anyone who makes an instance public), and the review behind both, `docs/legal/2026-11-04-liability-review.md` (§§6–7 record the second and third reviews). This file amends the "Draft consent texts" section of SRC-4's file without editing it (decisions README: a later file supersedes).

**Not legal advice.** All four reviews were done by AI assistants from secondary sources. The notice and the operator page go to Colorado counsel familiar with open-source licensing (owner queue, below; advisory, not a release gate: owner, 2026-11-04).

## Why a notice and not terms (architecture §18.7, the AGPL)
- **The AGPL is the only licence.** Its §9 says nobody has to accept anything to run the program, §7 allows only a short list of additional terms, and §10 forbids further restrictions. Conditions on use (age, conduct, an indemnity, acceptance of future terms) would be further restrictions that recipients may strip, and the project would no longer be AGPL software. So Lodestar follows qBittorrent: licence plus a short legal notice that informs and warns, restates AGPL §§15–16 (as §7(a) allows) and ends on the operator's sole responsibility for what they do with what it finds. No indemnity, no zero-dollar cap.
- **What the notice does, honestly:** it gives conspicuous notice of the network exposure, the nature of the results, the limits of the filters and the operator's responsibility; it is evidence against inducement; and it preserves the AGPL's disclaimers against claims by the operator. It binds no rights holder, regulator or prosecutor. Against them the project's protection is its position in law (*Sony*, *Grokster*, *Cox v. Sony* 2026): a self-hosted, metadata-only, general-purpose tool with real safeguards, not marketed for infringement and not operated by its maker.
- **Every factual sentence in the notice matches the shipped software**, and the prober is marked planned. A PR that changes what Lodestar requests, stores or sends updates the notice in the same PR and raises its version if the change is substantive.

## Where the language goes
1. **The notice ships in the binary** and is shown in full, in the app, without internet access, with its version and date. **A first-run step:** the first-run wizard gains a step with a required checkbox, unticked by default, "I have read the Legal Notice", with the notice one click away. `POST /setup` refuses a request whose `notice_version` isn't the current one (`409 notice_required`). The confirmation is stored in `settings` under `legal.notice_version` (no time, no author, like consent records; §1.8.8). This is the confirmation qBittorrent asks for at first start, built so a court would recognise it as conspicuous notice (*Berman v. Freedom Financial*, 9th Cir. 2022). It confirms reading; it doesn't condition the licence.
2. **A substantive change re-shows it.** `legal::NOTICE_VERSION` is a compile-time constant; raising it makes the admin's next login show the notice before anything else and asks for the confirmation again. Editorial fixes don't raise it. This mirrors `consent::text_version`.
3. **Settings → About gains a "Legal" section:** the notice (with version), the AGPL-3.0-or-later licence, the brand-assets licence, the operator guidance, and the existing third-party licences list.
4. **The Restricted and Overlay consent screens** keep their own texts and gain a link to the notice, with the closing lines amended below.
5. **Outside the app:** one sentence and a link in the README, in the user guide's chapter 1 (§1.2), and in each Unraid template's `Overview`, so the notice is seen before install.
6. **The private reporting route** the notice names (section 9) needs GitHub's private vulnerability reporting turned on for the repository (done by the owner, 2026-11-04).

**Slices (runner, 2026-11-04).** `LEGAL-1`, one Opus slice in v0.5.1 (rc.4), owns the server and UI parts together, because this is the auth and setup path and is never helper work: `POST /setup` → `409 notice_required`; the login gate that re-shows the notice when the version is raised; `legal::NOTICE_VERSION` and the `legal.notice_version` setting; the wizard step; About → Legal; the notice embedded in the binary. Its tests: 409 on a missing or stale version; the notice re-shown after a version bump; not re-shown after an editorial-only change that leaves the version alone; an install that finished setup before LEGAL-1 (no `legal.notice_version`) sees the notice at login. `LEGAL-2`, the crawler paused on a fresh data directory until setup completes, is adopted by the runner under §2.26 for after v0.5.1, so rc.4 stays small. In short: LEGAL-1, RP-DEF and RP-UC in v0.5.1 (rc.4); LEGAL-2 after v0.5.1. SRC-15b, which ships the consent screens, follows LEGAL-1. The notice body is served as one Markdown document rendered in-app and isn't translated; en and zh message keys hold the UI labels only.

## Standing content rule (refines owner decision §2.28 Q1 and architecture §18.7)
Nothing the project publishes shows or suggests using Lodestar to find commercial films, music, software or books without permission: not the README, screenshots, release notes, fixtures, plugin descriptions, in-app examples or the Unraid templates. Fixture and screenshot titles stay generic or public-domain. This is the *Grokster* / *Fung* evidence rule: marketing is what turned those cases. It's a refinement, recorded under §2.19 and flagged to the owner.

## Consistency audit (2026-11-04, after the fourth review)
The repository was checked against the notice and the operator guidance. The code's defaults match the story (metadata only; no peers stored with torrents; query log off; private-source rows excluded from exports and alerts; no telemetry library; locked floor), and no public-facing text invites infringement. These didn't match, and are fixed in this PR:
- **No source plugin is registered in this build.** `crates/sources-plugins` (Nyaa, Prowlarr, academic lists, clients) exists as library code, and `register_source` is called only by the import CLI and tests; the Settings "sources" screen is a placeholder id. The notice now says which features this version has and marks the source classes as planned.
- **The crawler starts at program start, before setup finishes** (`Services::start` builds the pipeline with `paused` from settings, default off; nothing waits for the admin password). The notice says so. **Adopted by the runner as `LEGAL-2` (UX, §2.26), after v0.5.1:** the crawler starts paused on a fresh data directory until setup completes, so nobody's address joins the DHT before anyone has read the notice. Existing installs are unaffected.
- **Drop doesn't delete stored records** ("Torrents already stored stay as they are"); Hide applies to stored and future copies but the record stays retrievable by authenticated users; Quarantine removes the stored record (the hash and sealed name stay in the review queue, where **Confirm as abuse** moves the hash to the floor); the API's `report_abuse` change has no production UI, only a dev route. The operator guidance describes Quarantine as the removal action. **Recommendation for the runner:** a plain **Delete** action for takedowns (Hide plus deleting the stored record, logged in the filter hits, with no queue entry) would be simpler for operators, as a small filters slice.
- **`lodestar backup` copies `seal.key`,** the key that unlocks stored secrets, with `auth.json`. The notice says so instead of "the key stays out".
- **The first-run confirmation isn't built,** so the README, the guide and the templates say to read the notice before installing and mark the in-app step planned; the notice carries a status line until the slice ships.
- **Privacy claims that outran the notice:** the wizard's "Nothing about your searches leaves this machine" and the guide's "the DHT crawl is its only regular traffic" are qualified (sources, when on; tracker swarm counts).
- **Owner choices applied (2026-11-04):** the release-parser golden corpus keeps its real release names, with its README stating that they are strings used to test the parser's reading of naming conventions, including cracked software and camcorder rips, so the classifier and filters can recognise them; the Unraid templates move from "Downloaders" to `Network:Web Tools:Utilities`; and the parser's `cracked` flag is to feed the malware pack as a junk reason (slice `RP-UC`, since packs are versioned and signed and the junk-reason names are stable API values).

## Rule packs on by default, and the unauthorised-copy pack (owner, 2026-11-04, §2.39)
The owner asked whether the release-parser corpus could serve as an optional exclusion filter for copyrighted material, and then decided: every filter pre-checked by default, reviewed in the wizard, opt-out rather than opt-in, shipped in the first release. Recorded as owner decision §2.39; the earlier "off until chosen" (§2.8, architecture §16 "M5 decisions", the v0.6 rule-pack plan's first fixed constraint) is marked superseded in place.
- **Not the corpus as a list.** Matching the corpus's names would catch almost nothing in a live index while showing that those exact releases were known to be infringing. The pack is pattern-based, on the parser's fields (source, group, kind, the `cracked` flag), and the corpus is its test set, with a lawful-names set (public-domain and Creative Commons releases, Linux ISOs, archive.org films) that must never be hidden.
- **The pack is named for what it detects:** `unauthorised-copy` patterns, not "copyrighted material". Almost everything is copyrighted, Creative Commons films included, and no filter can know who holds the rights. Its wizard description says so and names Allow rules as the way back.
- **Tiers:** UC-1 camcorder and screener rips (no lawful public release; very high precision, but the parser sets the source from a weak token on a title word directly before a strong marker, so `Nanny Cam 1080p x264-GRP` and `Little Ts 720p` are must-not-hide names in its precision set and RP-UC tightens the rule until they pass, for example by requiring a strong source marker or a weak one in brackets or after the year, with precision evidence in its PR); UC-2 cracked software (the same signal also becomes a new malware-pack rule, MW-6; both need the flag and a crack-specific token, never `patch` alone); UC-3 scene-style film and TV rips with the official-source exemption and the allow list (high coverage, medium precision). Music and books are left out of the first version: names alone don't give enough precision.
- **Why it helps legally, and the trade-off:** developing filtering tools is weighed in the maker's favour (*Grokster*; isoHunt and LimeWire were faulted for taking no steps), and operators who run the pack lower their red-flag exposure under §512(d). *Cox v. Sony* (2026) confirms that knowing users may misuse a tool is not by itself contributory liability, which covers a user's choice to switch a pack off; inducement stays the test, and the pack is evidence against it. On by default, a fresh install hides most commercial film and TV rips, which some users will undo; that is their choice under the notice.
- **Two slices, scheduled by the runner for v0.5.1:** (1) packs on by default: `load::pack_switches` keeps reading no row as off, a fresh store writes an explicit on row for every pack in the build, the stored switches record what the user turned off, the wizard's packs step shows all checked and no longer treats "at least one pack on" as a stored choice (`docs/decisions/2026-10-31-S-wizard.md` is superseded on that point; the wizard needs an explicit reviewed marker), and the wizard and Settings copy change in en and zh; (2) the `unauthorised-copy` pack with its three rules, the `cracked` junk reason and its new malware-pack rule MW-6, the allow list, the precision set, API goldens, UI keys and the user guide's filters chapter. Both are core content-safety work: main session or an Opus agent, fresh Opus review.
- **Slice ids and ownership (runner, 2026-11-04: `LEGAL-1`, `RP-DEF`, `RP-UC`; all three in v0.5.1 through rc.4).** `RP-UC` lands after `RP-DEF`:
  - **`RP-DEF` owns** `crates/filter/src/load.rs` and `crates/filter/src/packs/upgrade.rs` (the markers, the explicit off rows, fresh seeding, `pack_switches` and their tests; it builds no accept route or UI, so it doesn't overlap RP-3 or RP-6), `crates/store`'s schema and lib (the `packs.fresh` marker written by the migration that creates the store), `crates/api/src/filters.rs` (the pack seed and start only; RP-3 also owns this file but isn't in v0.5.1, so RP-DEF lands first and RP-3 brings `main` in), `crates/api/tests/golden/`, `web/src/lib/features/wizard/PackStep.svelte` and the wizard's packs copy in `web/src/lib/features/wizard/messages/{en,zh}.json`, the Settings → Filters & Safety copy on defaults, the "off until chosen" sentences in `docs/user/` (the filters chapter and chapter 1), and its own decisions file (it supersedes `docs/decisions/2026-10-31-S-wizard.md` on the "at least one pack on" marker).
  - **`RP-UC` owns** a new `crates/filter/src/packs/unauthorised_copy.rs` plus its registration in `crates/filter/src/packs/mod.rs` and `manifest.rs`, the malware pack's new MW-6 rule, the `cracked` junk reason in `crates/classify/src/junk.rs`, the allow list and the precision set under `crates/filter/tests/` (built from `crates/release-parser/tests/golden/`), the pack's rows in `crates/api/tests/golden/`, the pack's description keys in `web/src/lib/features/filters/messages/{en,zh}.json`, the `PackSwitch` warning text in `crates/api/src/filters.rs` (it names five topics and gains the sixth), and the pack's section of the user guide's filters chapter.
  - **Order:** `RP-UC` depends on `RP-DEF` and lands second (runner, 2026-11-04). Their owns overlap in the filters guide chapter and `crates/api/tests/golden/`, and a sixth pack is on by default only once `RP-DEF` has landed, so `RP-UC` adds its rows to goldens that already show every pack on.
  - **Existing installs are offered, not flipped (runner, 2026-11-04; a §2.19 refinement flagged to the owner).** Facts from `main`: the wizard writes only "on" rows and Skip writes nothing, and `load::pack_switches` reads no row as off; `upgrade::seed_record` seeds the baseline rules at version 1, and a version is recorded at its current number only on an off → on switch (`turned_on`, `set_pack_switch`); no route exposes `accept_pack_versions` yet (RP-3 and RP-6 are unmerged). So `RP-DEF`'s upgrade step is: on the first start of the RP-DEF build against a store that existed before it, write an explicit "off" row for every pack with no row, the new `unauthorised-copy` pack included, and set a marker (`packs.defaults = 1`); existing installs then keep exactly today's behaviour and nothing flips silently. **A fresh data directory is a store created on this start, with no prior schema, and its freshness is recorded durably:** the transaction that creates the store (the migration from schema version 0) writes a `packs.fresh = 1` settings row, with no new table. The server's pack start (`seed_packs` / `start_packs`) consumes it: it writes an explicit on row for every pack in the build, seeds every rule at its current version (so MW-6 and UC-1 to UC-3 apply, not pending), sets `packs.defaults = 1` and clears `packs.fresh`. An existing store without either marker gets the explicit off rows and `packs.defaults = 1`. This covers a store first created by `lodestar import`, and a crash between create and seed. `load::pack_switches` keeps reading no row as off, which keeps two paths safe: an Undo to a pre-RP-DEF snapshot (`replace_rule_set` deletes all switch rows) returns to that snapshot's off state, and a store upgraded by an RP-DEF build that lacks RP-UC reads `unauthorised-copy` as off. The wizard then shows every pack on and the user may opt out: unchecking a pack in the wizard goes through the weakening flow inline (effect warning plus the password just set), and per-rule opt-out is in Settings. Packs that are off on an existing install are offered by the wizard's review or the filters screen through the existing `POST /filters/preview` → apply flow (`applyPacks`): that is the §1.8.7 impact preview, and turning a pack on records its current versions. New rules in a pack that is already on (MW-6 in a malware pack the user turned on) stay in shadow, as RP-0 designed, until RP-3 and RP-6 ship the accept route and UI; they are not in v0.5.1, and the release notes say so. `RP-DEF`'s tests: an upgraded store keeps every pack's state; a fresh store has every pack on, with UC and MW-6 applied; turning a pack on through preview → apply records its current versions; a store created by `lodestar import` and then served is seeded fresh; a crash after create but before seed is still seeded fresh on the next start. `RP-UC`'s test: on an upgraded store, `unauthorised-copy` is off until turned on.
  - **MW-6, fully specified (malware pack, new rule; owner's words in §2.39: "Go with your recommendations" to wiring the `cracked` flag into the malware pack).** Matches a release the parser classifies as software that carries the `Cracked` flag **and** a crack-specific token (crack, cracked, keygen, keymaker, activator, k'ed, pre-activated); `patch` alone, which the lexicon also rates a weak `Cracked` token, never matches, so lawful patches such as `Firefox.120.0.1.Patch` pass. The parser stays unchanged, and lawful patch and update names join the precision set. UC-2 uses the same test. Action: hide. Guards: the parser's flag only, never a title that merely contains the word; the software kind is required, so a game level, a track title or a film title containing the word isn't matched; music-production packs and games are excluded by the kind; the release-parser corpus is its precision set. SC-1's guard "offline software cracks aren't covered (a preference, not a scam)" is amended to point at MW-6. Because MW-6 sits in the malware pack with its own switch, **switching `unauthorised-copy` off doesn't restore cracked software**; a user who wants it switches MW-6 off, with the effect warning that any weakening change gets.
  - **`LEGAL-1` and `LEGAL-2`:** see "Slices" above. The runner kept LEGAL-1 whole (auth and setup path, Opus) and moved the crawler pause to LEGAL-2 after v0.5.1.
- **Unchanged:** blocklist subscriptions stay off until chosen (§2.9, offline by default). The owner was told this and can extend the decision.
