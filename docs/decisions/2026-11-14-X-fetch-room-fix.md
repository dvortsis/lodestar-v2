# X-fetch-room-fix decisions

This builds on X-fetch-meta-reserve (`2026-11-06-X-fetch-meta-reserve.md`) and X-fetch-pool-bytes (`2026-11-06-X-fetch-pool-bytes.md`). Architecture §6 (hostile-peer limits) and §18.4 (the fetch pool). The fetch pool now keeps room for new metadata fetches whatever the fetches already running do. This file says only what the code now does. The numbers are the runner's calls under `docs/owner-decisions.md` §2.26. No owner-set number moves; the memory budgets (`max_held_bytes`, a worker's 4 MiB) are unchanged.

## Calls
- **A floor for small dicts** (`Budget::floor`, a quarter of the budget). A whole claim of at most `SMALL_CLAIM` (80 KiB: one request window with its client name and PEX peers, and the key) is a small one.
  - Partial and grown claims, the ones for dicts larger than one window, together stay out of the floor, whatever room the budget has.
  - A large dict's first window is admitted only while the largest growth any partial claim still needs would fit beside the floor too, so once grown claims and finished results drain (the pace and the deadlines bound that), one of them can grow.
  - **A network's partial claims share an eighth of the room beside the floor** (`PARTIAL_NET_SHARE`, by the same networks as below). A network's first partial claim is always let in; one past its share waits apart and holds up no other network's claims, and once its network's share frees it takes a place in the queue like any other, so no later claim overtakes it. With a worker's 4 MiB budget that is 384 KiB, about five first windows; with the main node's 64 MiB, 6 MiB, about 85. Reason: the room for large dicts' first windows is shared across at least eight networks rather than taken by one.
  - While a growth or a partial claim that only lacks room waits, small claims are still granted inside the floor and nothing else starts. Finished results the consumer hasn't taken yet share the floor with small claims meanwhile, so room outside the floor only comes free. Small dicts then never keep a large one out, and a large one never keeps small ones out for longer than the consumer takes.
  - Reason: most dicts are 10–60 KB (architecture §3). Keeping a quarter of the budget for them keeps the pool fetching them while large dicts are under way.
- **Grown claims share a cap** (`Budget::grown_cap`, half the budget). A growth that would take grown claims past it keeps its turn in the growth queue: the growths behind it wait, and the grown claims drain until it fits. One large dict at a time may use the room above the grown cap, never the floor: a growth with no other grown claim beside it may pass the cap, so a dict up to the largest size (below) is still fetched. Meanwhile small claims keep the whole floor and any other growth waits. A growth with nothing else reserved or held is granted whatever its size, as before, so a dict over the limits below is still fetched alone if a fetcher allows it. A whole claim over the small size (peer-wire makes none) is counted with the grown bytes but takes no network's place.
- **One grown claim per network** (an IPv4 /24, an IPv6 /48, as the per-network peer limits count them). A network's next growth waits apart from the growth queue until its grown claim ends: only that claim holds it back, so it holds up nothing. Reason: the room for large dicts is shared across sources rather than taken by one.
- **The largest dict a pool fetches is what fits beside its floor** (`pool::largest_dict`: three quarters of the budget, less a 32 KiB margin for the key, the client name and the PEX list).
  - **Workers:** `MAX_METADATA` follows it. A worker now fetches metadata up to 3 MiB (less the margin); larger dicts are left to a later hand-back slice. Until then, a key whose dict is larger is lost as oversize on a worker, as one over the old limit was.
  - **The main node:** the `max_metadata_mib` setting is held to 47 MiB (`MAX_METADATA_MIB`), the whole number of MiB that fits beside the floor of the default 64 MiB budget. A value above 47 acts as 47, the setting's help says so, and one line is logged at start when it does (`fetch.metadata_limit_clamped`). The setting's range stays 1–64, so a saved value above 47 still loads after an upgrade.
  - Reason: a dict larger than that would need room in the floor.
- **A fetch must keep pace once its dict is larger than one window** (peer-wire, `Pace`). The dict's pieces past the first window are split into 8 parts of the time left before the deadline. From the second part on, and never in the first 2 seconds (`PACE_FREE`), a peer that has sent less than half an even pace (less one piece) ends with a timeout. With 2 seconds or less left, there are no checks and the deadline alone ends the fetch. It is checked before every read and while a read waits. Every write on the connection is bounded by the next check or the deadline, so a peer that stops reading still reaches its checks, and once the pace starts the peer's metadata requests aren't answered (we serve none; the reject was a courtesy). The clock starts once the growth's room is granted, so a wait for room is never the peer's. Dicts of one window are left to the deadline, as before. The rule is always on; there is no setting.
- **A peer's word on a dict's size isn't enough to lose a key** (`OVERSIZE_AGREE`). An announced size over the limit ends that fetch. The key is lost for it only once peers in 3 different networks have said so. Until then its other peers are tried. A key that runs out of peers is lost for the reason its last peer gave, as before. Each entry keeps the first two networks that said so (+8 bytes per entry, boxed, so `diagnostic_entry_storage_is_bounded` still holds).
- **Wait lines shrink with a gap** (supersedes the memory line in `2026-11-14-X-pipe01-followup.md`, What it costs). A line that drains clears out the turns left behind once it has room for more than four times its waiters (and 8 turns), and then keeps room for about twice the turns left. So a line that shrinks and grows by one doesn't reallocate each time. At the slackest, a waiting peer costs under 1 KB: a line alone at its address can keep room for a dozen turns, and the maps can be mostly empty after a burst, never larger than at the bound. That is under 50 MB with the default queue (200,000) and under 13 MB with a queue of 50,000. As a rule it is about 180 bytes, unchanged.
- **The lookup count follows the requests map** when a key in a lookup gains peers: it is decremented only if the key's request was there, and a debug assertion checks the two agree.

## Tests
- **Budget** (`pool.rs`, `mod budget`, paused clock): growths are served one at a time and all complete; small claims keep to the floor while a growth waits; finished results share the floor while a growth waits; grown claims stay within their cap, and one held back by it keeps its turn; a network has one grown claim at a time; large claims stay out of the floor; large claims are admitted only while one can grow; one network's partial claims keep to their share; a claim its network's share held back keeps its turn once the share frees; a lone growth passes the cap, and not the floor unless nothing else is held; beside a lone growth, small claims keep the floor and other growths wait.
- **The largest dict** (`pool.rs`, real TCP): with a worker's budget, the real fetcher fetches a dict of exactly `largest_dict` bytes and refuses one byte more at the handshake.
- **The size rule** (`tests/pool.rs`): one peer's word doesn't end a key; peers in three networks agreeing do; one network's peers count once; a key out of peers is lost for its last peer's reason. `silent_claimers_pin_one_small_window_and_small_dicts_still_flow` now puts each silent peer in a network of its own, as the network share would otherwise hold most of them back.
- **The pace rule** (peer-wire `fetch.rs`, paused clock): a peer under half pace ends at the next check; a slow start keeps pace; rounding never ends a fetch on pace; no pace starts past the deadline; the first check leaves two seconds; a peer that stops sending, sends only keep-alives or asks for metadata without reading ends at its next check; a peer that stops reading while we send our requests ends at its check; a slow honest peer with 2 seconds left finishes; a peer that floods other messages ends at its first check (real time, about two seconds); a small dict's peer is left to the deadline; a wait for room isn't the peer's.
- **The clamp** (`config.rs`): the setting is used as it is up to 47 MiB and held to 47 above.
- Each part's test fails without its part.
