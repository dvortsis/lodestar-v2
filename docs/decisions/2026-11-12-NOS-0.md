# NOS-0 decisions (#789)

NIP-01 relay messages and NIP-35 torrent events (`crates/sources-feeds/src/nostr.rs`), per plan `docs/plan/2026-11-06-v08.md` §5.3. Pure and unused until NOS-3 (plan §5.4: lands early, inactive).

- **Revisions followed:** NIP-01 and NIP-35 as published in `nostr-protocol/nips` on `master` at 2026-11-12, as known to this session; the cloud session had no fetch of the repository, so the next Nostr slice (NOS-1) re-reads both and records the commit. The rules relied on: NIP-01's canonical serialisation and its seven escapes, its relay messages (`EVENT`, `OK`, `EOSE`, `CLOSED`, `NOTICE`, plus NIP-42's `AUTH`), the 64-character subscription id, and NIP-35's `title`, `x`, `file`, `tracker`, `i` and `t` tags. NIP-36's `content-warning` tag.
- **Canonical escaping follows NIP-01's text, not `JSON.stringify`:** `\n`, `"`, `\`, `\r`, `\t`, `\b` and `\f` are escaped; every other character, including other control characters, is written verbatim. Clients built on `JSON.stringify` write `\u0001` and the like instead, so such an event is refused as an id mismatch. That errs toward dropping, the safe side, and real torrent events don't carry control characters. Golden vectors in `tests/nostr.rs` were computed independently (Python's `json.dumps`).
- **The id covers the decoded strings:** a wire `\u00e9` or `\/` decodes before hashing, so equivalent spellings give the same id.
- **Shape is strict:** id, pubkey and sig must be lower-case hex of 64, 64 and 128 digits; `created_at` a non-negative integer that fits `u64` (no fraction, exponent or sign); `kind` 0–65535; `content` a string; each field exactly once (a repeated key is refused, so two readers can't see two events); unknown fields are read past. An event key written with any escape (`"\u0069d"`, say) refuses the event (`EscapedKey`), as a repeated key does: its decoded form could be a field another reader takes (runner, review of #789 O2, consistent with LEM-0).
- **Caps (plan §5.3):** a message over 128 KiB is refused unread; ≤ 512 tags; ≤ 16 strings in a tag (NIP-35's longest tag has three; 16 leaves room for other kinds) and at least one; JSON nesting ≤ 4 (the depth of an event's tags); values ≤ 64 + tags × (strings + 1). A refused message keeps nothing.
- **`AUTH` is refused** with its own reason (no NIP-42); unknown types (`COUNT`, lower-case names) are `Unknown`.
- **Relay text** (`NOTICE`, `CLOSED`, `OK` reasons) has hidden characters removed and is cut to 256 bytes: it's only logged.
- **Claims are a parser-local `Release`,** as the other feed parsers do, with every limit pinned equal to `lodestar_sources::claims` by a test. NOS-3 maps it onto `Claims` (`lodestar-sources` is only a dev-dependency here).
- **The key comes only from `x`:** 40 hex digits, not all zero. Two `x` tags that disagree drop the event (ambiguous). A magnet in `content` is never read.
- **`i` tags split at the first colon:** `imdb:tt0111161` → (`imdb`, `tt0111161`), `tmdb:movie:693134` → (`tmdb`, `movie:693134`); the scheme is lower-cased ASCII alphanumerics. Each keeps its own scheme, as §5.3 asks; the claims store validates values per scheme. `tcat:` and `newznab:` go to tags whole.
- **Trackers** are kept only with a `udp`, `http`, `https` or `wss` scheme, ≤ 2048 bytes and no whitespace or hidden characters, deduplicated. Whether one may be announced to is `net::tracker::public_tracker_url`'s check where it's used (NOS-3), not here.
- **Adult:** a `t` tag equal (ignoring ASCII case) to `nsfw`, `porn`, `xxx` or `adult`, or a `content-warning` reason with one of those as a whole word (both read after hidden characters are removed), sets `adult`, the `content-type:adult` claim. Other warnings don't. What the claim may do is ADH-0's (owner §2.47).
- **Publisher** is the pubkey as a NIP-19 `npub` (bech32 written in-house, ~40 lines, checked against NIP-19's example).
- **Kept tags:** ≤ 64 of ≤ 128 bytes, hidden characters removed; a longer one is dropped, not cut. Descriptions keep line breaks and tabs, drop other hidden characters and are cut to 64 KiB.
- **Dependency:** `sha2 = "=0.11.0"` (approved in the brief; already the lockfile's one version).
