Architecture §12.1 and §12, owner's decisions §2.11 and §2.19, and the B-log1, B-log2, B-log2-files and B12 decision files. No owner decision is reversed or loosened, and no security or content-safety decision is weakened. The refinements the runner flags to the owner (§2.19) are marked **(§2.19)**. Parts 2 and 3 (the sweep) have their own files (`-r2`, `-r3`).

## The hot path
- **An event's counter is striped by thread, each stripe on its own 128-byte cache line (§2.19).** `Event` holds `on` with its declaration, which nothing writes, and `count: [Padded<AtomicU64>; 8]`. `Padded` is `#[repr(align(128))]`: 128 covers x86's adjacent-line prefetch and Apple silicon's 128-byte lines. A thread takes the next stripe the first time it counts anything; `count()` sums them. It stays `const`-constructible for `declare`. No new dependency.
  - **Why striped:** the bench showed one hot event's single counter is the cost. Before, with the switch and counter side by side, one event hit by 4 or 8 threads cost 50–130 ns an occurrence, and adjacent events 60–110 ns. Padding alone fixed adjacent events but not one shared event (71–75 ns). Fetch failures come from every fetch-pool worker at once, so the brief's condition for striping holds.
  - **The increment is a relaxed load and store on the thread's own stripe, not a locked `fetch_add`.** A locked increment costs about 5.5 ns on its own (the bench's unpadded single-thread case), over the 5 ns gate. With more than 8 counting threads, two can share a stripe and, racing, lose an increment. The count only feeds the volume estimates and rates per hour, which are estimates already.
  - **Memory:** an event is now 1,152 bytes of statics (one line for its declaration and switch, eight for its counter). At a few hundred events that's a few hundred KiB.
- **The bench is ignored release tests in the binary crate** (`logging/bench.rs`, the runner's decision 4): `hot_off`, `hot_contended`, `hot_on`, `hot_distinct`. One CI job (`log-bench`) runs them with `LODESTAR_LOG_BENCH_GATE=1`, `--test-threads=1`, when `lodestar-log` or the binary's `logging/` changes (`ci_changes.py`'s `log_bench`). It isn't in `ci-ok`, like the other bench gates.
- **Gates (runner's refinement §2.19):** (a) an event that's off costs at most 5 ns through `log_event!`; (b) padded, 8 threads on adjacent events cost at most twice (a). (b)'s threads are normalised to the cores the runner has (the wall time times `min(threads, cores)`, over the events), so a 4-core runner isn't charged for time-slicing 8 threads, and its (a) is the same harness's one-thread figure, so the ratio is contention alone. (c) and (d) are reported.
- **The `default_on` rule (runner's refinement §2.19).** An event the DHT or fetch sweep adds or converts may be `default_on: true` only if, at its peak rate with distinct lines, its CPU (rate × (d)'s ns a line) is at most 1% of one core **and** its file volume (rate × (d)'s bytes a line × 86,400) is at most 256 MiB / 14 days (about 18.3 MiB a day). Over either, it's `default_on: false` (it joins at Detailed). **Over either by 10× or more, it's `Trace`** (it joins only at Everything), with a periodic `Info` summary on by default instead. The brief said "if it would still swamp Detailed by the same measure"; 10× is the reading chosen, since Detailed's own budget isn't defined anywhere else.
- **Peak rates, from the code:** the DHT at Aggressive sends 1,000 queries a second, half of them `sample_infohashes`; a reply fits a 1,500-byte datagram, so about 70 samples: **35,000 sampled hashes a second** at most. `get_peers` and `announce_peer` come from other nodes; the actor sizes for **2,000** replies a second. Fetches run 1,000 at once at Aggressive; a refused connection fails in far less than the 4 s connect timeout, so at 50 ms an attempt **20,000 failures a second** at most.
- **Measured (4-vCPU container, release; the PR has CI's):** (a) about 1.5 ns. (b) padded, 8 threads, adjacent about 1.5× (a); one shared event under 2 ns, against 50–130 ns unpadded. (c) about 3.7 µs a line, the same line each time (folding takes it after the first). (d) about 11.5 µs and 296 bytes a distinct line. **Verdicts:** `dht.key_harvested` from samples (35,000/s: 40% of a core, 830 GiB a day), from `get_peers`/announce (2,000/s: 2.3%, 48 GiB a day) and `fetch.failed` (20,000/s: 23%, 480 GiB a day) are all **Trace**, each with a periodic summary (part 3).

## URL scrubbing
- **A raw space, `"`, `<`, `>` or backtick in a URL's password goes whole.** After `scheme://` in free text, the userinfo is the wider of two rules: the URL rules up to a hard stop (unchanged), and **the last `@` before any `/` within 512 bytes on the same line** (`url::USERINFO_LOOKAHEAD`). So `https://u:pa ss@host/x` shows `https://[redacted]@host/x`. **Over-redaction is accepted (§2.19):** `see https://a.example and me@x.org` shows `see https://[redacted]@x.org` ("never too little", B-log1 round 3). An `@` past 512 bytes, on the next line, or after a `/` isn't userinfo.
- **A request target in absolute form** (`http://u:p@host/`, as a proxy gets) gets the absolute rules in `Url::request`, and **a query value holding `://` is removed** whatever its name (`/go?to=https://u:p@h/` shows `to=[redacted]`).
- **`Debug` forms of `url::Url` / `reqwest::Url`** (`Url { …, username: "u", password: Some("p"), path: "/ann/<passkey>", query: Some("passkey=k"), fragment: Some("f") }`, plain or `{:#?}`): the password, username, query and fragment values read `[redacted]`, and the path keeps only what the URL rules keep (passkey-like runs removed). A credential name's value written as `Some("…")` is the quoted value inside. Tested with the real `Debug` output (`url` is a dev-dependency of `lodestar-log`, as the brief approved; `proptest`, already in the lockfile and every parser crate's dev-dependencies, is one too).
- **`scrub_text` is linear.** It remembers the next match of `://` and of `magnet:` and only searches again once it has passed them, and the userinfo look-ahead is bounded, so each byte is searched a bounded number of times. `redact_secrets` and `hide_runs` were already single passes (each scan ends where the next one starts). A test holds 1 MiB of each adversarial pattern (`a://`, `magnet:`, `token=`, `@`, `://u:p@`, and spaced variants) within 30× the time for 64 KiB, by ratio, never by wall-clock alone; a property test holds that a planted `u:<secret>@` or `?passkey=<secret>` never gets through, and that nothing panics.
- History and redacted downloads re-scrub with these rules (B-log2's tests pass unchanged).
- **No swept line may `?`-format anything holding a URL.** `tracker_scrape::{TrackerUrl, HttpTracker}` derive `Debug` over one; that's a carry-forward for that crate's owner (runner's decision 6).

## Windows log-folder privacy
- **A shared helper for `%SystemRoot%\Temp\lodestar-service.log`** (`windows::temp_log`). It opens the file for append with `FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT` through `OpenOptionsExt::custom_flags`, so a link is opened as itself, then checks **the opened handle's** attributes: anything but a regular file that isn't a reparse point is refused and nothing is written. A junction can't be opened as a file at all. No `unsafe`. The logging folder's `service_log` (prefix `logging`) and the service's `log_refusal` (prefix `service refused to start`) both use it; the logging lines still name no path. A hard link planted there isn't detected (std exposes no link count on stable); a carry-forward if the runner wants one.
- **The owner and the DACL decide "Private", not the index's flag (§2.19: ACL-based folder privacy).** For an existing folder, `windows::acl::log_folder` reads its SDDL (`Get-Acl` through Windows PowerShell from System32, the path in an environment variable). It's private only when:
  - its owner is SYSTEM, Administrators or the account Lodestar runs as (from `whoami /user`, read once; the service's virtual account `NT SERVICE\Lodestar` under the service); and
  - its DACL, inherited entries included, allows only those, `OWNER RIGHTS` (how `restrict_dir_acl` grants the owner) and `CREATOR OWNER`. Deny entries are fine; a NULL DACL, object or conditional entries are refused.
  - **Unlike the data folder's check, the DACL may inherit**: the service's logs folder is made inside the private data folder and inherits its ACL, and must read as private. The data folder's own check (`check_dacl`, protected only) is unchanged; both share one parser.
  - A folder whose descriptor can't be read, or that is a link, isn't private (fail closed: `logging.folder_shared`, file logging stays on as before).
- **`restricted` is now a hint:** `Option<bool>`, written `true` only when Lodestar made and restricted the folder. A rebuilt (corrupt) index has `None`, and `false` is never written, so a save after a rebuild can't turn a folder Lodestar made into a shared one. A planted `"restricted": true` in a shared folder changes nothing.
- **`files.rs::folder()`** (the API's not-yet-opened folder) runs the same check on Windows and returns `not_private` when it fails. The answer is cached per folder for the run, since the check starts PowerShell. An existing folder's ACL is never changed.
- **The service's runtime wiring** (`init_with_writer`, its four lines as events) is in part 2, with the rest of `windows/**`'s sweep, as the brief's PR plan lists it.

## The CI check and `TARGETS`
- **`log_lint.py` parses manifests with `tomllib`** and walks every dependency table outside dev-dependencies: `dependencies`, `build-dependencies` (and the underscored spellings), and the same under `target.<cfg>`, inline tables included. It resolves `package = …` renames and `x.workspace = true` through `[workspace.dependencies]`. It refuses the `log` crate and a renamed `tracing` there. The bench's manifest is checked too. A manifest that doesn't parse is a finding.
  - Changed from before: a renamed `tracing` in **dev-dependencies** is no longer refused, as the brief says ("anywhere but dev-dependencies"): tests may log any way they like.
- **`--base` fails closed:** a base whose copy of the script exists but whose `NOT_YET_SWEPT` or `EXEMPT` can't be read is a finding, and so is a `--base` ref that doesn't resolve. A base without the script still compares with nothing.
- **`EXEMPT` may only shrink too:** with `--base`, a `(path, rule)` the base's copy doesn't have is a finding.
- **`TARGETS` covers every crate:** a test reads every workspace member's `package.name` (`crates/*` and `bench`) and holds that each has an entry and each entry names a crate. `lodestar_update` was missing; it's added under `System`, and so is `lodestar_bench` (the fixture generator linked into `serve --fixture`) rather than exempting it.
- **Every declared catalogue is registered:** a test finds each top-level `catalogue!` in the workspace's sources (a test's own catalogue sits indented in its test module) and holds that its name is in `logging::CATALOGUES`. A catalogue left out fails it.
