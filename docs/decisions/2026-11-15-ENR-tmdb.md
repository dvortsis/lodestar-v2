# ENR-tmdb decisions (TMDB parser and plugin, Account)

Slice ENR-tmdb (API lane brief, 2026-11-15). Spec: `runner/slices.yaml` (ENR-tmdb: §18.2, §18.7; notes "/security-review; attribution shown per its terms"), plan `docs/plan/2026-11-05-v07-m7.md` (Q5, Q12, the TMDB cap ≤ 10/s), UI review `docs/review/2026-11-13-ui-ux/02-skills-pass-and-future-modules.md` §5.5, and ENR-14's pattern (`docs/decisions/2026-11-12-ENR-14.md`, "How to add the next plugin").

## Terms of use (owner §2.28), checked 2026-11-15
- **How it was checked:** TMDB's own pages, read directly on 2026-11-15: the API Terms of Use (`https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use`, last updated 2023-10-20), the rate-limiting page (`https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/rate-limiting`) and the `search/movie` reference (`https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/search-movie`).
- **What they require, and what the plugin does:**
  - **Attribution.** The terms require (a) the notice "This [website, program, service, application, product] uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB." shown prominently, and (b) TMDB's logo to identify the use, less prominent than the app's own branding. The notice is carried in `Terms::attribution`; see "Terms (ENR-terms)" below, which also records the logo still needed.
  - **Rate limits.** TMDB documents "somewhere in the 40 requests per second range", subject to change, with a 429 over it; the terms ban use that degrades the service. **The plugin holds the plan's cap: at most 10 requests a second, no burst** (`HostRate::per_second(10)`), one request a lookup, and the host limiter backs off from 60 s to 6 h after a 401, 403, 429 or 5xx. The runtime's own daily cap (`PER_DAY`, 100,000) and caches apply on top.
  - **Caching: at most 6 months.** The terms bar caching TMDB information for longer than six months. ENR-10's lookup cache keeps a match 30 days and a miss 7 days, within it. Stored claims are settled by `retention: Retention::days(180)`; see "Terms (ENR-terms)" below, including the X-snapshot-claims-retention dependency.
  - **Non-commercial use.** The base licence excludes commercial use (charging for the app, ad revenue). Lodestar is free and self-hosted, and each install uses **the user's own TMDB account and token**, under their own acceptance of TMDB's terms. Nothing is resold or sublicensed.
  - **No AI/ML training, no redistribution, no derivative datasets.** Lodestar does none of these. Whether TMDB-sourced claims may leave the install through Torznab or the bitmagnet export (OUT-2) is settled by `redistribute: false`; see "Terms (ENR-terms)" below.
  - **Images.** No poster or backdrop path is read or kept (UI review 02 §5.5 already decided no posters).
  - **The key.** The terms have no confidentiality clause, but the token is the user's account credential: it lives only in the credential store (SRC-4), is read on each lookup, sent only in the `Authorization` header and never in a URL, a log, an error, Debug output, a fixture or a commit.

## Terms (ENR-terms), checked 2026-11-16
ENR-terms (#962) gives every plugin a `Terms` and an adult marker on each answer (`crates/sources/src/terms.rs`). TMDB's `tmdb::TERMS`, from its API Terms of Use (last updated 2023-10-20, read again on 2026-11-16):
- **`background_walk: false`.** The licence is "non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable", bars "an excessive amount of bandwidth" and treats collecting data sets of TMDB content as commercial use. Nothing clearly allows bulk use, so it looks up only the items you open, within its cap.
- **`redistribute: false`.** The terms bar sublicensing or passing on TMDB content without a written agreement. Its answers stay in Lodestar's own UI and never rename the record that search, Torznab and export serve. This settles the redistribution question above the strict way.
- **`retention: Retention::days(180)`**, set explicitly rather than through the shared default, so a later change to the default can't loosen it. The terms bar caching anything from TMDB "for longer than 6 months". The runtime refreshes a claim on the next interest after 150 days and drops it at 179 days whether anyone opens the item or not, under the shortest six months (181 days); `tmdb_terms` checks it. This settles the caching question above. **Merge order:** ENR-terms (`docs/decisions/2026-11-16-ENR-terms.md`, line 84) records that X-snapshot-claims-retention must merge before ENR-tmdb, because migration snapshots (`lodestar.pre-v<N>.db`) are kept 90 days and would hold TMDB claims past this retention. That slice has no entry yet; the runner lands it first or records a changed order.
- **`attribution`: `tmdb::ATTRIBUTION`, linking `tmdb::ATTRIBUTION_URL` (`https://www.themoviedb.org`).** The notice in the terms' current words ("… uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB."), with Lodestar named. The older wording ("This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.") is superseded by it. **`logo: None` for now:** the terms also require TMDB's logo, and no bundled logo asset exists yet. Adding TMDB's logo from its own logo page is still needed before the source can be switched on.
- **`adult_marker_required: true`, `adult` from TMDB's own `adult` field.** Every TMDB search result has `adult`. The parser now reads it as given (`Hit::adult` is `Some(true)`, `Some(false)`, or `None` when the key is missing) instead of reading a missing key as not adult. A result is chosen only when TMDB marks it not adult (`adult: false`): one marked adult, or with no marker, is never chosen, so `choose` fails closed by itself as well as through the runtime's terms check (`an_unmarked_answer_is_refused_by_the_terms`; independent review, 2026-11-16). The request still sends `include_adult=false`.

## Account class, off by default
- **The manifest:** `tmdb`, an Enrichment source, Consent `Account`, endpoint `https://api.themoviedb.org` only, `enabled_by_default: false`, risk Low, never always-adult, never finds private.
- **The credential:** one, `read_access_token` (label "API read access token", Secret, required). TMDB offers a v3 "API key" (sent as the `api_key` URL parameter) and a "read access token" (a bearer token). **Only the token is accepted**, so the credential never goes in a URL, where proxies and logs could keep it. A token is 1–2,048 bytes of ASCII letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.` (a JWT), checked before it's put in a header.
- **No host wiring** (as ENR-14): library code listed in the catalogue; the host wiring and the Legal Notice line land with the slice that registers enrichment plugins.

## What is sent (Q5)
- **One request a lookup, from a fixed template** (`search_request`): `GET https://api.themoviedb.org/3/search/{movie|tv}?query=<title>&include_adult=false&language=en-US&page=1[&primary_release_year=<year> | &first_air_date_year=<year>]`, headers `Accept` and `Authorization: Bearer <token>` only.
- **The year is sent** when the query has one (Wikidata didn't send it). TMDB's one page holds 20 results ranked by popularity, so a common title's right film can be past it without the year; Q5 allows the year, and the match still requires it locally. **The kind** is the path (film or series), as Q5 allows. Nothing else: never the hash, the name, the files.
- **The title is one URL parameter**, encoded by the `url` crate's form encoder (`a_hostile_title_is_one_parameter`). `include_adult=false` is fixed, and an adult-marked result is never chosen anyway.
- **The Sources row's text** (UI review 02 §5.5): `tmdb::SENDS` says exactly that: "The parsed title, its year and whether it's a film or a TV series, with your TMDB read access token, to api.themoviedb.org. Never the info hash, the torrent's name or its files." Like the attribution, no manifest field exists for it yet (no plugin has one); **for the runner:** the Sources row should read it from the plugin when that field lands.
- **Kinds: Movie and TV.** Any other kind, or a title that folds to nothing, sends nothing and reads no token.

## The parser (`sources-feeds/src/tmdb.rs`)
- **On the shared bounded JSON reader**, as Wikidata's. Pure, no I/O, no panics, no unwraps, no truncating casts. Linear time: repeats of read keys are found with a bitmask (`many_unknown_keys_are_read_in_linear_time`: 300,000 unknown keys).
- **The caps (`LIMITS`):** 512 KiB (a page of 20 results with overviews is a few tens of KiB), depth 8 (a result's deepest part is `genre_ids`, depth 3), 50,000 values, 1,000 elements per array, 4 KiB per decoded string, 20 results (TMDB's page size; more stops the parse), 16 genre ids per result (TMDB lists 19 film and 16 TV genres; a title has a few).
- **Strict shape:** whole only when read to its end, `results` an array, no `status_code` or `success` key (TMDB's error object), no repeated or escaped root key, no trailing bytes and nothing skipped. In a result, an escaped or repeated read key, a wrong type, an id that isn't a positive integer in plain digits, a date that isn't empty or `YYYY-MM-DD` (years 1–9999), or a genre id that isn't a `u32` spoils it. `null` is accepted for the date and the original language only. `xx` (TMDB's "no language") isn't kept.
- **What it keeps:** id, title (`title` or `name`), original title, original language, year, genre ids, adult flag. Overviews, image paths, popularity and votes are passed over undecoded.

## Choosing a match (`tmdb::choose`)
- **One result must agree:** not marked adult, its title or original title equal to the query's after folding (Wikidata's `fold`), and the query's year when it has one. None, or more than one, is no match.
- **Confidence:** 90 with a year, 70 without, under ENR-10's 95.
- **The answer:** `tmdb:<id>` only (the search doesn't return IMDb ids; that would be a second request per lookup); the English title as canonical; the original title, with its language, as an alternative when it differs; genres by name from TMDB's fixed genre lists (`tmdb::genre`), so no answer text becomes a genre; the year. ENR-10's `into_claims` clamps it, and the writer's ingest judges every text (`an_answer_is_judged_and_a_held_one_is_not_stored`).
- **TMDB film and series ids share the `tmdb` scheme**, as Wikidata already writes them; ENR-11's `tmdb:` field is the same for both. A film and a series with the same number are different works; the torrent's kind tells them apart.

## Errors
- 200 parsed; 401 or 403 "TMDB refused the read access token"; 429 "limiting requests"; after any of those or a 5xx, the host limiter holds the host and the next lookup fails at once without a request ("on hold"); a 3xx is never followed; any other status or a transport failure "the request failed"; a 200 that isn't whole, including TMDB's error object, "wasn't understood". No token stored: "needs setup". **None of them is a miss**, so none is cached as one, and no error holds the service's text or the token.

## Fixtures
