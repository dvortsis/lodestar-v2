# X-purge-index-shapes decisions (#1135)

Slice session, 2026-11-27. Spec: architecture §20.5 and §20.9; `docs/decisions/2026-11-24-ENR-G.md`, `2026-11-24-ENR-11.md` and `2026-11-24-X-purge-rederive.md`; the runner's brief `briefs/X-purge-index-shapes.md` (2026-11-27T08:30Z), from its audit of #1117. Baseline: `main` at 1b46c223.

- **The gap.** The index serves an answer when its details section holds a match confidence (`Store::served_identifications`, ENR-11). A purge re-derived only torrents whose lost claim `Claims::shapes_the_record`, which also asked for a canonical title (ENR-G). So an answer with ids or alternative titles and no canonical title was served but never re-derived, and its ids and titles stayed in the index after its source was purged. A purge also never logged a torrent for the index itself; the re-derivation's `reindex_log` entry was the only route, and it needed a later backfill pass.
- **One shared function, not a property test alone.** `Claims::identification` (an answer and its confidence) is the claim-level test both predicates make: `served_identifications` applies it, then its row, torrent and terms checks, and `shapes_the_record` is an adult section with a kind or `identification()`. So every served row shapes the record by construction. `crates/store/tests/unverified.rs::served_identifications_and_record_shapes_agree_on_every_shape` holds them together anyway over every shape (18 valid ones: no section, adult with and without a kind, and every combination of canonical title, confidence, alternative title and id).
- **`shapes_the_record` widens; the served rule doesn't narrow.** Narrowing serving to answers with a canonical title would drop ids ENR-11 serves today. Widening the shape means a purge (and the pipeline's `enrich::mark_matched`, which shares the test) now also re-derives torrents holding an answer without a canonical title. Their derivation produces the same record (the effective title needs a canonical title), so the cost is the backfill pass for those torrents only.
- **A purge logs the torrent for the index in the same transaction as the delete.** The Claims stage writes a `reindex_log` entry for each torrent whose lost row shaped it (or didn't decode), beside the ENR-G mark. The index reads served answers from the claims rows, so its next pass drops the purged ids and titles whether or not the derivation changes anything, and a crash after the commit still leaves the torrent logged. `a_purge_deletes_claims_and_logs_them_together_or_not_at_all` fails the log insert and checks the delete rolled back.
- **The two other live paths that delete a claims row do the same.** A refresh's drop or retention expiry (`drop_one`, through `Store::drop_claims` and `Store::drop_claims_outside`) and a refile that holds a claim back (`Store::refile_claims`) now log a shaping row's torrent in their transaction. Before, a drop reached the index only through the backfill's re-derivation, and a refile not at all: a held-back answer stayed served. Plain feed claims still log nothing.
- **A refile also re-derives what the held-back claim shaped** (found by the self-review). It marks the torrent (`parser_ver = 0`) and moves the classification generation once, as the purge's Claims stage does (§20.9), so a held-back answer's canonical title doesn't stay the record's effective title (ENR-10) and a backfill batch that read it is refused. `ClaimsRefiled::rederive` reports it, as `PurgeStep::rederive` does.
- **Not done here, for the runner:**
  - **Re-arming a running writer after a refile.** The pipeline's refile job (`crates/pipeline/src/unverified.rs`, outside these owns) doesn't read `ClaimsRefiled::rederive` yet, so the record is re-derived at the writer's next backfill pass (the next start, or the next purge or version change), not at once. The index drops the ids and titles at once either way.
  - **An answer replaced in place** (`put_claims` over an existing row) isn't logged for the index by the store itself; it reaches the index when the pipeline derives the torrent again after writing it. That's not a purge, so it's outside this brief.
  - Snapshot purges (`snapshots.rs`, `snapshots/claims.rs`) act on snapshot files the index never reads, so nothing there changed.

## Tests
