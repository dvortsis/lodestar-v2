# ENR-9b decisions (details section stored and judged)

Slice ENR-9b (runner brief, 2026-11-06T21:35Z). It turns on the sources→store mapping of ENR-9's details section (`docs/decisions/2026-11-06-ENR-9.md`, "Outside the brief's owns"), judged before storage as the adult section is. Spec: plan `docs/plan/2026-11-05-v07-m7.md` (ENR-9, ENR-10 rows), owner §2.37, §2.46/§2.47 (Q5), and the #383 rule that every claim text that is stored passes the filter.

## Still inactive in practice
- **No source sends a details section yet.** The mapping is on, but nothing produces the section until UN-1, IRC-1 or ENR-10 lands, each off by default (§18.7). So the plan's "may land before the cut, inactive" still holds: no row is written as version 3 on an install until one of those plugins is enabled.

## The mapping (`store_claims`, `attach_details`)
- **The section is stored as the emitter's clamp left it** (`lodestar_sources::Claims::clamp`, which applies ENR-9's rules). The mapping doesn't clamp again; it checks.
- **Never beside an adult section.** The clamp drops the section there; `attach_details` drops it too, so it doesn't rely on that. A `debug_assert` was considered and rejected: the property test sends unclamped claims, and an assert would turn a refusal into a panic.
- **Never beside a description or publisher.** On a clamped input they're already gone (the clamp drops them when a section survives), so the section is kept. On any other input, the **section** is dropped and the description and publisher are kept, which is exactly what version 1 stored before. Dropping the section, not the personal fields, keeps every v1 input byte-identical.
- **Only when the store would take it.** The claims, section included, must pass `Claims::check` (limits, `enrichment ids`, the total). Otherwise the section is dropped and the rest goes on as before. So a feed can't get its other claims refused by attaching a bad section, and `ingest`'s `invalid` counter never counts a section the mapping could have dropped.
  - The check runs before the trackers go in; they're then fitted to the room the section leaves (`stored_trackers`), so the total still holds.
- **The property test** (`unverified_details.rs`) checks both directions: clamped claims keep the section exactly when the clamp kept it and round-trip; claims with an unclamped section always pass `check` and either store the section as sent or drop it. Neither ever errors or panics.

## `private_source`
- **A private find keeps its section, with no extra rule.** My reading:
  - `external_ref` and the trackers are dropped for a private find because a private tracker's links can carry a passkey (runner, L3). No details field is a link or a credential: a category string, a posting time, group names, titles, genres, a year and a confidence.
  - What private-source isolation (§18.7) needs is that the row never leaves the instance. The row carries the `private_source` flag for the claimed name and files already, and the section is part of that row, so it gets the same treatment: never served over Torznab or Stremio, exported or sent to public peer finding.
  - A private tracker's category names are its own taxonomy, not personal data. A Newznab poster (often an email address) can't reach the section: it has no such field (ENR-9 "No personal fields"), and UN-1 can't store it as a publisher beside a section.
- **For ENR-11 and ENR-12:** reading the section for search or the detail view must apply the row's `private_source` flag exactly as it applies to the claimed name.

## Judging (`judge_in`, `details_texts`)
- **Every free-text field is judged as a name, through the same full pipeline** as the claimed name, description and publisher: the locked floor, the core, your rules, packs and lists, with your classification rules, and judged as adult too when an always-adult source sent it (a test shows the Adult-only core holding there). The texts are the canonical title, each alternative title, the source category, each genre and each newsgroup.
- **The same verdict combination:** a floor or core hit on any text returns at once and holds the key; otherwise the first other hold drops the source's claims and holds nothing (#383).
- **Joined texts as well, stricter only** (my call, as the adult section does with its context, `enrichment_texts`):
  - all the section's texts together;
  - the claimed name with each text, and with all of them together;
  - **the verified name of a torrent stored under the key** with each text and all of them, at ingest, in the refile and at promotion. ENR-10's rows are mostly about stored torrents, and may claim no name, or another one.
  - A feed's category or a plugin's genre describes that very item, so a pattern split between them is caught: a child-role genre on a sexual name.
  - **Each pair is its own text,** not the name with every field in one string. The core's pair rules look within 8 words (12 for Adult-only), so one long string would let padding in one field (a long canonical title, 32 alternative titles) hide another. A test pads both before the genre.
  - The joined texts can only add a hold. Every text is still judged alone.
  - **Cost in filter runs:** at most 90 texts alone (1 canonical title, 32 alternative titles, 32 distinct language tags, 1 category, 16 genres, 8 newsgroups), one joined, and 91 more for each of the two names: 273 runs per hash at the limits. A Newznab or IRC item with a category and a few groups needs about a dozen.
  - **Cost in bytes** (`details_judged_len`, exact): about 0.5 MB of text for one row at the limits, mostly the 91 pairs with a 4,096-byte verified name (`max_name_bytes`) and a 1,024-byte claimed name. A small section on a long name costs a few KB. The refile's budget below bounds a step; ingest is bounded below too.
- **Language tags are judged too** (runner review of #551, blocking). `language_ok` lets a tag carry up to 35 bytes of `-`-separated subtags, so a tag such as `rolea-lexa` can spell short words. Each distinct tag is judged as a text with its `-`s read as spaces, alone and with each name, as the adult section reads a scene id's slug. A test holds a key on a tag alone.
- **Not judged, because they aren't text:** `posted_at`, `year` and `match_confidence` are numbers; the ids beside the section are scheme–value pairs `id_ok` checks. The doc comment on `details_texts` says so.
- **The refile re-judges the section** with no new code: the sweep calls `judge`/`judge_in` on each stored row, and they judge the section. A test shows a rule added after storage blocking a key through an alternative title alone, on an unverified item and on a stored torrent's claims, and a core term added later holding the torrent's hash.

## Files outside the slice's owns (runner, 2026-11-06T21:48Z)
- **`crates/pipeline/src/writer.rs`, the `promote` call site only.** The `/security-review` pass found that promotion re-judged an unverified item with `judge`, with no context. A details pair completed by the verified name (a child-role genre claimed first, a sexual name fetched later) was then promoted. Only a refile after the next rules change would have caught it, so arrival order, which a feed controls, decided whether the pair was judged.
  - The runner approved the fix in this slice: "A content-safety gap that depends on arrival order belongs with the change that makes details live."
  - `promote` now calls `judge_in` with the torrent's verified name when the claims carry a details section. Nothing else in `writer.rs` changes.
  - A pipeline test (`unverified_details.rs`, `promotion`) holds the pair at promotion and promotes a clean item beside it. It fails without the fix. The other order (the torrent stored first, the claims after) is the ingest test `the_section_is_judged_with_the_verified_name`.
  - The runner adds `writer.rs` to ENR-9b's owns in `runner/slices.yaml`.

## Sizes
- **The refile judges at most `REFILE_JUDGED_BYTES` (32 MiB) of text per step**, past its first row (runner review of #551). SRC-1's pages bound the decoded claims (`PAGE_BYTES`, 8 MiB, and 1,024 rows), but joining a section with the names multiplies the judged text: 1,024 rows at the limits would be about 500 MB in one blocking step, holding the writer's store.
  - A step that reaches the budget ends early, and the next goes on from the last row it judged. Its first row is always judged, so the sweep always moves; one row is at most about 0.5 MB.
  - Both phases count it: items with no verified name, claims with it. `judged_len` is the claims' own text plus the section's joined texts exactly.
  - A test with 4 KB names and full sections shows the steps ending early, every step moving on, and the sweep reaching every row.
- **Ingest's bound is the claims channel's, not this budget.** A writer step takes at most `CLAIMS_STEP_KIB` (8 MiB) of claims. `claims_kib` doesn't know the verified name, so it doesn't count the pairs. Worst case: 8 MiB of the smallest sections (one genre, about 1 KiB charged each), each on a stored torrent with a 4 KB name, is about 8,000 discoveries of a few pairs each, about 40–70 MB of judged text in one step. A full section is charged at its size, so fewer of them fit. That's within what the crawler's own batches judge. Nothing sends a section yet; if UN-1 or ENR-10 shows ingest steps running long, a judged-bytes split there is the follow-up.
- **`Claims::check` runs twice for a discovery with a section** (`attach_details`, then `ingest`), deliberately: `ingest`'s check covers every claim, and claims with no section don't reach `attach_details`'s. It's linear in the claims and cheap beside judging them.
- **`claims_kib` already counts the section** at `Details::charged_len` (through `lodestar_sources::Claims::text_len`), so the claims channel's byte budget bounds it. A test shows a full section adds its charge, and a full section beside a file list at the total still stores within `MAX_TOTAL` and `MAX_STORED`.

## Notes for the slices that depend on this
- **ENR-10:** write the section through `Found::claims` and the emitter; don't build store claims yourself. Always a non-empty section (at least `match_confidence`, ENR-9), so the closed id schemes apply. Your rows go through `ingest` and are judged here; don't judge them again in `identify.rs`, but don't skip `ingest` either. An overview goes nowhere: no description beside a section.
- **UN-1:** the Newznab category, `usenetdate` and groups go in the section. The poster is never stored: not as a publisher (it would drop the section on an unclamped path, and the clamp drops it anyway), not anywhere else. Normalise ids to `id_ok`'s forms first, or the clamp drops them.
- **IRC-1:** the announce's category goes in `source_category`. It's judged as a name, so a category alone can drop or hold a key. Never put a URL or anything passkeyed in any section field: none of them is stripped for a private find.
