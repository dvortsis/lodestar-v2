Spec: plan `docs/plan/2026-11-05-v07-m7.md` §4 (ENR-10 row), §6 Q5 and Q12 (decided as the plan rows, owner §2.46/§2.47), architecture §6, §18.1, §18.2, §18.7 and §20.9, `docs/decisions/2026-11-06-ENR-9.md`, `2026-11-06-ENR-9b.md` and `2026-11-07-runner-followups.md`.

## Inactive until a plugin is enabled
- **The harness is library code with tests, and has no host wiring** (runner, 2026-11-12). `lodestar_sources::identify` is the plugin contract and `lodestar_pipeline::identify` the runtime. No plugin exists yet, and neither `PipelineConfig` nor `services.rs` registers one: ENR-14, the first plugin, wires it in. Even then, nothing is sent until you enable its source and its consent holds (§18.7). So the plan's "may land before the cut, inactive" holds.
- **Named `identify`** so it's never confused with ENR-1's adult `enrich` contract. Nothing here touches `adult_enrich.rs`: Adult goes only through ENR-1's services (Q5).

## What may be sent (Q5)
- **A `Query` is the only thing a plugin is ever handed:** the parsed title (at most 256 bytes, control characters removed), the year and the kind of work. It has no field for an info hash, the raw name, files, trackers or anything about the install. Its `Debug` prints lengths only.
- **The title and year are parsed from the torrent's name alone, never with its files,** as ENR-1 does. A name that's ambiguous, or that only resolves from the file tree, sends nothing (`Ineligible::NoQuery`). My reading: Q5's "never … files" covers a title taken from a file or folder name. That's stricter than reading it as the file list only, so an item whose name says nothing is never looked up by title.
- **The kind** comes from the stored classification. Only Movie, TV, Music, Audiobook, Ebook, Comic, Software and Game are ever sent; Video, Images, Other and Adult never are. A plugin lists the kinds it serves (`Identifier::kinds`), and an item of any other kind isn't sent to it. So a software title never reaches a film database.

## Eligibility (one gate, `identify::check`)
- **Right before every lookup, user interest and background alike, the gate reads:**
  - the plugin's kill switch (SRC-5);
  - its source is enabled;
  - its consent condition holds (`consent::need_in`, SRC-4).
- **The item must be all of these:**
  - a stored torrent with verified metadata. An unverified item's id isn't a torrent's, and a partial import (`FILES_PARTIAL`) wasn't verified;
  - none of its swarm hashes held (tombstone, quarantine, the floor's or a dropping list);
  - not hidden, and not dropped, quarantined or hidden by the rules as they judge it now;
  - not `PRIVACY` (a private source or BEP 27, verified or not);
  - not Adult by any reading: stored adult, an Is adult correction, an always-adult source (unless your Not adult correction), an Is adult rule now, or detection now;
  - not marked for reclassification. Until the backfill reaches it, its kind may be stale.
- **The plugin-wide conditions come first.** While one fails, nothing is read about the item, and an opened item is dropped, never sent later. A property test over random stores and switches shows that the running runtime hands the plugin exactly the eligible items.

## Order and caps (Q12)
- **User interest first** (`Interest::opened`, the detail drawer; a queue of 64), **then a background walk** over stored torrents by id.
- **At most 100,000 lookups per plugin per UTC day** (`PER_DAY`, Q12), opened and background together, and never more than the host sets.
  - One cap for both is stricter than Q12's "after user interest", which would leave interest outside the cap (runner, 2026-11-12, accepted as stricter). It's the same rule as ENR-1.
  - An opened item jumps the queue but waits, first in line, once the day's count is spent.
  - The count fails closed: one that can't be read or written is spent. A clock that stepped back can't hold the count (ENR-1's #502 fix).
- **Pacing:** each lookup waits at least the plugin's own interval (`Identifier::min_interval`, its documented rate cap, never below 100 ms). The plugin's `Policy` still caps every request through its client.
- **A refused background item costs a 5 ms pause,** so a walk over a large store is a steady, light load of about 200 items a second.
- **Restart resume:** the cursor, the round's rest and the day's counts live in the plugin's `source_state` under `enr10/`. The plugin can neither read nor write that prefix.

## Cache
- **In `source_state`:** a hit is cached for 30 days, a miss for 7, as ENR-1's numbers. The runner can change them under §2.26. A failed lookup isn't cached.
- **256 buckets of 4,096 entries:** about a million entries, a quarter of the plugin's 1,024 state keys. A full bucket drops what expires soonest. An item dropped early is only asked again sooner, within the day's cap.

## Answers
- **An `Identification` becomes claims through `into_claims`:** the work's ids, plus a details section with the canonical title, the alternative titles, genres and year. The section always holds the match confidence (capped at 100), so it's never empty and ENR-9's closed id schemes apply. There's no name, description, publisher, file or tracker: an overview or a poster could name a person (ENR-9). The emitter's clamp applies ENR-9's rules. A property test over arbitrary answers shows the claims are always bounded, in stored form and free of those fields.
- **One row per plugin:** each plugin's answer is its own claims row under its source id, so two plugins that disagree are both kept, each with its confidence ("conflicts kept as alternatives"). The surest one with a canonical title names the effective title (`identify::stored_title`).
- **Only a source that didn't find the torrent can name its title.** The backfill has no list of the plugins (its context is built in `writer.rs`, which this slice doesn't edit), so `stored_title` uses the store's own invariant instead. Every finder's claims arrive with its bit in the torrent's `sources` (`put_unverified`), and an identification never sets one. So a feed that sends a canonical title for a torrent it found can't rename it. Private claims never count.
- **Never a find** (`unverified::is_identification`, runner 2026-11-12). An answer is a details section with only ids beside it, from a registered `Kind::Enrichment` source that isn't a listed adult enricher. `ingest` gives it ENR-1's match path: `put_claims` on the stored torrent under its key. So:
  - it sets no source bit;
  - it isn't a sighting or a rediscovery;
  - there's no `make_public`;
  - it never creates an unverified item. Before this, the usual path (`put_unverified`) did all four. On a torrent deleted after the lookup, it created an unverified item the verifier would then fetch.
  - **It's refused** for a key no torrent holds, through a private source, or on a `PRIVACY` torrent. The runtime never asks about those, so such an answer is stale or forged. The torrent's flags are never touched.
  - Then the torrent is marked for the backfill, and the `reenrich` flag starts it.
- **Judged before storage, by the writer's ingest** (ENR-9b): every text alone, joined, and paired with the claimed and verified names; stricter only; a floor or core hit holds every swarm hash and stores nothing. This module doesn't judge the answer again, and never skips ingest. The tests send answers through `unverified::ingest`.

## Effective title (`classify/src/effective.rs`)
- **`ResolvedVia::Plugin` ("plugin")** joins files, folder and structure, so `resolved:plugin` works in queries and export.
- **`via_plugin`** resolves only an ambiguous name, and only when the plugin is surer than the file tree was. Its confidence is capped at 95, so it never ties an informative name's 100.
  - The plugin's title and year replace the effective ones.
  - Episode numbers, the pack flag and the quality fields still come from the torrent: the plugin knows the work, not this release of it.
  - An informative name always keeps its own title.
- **`identify::with_identified`** rewrites only the title's part of a stored record (release fields, ambiguous, resolved, title confidence). The type, kind and junk score stay what classification made them.
- **Where it applies (`enrich.rs`):**
  - the backfill (`enrich_ids`), after the adult match, so a parser or rules change keeps "resolved via plugin";
  - `apply_matches`'s rewrite;
  - `mark_matched` also marks torrents holding an identification, so a partial import's upgrade, which writes prepare's record, gets its title back.
  - The backfill writes `reindex_log`, so the index picks up the new title.
- **`worker.rs` is unchanged** (runner, 2026-11-12). The plan row said it would read v3 claims, but the prepare stage has no claims: `FetchedKey` carries none, and a remote worker has no store. A stored torrent is reclassified with its claims in the backfill instead. So it isn't in this PR's owns, and #737 doesn't overlap.

## Judged-bytes budget at ingest (runner #557)
- **`claims_kib` charges a details section at what ingest judges for it** (`details_judged_len`). That's the section joined with the claimed name and with a verified name of `JUDGED_NAME_MAX` (12 KiB, the decoded worst case), since intake can't know the torrent the claims land on.
  - The writer's existing step loop then bounds the judged text per ingest step: `CLAIMS_STEP_KIB`, 8 MiB, plus one discovery. It was about 150 MB before.
  - Nothing in `writer.rs` changes.
  - A full section is charged at about 0.6 MB, so the 64 MiB claims channel holds about a hundred in flight.
- **Items phase:** a test in `tests/identify.rs` shows the refile's `REFILE_JUDGED_BYTES` budget ending Items-phase steps early on unverified items with full sections and long names. Every step moves on, and the sweep reaches every item.
