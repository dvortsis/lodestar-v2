# X-utp-spurious-rto decisions (#838)

The follow-up named in `2026-11-12-X-utp-backoff.md` ("Known limit") and `2026-11-12-master-plan-r4.md`. Affects `crates/utp` only (the peer-wire transport, architecture §6). No wire-format, setting or owner-set number changes.

- **A resent packet's ACK gives a round-trip sample when the peer's echo says which copy it answers.** A uTP header carries the sender's timestamp and the delay it measured on the last packet it received (BEP 29), so their difference is our own timestamp on that packet plus how long the peer held its reply. An honest echo is therefore never earlier than the echoed packet's send time, and `now - echo` is a real round trip. Each packet now records when its first, second-to-latest and latest transmissions went out. The sample is measured from the latest of those at or before the echo. When that is the first transmission, the timeout is treated as spurious (under reordering or a stale echo it may have been real, and the sample is then an overestimate, never too short); when it's the latest, the resend got through. A first transmission acknowledged by the same ACK gives the sample instead, since it is exact. Either way the sample resets the backoff, as a first transmission's does, so on a lossy link the backoff no longer compounds across timeouts separated by progress. The sample is never shorter than `now - echo`, which is what Karn's rule protects: an ACK of an earlier copy can't be read as a near-instant answer to the resend.
- **Karn's rule still holds wherever the echo can't vouch for a copy.** No sample is taken when the echo falls before the first transmission or after `now` (forged, wrapped or stale), when the peer sends a zero delay (it had nothing to measure), when the sample would be zero, or for a SYN: the accepting side doesn't refresh its echo for a repeated SYN, so the echo there can be seconds stale. (Taking it broke a `streams_survive_a_bad_link` case: a 4 s sample lifted the first data timeout past the peer's handshake deadline.) The handshake drops the SYN backoff anyway.
- **The peer's timestamps are untrusted; the sample is still our own clock's reading.** The echo only chooses among our own transmission times, so a forged one can't produce a negative or invented duration. Comparisons wrap with the 32-bit microsecond clock, and a transmission older than **`ECHO_HORIZON` (2³¹ µs, about 36 minutes; a new internal constant, the runner's call under §2.26)** isn't matched, which keeps the wrapping comparison unambiguous. Timeouts that count give up within about 7 minutes, but probing a closed window doesn't count, so a packet can outlive the horizon; past it, Karn's rule applies and there's no sample. **`Rtt::sample` now counts any sample beyond `MAX_RTO` (60 s, unchanged) as `MAX_RTO`,** so one outlier can't hold the smoothed round trip up. The worst a lying peer can do is pick the latest resend's time and get a small sample, which it can already get by acknowledging early; it costs that peer's own connection extra resends, within the congestion window.
- **The congestion window isn't restored after a spurious timeout.** Eifel (RFC 3522) also undoes the window cut; we only undo the timer backoff, which is what this slice is about. LEDBAT's slow start back to half the old window recovers it within a few round trips.
- **The timeout count resets only on progress:** our oldest unacknowledged packet being acknowledged. Before, any newly acknowledged byte reset it, so a peer that selectively acknowledged every new packet but never the oldest kept the count at zero for as long as we had new data to send. Now it reaches `MAX_TIMEOUTS` and the connection times out (`a_peer_that_only_acknowledges_later_packets_is_given_up_on`). The retransmission timer still restarts on any newly acknowledged byte, as before.
- **The X-utp-backoff bounds hold and improve.** `a_round_trip_jump_costs_few_retransmissions` (at most 8 resends) now costs 2, 2, 4 and 5 resends at the 1.8, 2.5, 5 and 10 s jumps, against 2, 3, 5 and 6 before: the ACK of the slow first copy now gives the sample a later first transmission used to. The bound itself is unchanged.
