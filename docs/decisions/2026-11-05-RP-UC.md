# RP-UC decisions: the unauthorised-copy pack and MW-6 (PR to be numbered)

Owner §2.39 (an `unauthorised-copy` pack, and cracked software in the malware pack: "Go with your recommendations"), built as `docs/decisions/2026-11-04-X-legal-docs.md` specifies it ("`RP-UC` owns", the tiers, MW-6 fully specified). Ships in v0.5.2 (§2.40). Architecture §20.6 has the rule tables.

- **The `cracked` junk reason** (`classify::junk`, weight 40): a **software** release with the parser's `Cracked` flag **and** a crack word (crack, cracked, keygen, keymaker, activator, k'ed, pre-activated) that the parsed title doesn't account for. `patch` alone, which the lexicon rates a weak cracked token, never counts, so `Firefox.120.0.1.Patch` passes. A title such as `Crack Attack v1.2 Patch` names the program, so its own crack word is taken off. The parser is unchanged. A classification now changes, so **`CLASSIFY_VERSION` goes from 3 to 4**: stored torrents are re-derived in the background on upgrade, as with any classifier change. The reason also feeds the junk score and ranking prior, as every junk reason does (§20.6: the packs and the junk score never disagree). **So switching MW-6 (and UC-2) off shows cracked software again but still ranks it lower:** its 40 junk points stay, as for any junk reason; a *Not junk* correction clears them.
  - **Read from the right text** (runner, #469 review): the crack words are read from the text the release was parsed from: the torrent's name, or for an uninformative name the file or folder name it was resolved from (`Effective::resolved_from`, new). So `a1b2c3` holding `Photoshop.2024.Cracked.exe` is held, and the same name holding `Photoshop.2024.Update.exe` isn't; the packs' Neutralize check reads the same text.
  - **Physical damage isn't a crack:** `crack` or `cracked` right before `screen`, `glass`, `display`, `lcd` or `phone` doesn't count, so `Linux.ISO.cracked.screen.fix` with an `.exe` passes. The runner suggested a fix/repair guard; it isn't taken, because scene `Crack.Fix` releases are fixes for a crack and still cracked, so that guard would weaken the rule. The narrower damage guard costs nothing real.
- **How an upgrade re-derives (`CLASSIFY_VERSION` 3 → 4; the runner flags the bump to the owner).** No migration: the version is part of `ENRICHMENT_VERSION`, and the writer's existing background enrichment does the work.
  - **Incremental and bounded.** `Store::unenriched` selects rows whose stored version isn't the current one, by id, `BACKFILL_BATCH` (256) at a time. Each batch is one short write transaction (`set_enrichments_at`, generation-checked), taken between ingest batches, with the existing retry backoff (5 s doubling to 300 s) after a store error. A `reindex_log` entry per row lets the search index follow in place, with no rebuild.
  - **Resumable.** Progress is the rows themselves: a re-derived row carries the new version, so a restart, a crash or a power cut resumes with the rows still on the old one. Nothing is redone and nothing is lost.
  - **Cost.** Measured with `search-bench --torrents 100000` (release build, a 4-vCPU cloud container): enrichment runs at about **17,300 torrents/s** in a tight loop. That is about **1 minute of writer time per 1M stored torrents**, and **about 10 minutes at 10M**. It's spread over the batches above, so ingest keeps running. Re-indexing the touched documents follows through `reindex_log` at the live indexer's pace, which this measurement doesn't include.
  - **Nothing is filtered differently until each row is re-derived, and re-derivation alone filters nothing new.** Until a row is re-derived, its stored record has no `cracked` reason. Re-deriving a row judges it only if it becomes adult (`reclassify_row`, unchanged), and this PR changes no adult detection. So stored rows keep the hide, demote and flag marks they had. MW-6 and UC-2 act on a stored torrent only when the engine judges it again, which happens on a rule or pack change (the ordinary re-queue path). On an upgraded store `unauthorised-copy` is off and MW-6 is pending, so filtering is unchanged until the user turns the pack on or accepts MW-6. New torrents are judged at ingest with the new reason, under whatever is on.
- **MW-6 and UC-2 are the same test:** the `cracked` reason on a software release, with a crack word still evidence once neutralized words are blanked (CSX-5 *Neutralize term*). MW-6 sits in malware with its own switch, so turning `unauthorised-copy` off doesn't restore cracked software. SC-1's guard now points at MW-6 for offline cracks. The precision corpus's offline-crack item moved to MW-6's must-match examples.
- **UC-1, tightened as the spec instructs:** a film whose parser source is cam, telesync, telecine or screener, **and** a marker the parser's weak-token rule alone doesn't prove. A strong marker (`HDCAM`, `CAMRip`, `HDTS`, `TELESYNC`, `PDVD`, `HDTC`, `TELECINE`, `SCREENER`, `DVDSCR`, `BDSCR`, `WEBSCR`, `HD CAM`, `HD TS`) counts anywhere; a weak one (`CAM`, `TS`, `TC`, `SCR`) only in brackets or after the year. A name ending `.ts` whose torrent holds a `.ts` file is a transport stream named with its extension (a broadcast recording), so that last `ts` doesn't count; `Movie.2024.TS` and `Movie.2024.720p.TS`, with no such file, still match (runner, #469 review). `Nanny Cam 1080p x264-GRP` and `Little Ts 720p` pass. The cost: a weak marker with no year or brackets (`Title.CAM.x264-GRP`) isn't hidden. The corpus has no such name.
- **UC-3:** film or TV, a disc or web source (Blu-ray, remux, DVD, WEB-DL, WEBRip), and a release group. Exempt: the built-in allow list of public-domain and Creative Commons films (`packs::unauthorised_copy::ALLOW`; a common title needs its exact year, so a commercial film of the same title isn't let through), and **Internet Archive items, recognised by their `<id>_meta.xml` and `<id>_files.xml`**.
  - The filter engine has no per-torrent provenance (which source reported a torrent), so "items from official sources" is read from the torrent itself. Academic lists' datasets aren't film or TV with a group, so UC-3 doesn't reach them.
  - A provenance-based exemption needs a `Facts` field fed by the pipeline; it's a follow-up for the runner (a §2.19 refinement, flagged).
  - Faking the two XML files only gets a torrent past UC-3, a preference pack. The core and the other packs still judge it.
- **New rules are not baseline.** `manifest::ADDED_AFTER_RP0` lists MW-6 and UC-1 to UC-3. Seeding (`seed_record`) never records them, so on a store seeded before them they're pending, in shadow, until accepted (RP-3, RP-6) or until their pack is turned on (which records them, `turned_on`). A fresh store records every rule at its current version, so they apply there. The new `PackRule` variants are declared last, so `PackRule::index` stays the same for the rest. In evaluation order (`PackRule::ALL`) MW-6 sits with malware's hides.
- **Upgraded stores get the explicit off row (step 4 of `ensure_seeded`, `offer_new_packs`).** `packs.defaults` is set once, so a store an RP-DEF build set up never got a row for `unauthorised-copy`.
  - A new settings row, `packs.known`, lists the packs whose defaults are written. With no row, they're RP-DEF's five (`DEFAULTS_PACKS`).
  - Once the defaults are applied, every pack not covered and without a row gets an **off** row, and then `packs.known` is written. It happens once per new pack, so an Undo that later deletes the row isn't undone again. The common start is one settings read.
  - Filtering doesn't change: no row already reads as off. No store change was needed.
- **Precision set additions (runner, #469 review):** a film, an album and a book with crack words (the software kind keeps MW-6 and UC-2 off), the cracked-screen ISO, and `Movie.2024.TS` / `Movie.2024.720p.TS` as UC-1 must-match examples beside the `.ts` recording as a miss.
- **Precision evidence** (`crates/filter/tests/unauthorised_copy.rs`; run with `--nocapture` for the report). Every name in the release-parser golden corpus (317), with files of the kind its hand labels describe, judged against those labels:
  - UC-1 holds 5, all labelled pre-release sources (100%), and misses none;
  - UC-2 and MW-6 hold 3, all labelled cracked (100%), and miss none;
  - UC-3 holds 168, all labelled disc or web with a group, and misses 4 by design (two allow-listed public-domain films, two music releases);
  - none of the 31 lawful names is held by any pack rule: public-domain and Creative Commons films, Internet Archive items, Linux images, lawful patches and updates, weak-source title words, a `.ts` broadcast recording, a cracked-screen ISO, and films, tools and albums with "crack", "cracker" or "keygen" in the title.

  The pack corpora (`data/packs/unauthorised-copy/corpus.txt`, MW-6 in `malware/corpus.txt`) and the precision corpus gained the same cases with made-up names. A precision item that was a scene-style film rip (the scene RAR set) is now a public-domain title, since UC-3 rightly hides the old one. The per-rule golden in `manifest.rs` kept every existing rule's count and hash: the new names are appended after the generated set.
- **UI:** the wizard's pack step names the pack and says it goes by the name, can't know who holds the rights, and that **Always allow** shows what it hides (en, zh). The `PackSwitch` and Always-allow effect warnings name the sixth topic.
  - **The Filters screen (runner, #469: load on demand, don't raise the budget).** Its first view was exactly at its 31 kB budget on `main`; even a short description per pack went over it.
  - The packs list now loads on demand from the switches (`screens/Packs.svelte`), as the review queue already does. A "What each pack hides" disclosure under it loads `screens/PackInfo.svelte`, with every pack's description in en and zh, when opened.
  - The first view went from 31.00 to 30.93 kB.
  - Two new size-limit entries: "Filters packs list (on demand)", 1.3 kB against 1.6 kB, and "Filters pack descriptions (on demand)", 1.43 kB against 1.6 kB.
  - The pack switches render once the list's chunk arrives, as the queue does, which is a few tens of milliseconds on slow 3G.
- **`serve --fixture` (a #454 nit), checked.** The fixture's rows are enriched at build with no filter (`enrich_stored`'s default backfill), and the fixture serves offline with nothing to ingest. So its rows aren't pack-judged when packs are on, and its counts and the screenshots stay as they were.
