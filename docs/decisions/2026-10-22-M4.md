# M4 decisions (#48)

The second M4 PR: the scrape scheduler, search on hot columns, the swarm API and the binary wiring. The M4 plan and the decisions from #32 stay in `docs/architecture.md` §16; these were recorded on #48 and moved here when §16 closed to new entries (#90).

## Scrape scheduler (`pipeline::health`)

- **One round is up to 74 torrents** (one UDP packet).
  - Every tracker gets the round's hashes as one job. A task per tracker owns its client and pacer, and splits the job into requests that tracker accepts.
  - Interest, new and rediscovered torrents also get a DHT scrape (BEP 33). The due lane doesn't, because decay sweeps would swamp the DHT budget. The exception is a swarm only the DHT showed alive: the trackers alone would call it dead.
    - When a round doesn't ask the DHT but some tracker answers, the stored DHT estimate carries over (as one responder, so it doesn't count as fake-seeding evidence). Otherwise every due rescrape would erase it. With no tracker answering, it isn't carried, so an outage can't pass for an answer.
    - At most 8 DHT scrapes wait on the node at once. With all 8 permits taken, a torrent goes without a DHT estimate that round rather than joining a backlog, because the node serves about one scrape a second.
  - A round closes when every answer is in, or after 60 s with what arrived. Its torrents go to the writer as one batch.
- **Pacing:**
  - Rounds start once per tracker interval, with at most 2 in flight. The interval is 2 s at Balanced, 5 s at Low and 1 s at Aggressive; HTTP trackers default to three times that.
  - **A tracker still busy with the previous round sits the new one out** (`skipped`). A bad tracker never holds a round up; it just isn't counted as checked.
  - **Each request of a job must start by the job's limit:** the earlier of
    - one slot more than a full round takes (one request over UDP, three over HTTP at 32 hashes), and
    - the round's deadline minus the tracker's longest request (UDP: a connect and a scrape with their retries; HTTP: its timeout; at most half a round).
  - **Partial jobs:**
    - Requests are counted from the later of now and the pacer's next slot, an interval apart.
    - A tracker that can start some but not all of them in time sends those: a partial job. The rest of the round's hashes go unasked by it.
    - This covers a tracker that raised `min_request_interval`, or a Low intensity.
    - Before review, the fit was all or nothing, counted from a stale slot. A slow tracker then skipped job after job, or answered after its round had closed.
  - **Skips:** a tracker that can't start even one request (backing off, down, banned) skips the job at once.
  - **Gate:** each tracker task publishes when it's free, its pacer's next slot and its interval (`Gate`). The scheduler checks that at least one request fits before sending. A round starts only when some tracker can take it, or, with no trackers, when the DHT is on.
  - **Who was asked, per hash:**
    - A job reports how many of its leading hashes it asked about. A failed request's hashes count as asked; earlier requests' answers stand.
    - The round writes only the torrents some tracker or the DHT was asked about.
    - The rest learned nothing, not even an outage, and go back to their lanes. So an outage of our own making doesn't look like a dead swarm or use up an unreached retry.
  - **Late answers:** answers arriving after their round closed are dropped and counted as `late` (outcome 5), not `ok`. The deadline cap makes them rare.
  - That's about 37 torrents/s at Balanced, roughly 3.2M torrent-scrapes a day across every tracker.
- **Tracker health:**
  - **Backoff:** a failure waits 1 min, doubling (1, 2, 4, 8 min).
  - **Down:** after 5 failures in a row; probed hourly.
  - **Banned:** a refusal using a rate-limit or ban word (rate, limit, banned, blocked, abuse, denied, throttled, …), or the phrases "too many", "too fast" or "slow down", or HTTP 429/403. It lasts 24 h, doubling to a week.
    - Words match whole, so "inaccurate" isn't "rate".
  - **Ok:** any success resets it.
  - `min_request_interval` slows a tracker for good, never speeds it up.
  - Health isn't persisted in M4: a restart starts every tracker as Ok, and a banning tracker costs one request to re-ban.
- **Lanes, by deficit round robin:**
  - **Interest** (detail views, the top 10 hits of a first page) goes first, with up to half of a round while other lanes have work, and fills whatever they leave. A busy search page can't starve "new torrents once" (§15).
  - **Hints don't rescrape fresh data:**
    - An interest hint for a torrent answered in the last 30 minutes is dropped.
    - A rediscovered torrent is dropped if it was answered within the alive minimum (6 h), because popular torrents are rediscovered constantly.
    - Without this, a busy search page or a popular swarm would spend the tracker budget re-asking the same questions.
  - **New** (weight 4), **rediscovered** (2) and **due** (3) share the rest. An empty lane forfeits its deficit, so idle lanes can't save up a burst.
  - A torrent is in at most one round at a time. The interest and rediscovered lanes hold 50,000 entries each and drop their oldest.
  - **Rounds re-check what they load.** A new-lane torrent that already has a swarm row (a hint got there first) and a due-lane torrent that's no longer due are dropped, so the same torrent isn't scraped twice in a row. Dropped torrents don't take a round slot: launch tops the round up from the lanes.
  - The new and due lanes refill from the store, 1,000 at a time, when they fall below four rounds' worth.
  - The new lane follows a watermark. When nothing above it is unscraped, it moves to `max_id()`, so scraped ids aren't walked again.
- **The decay schedule (§15):**
  - **Alive swarms:** 6 h, doubling to 3 days while the counts hold. It resets when seeders or leechers move by a fifth (and at least 2).
  - **Dead swarms:** 2 days, doubling to 30 days.
  - **A round nobody answered:** retried after 1 h, doubling with each unanswered round in a row, to a day. The count lives in `swarm.flags` bits 8–11 (capped at 15). An answered round's flags replace it, and the next interval restarts at that class's minimum. The stored counts are kept: a tracker outage isn't a dead swarm.
  - Every interval has a deterministic ±10% jitter per torrent, so torrents scraped together drift apart.
  - The previous interval is `next_scrape_at − last_scrape_at`.
- **Aggregation:**
  - **Reporting:** trackers answering s+l+c > 0. **Checked:** every tracker that answered at all.
  - **Counts:** the most any reporting tracker gave, because trackers see different slices of a swarm. With no tracker reporting, the DHT's seeds and peers stand in.
  - **Fake seeding:** a tracker claiming at least 1,000 seeders and 50× everything else (other trackers, or a DHT estimate from 2+ nodes) is flagged `SUSPECT`.
    - Its claim is kept per tracker, but it isn't counted as reporting, and the counts come from the other sources.
    - With only the outlier and the DHT, the DHT stands in (`seeders_approx`) rather than a false 0.
    - One tracker alone, or a one-node DHT estimate, is too thin to call fake.
- **Never scraped:** `PRIVATE` and `PRIVATE_SOURCE` torrents, checked again when a round loads them, since the interest and rediscovered lanes bypass the store's lane filters.
- **Write path:**
  - Updates go through a writer input (`WriteBatch.swarm`). The hot columns change after the commit, so SQLite is always ahead of them.
  - The writer also sets last-seen on every rediscovery, and hands the torrent to the rediscovered lane.
  - At shutdown the scheduler stops first. Rounds in flight are dropped; their torrents keep their old schedule.
- **Hot columns across restarts:** `open_hot` loads the snapshot when the stamp in `settings` matches, and clears the stamp at once. `save_hot` writes a new snapshot and stamp after the pipeline stops. A crash therefore always rebuilds from SQLite.
- **The `Flags` hot column** carries the suspect bit (bit 0) for the ranking penalty. User flags (§7) will take bits 8 and up. It costs 4 B per torrent, so hot columns are 24 B per torrent (about 240 MB at 10M).
- **Rollups:**
  - `SCRAPE_REQUESTS` (5): jobs per tracker by outcome (ok, timeout, banned, error, skipped, late).
  - `SCRAPED` (6): torrents by lane and result (alive, dead, unreached).
  - `DHT_SCRAPES` (7).
  - `Rollups::add_dim` takes a dimension that isn't a source.
- **Tests:**
  - property tests for the schedule bounds and for lane fairness and completeness;
  - unit tests for the pacer states, ban detection and aggregation (including fake seeding);
  - end to end with the fake UDP tracker: counts reach the store and hot columns, private torrents are never scraped, a rediscovery moves last-seen, a fresh hint doesn't rescrape, an unreachable tracker leaves counts unknown, a due rescrape keeps the DHT estimate it didn't ask for, and snapshots survive a clean restart but not a crash;
  - end to end with the fake HTTP tracker: back-to-back rounds of four requests each are all answered, none skipped; a tracker that raises `min_request_interval` to a second answers partial jobs until every torrent is scraped, with none late or unreached (it fails with all-or-nothing jobs);
  - end to end: a banning tracker stops rounds from starting, so its torrents' unreached count stays at 1; shutdown with a round in flight returns at once and keeps the old schedule;
  - unit tests for who gets written (`settle`), round expiry, request fitting, the interest cap and the suspect fallback;
  - store tests for the unreached count: it climbs, caps at 15, keeps other flags and resets on an answer.
  - All stable over repeated runs.

## API, binary and Serve rollups

- **Config:**
  - `[scrape]` holds `enabled`, `trackers` (ten long-lived public UDP trackers by default), `dht`, `interval_secs` and `http_interval_secs`. `LODESTAR_SCRAPE_ENABLED` overrides `enabled`.
  - Tracker URLs are canonicalised. The same tracker listed twice counts once, and one that can't be scraped (`wss://`, an HTTP URL without a BEP 48 scrape URL) is a startup error. Errors name the list entry by position, never the URL, which can carry a passkey.
  - Scrapes bind the DHT's IPv4 address when it's specific, so they leave through the same interface or VPN (§5).
  - **Subscribed IP blocklists (§20.13) apply to trackers too.** This uses the same `SharedIps` check as the DHT and the fetch pool.
    - A UDP tracker uses the first allowed address its name resolves to. Its address is checked again before every request, so a list update applies at once.
    - HTTP trackers resolve through a filtering resolver, and a literal-IP URL or redirect target is checked directly.
    - A tracker with no allowed address fails with `Denied` and backs off like any failing tracker. It's never contacted.
  - `[ranking]` sets every `RankWeights` field plus the suspect multiplier.
- **Search API:**
  - Sorts gain `seeders`, `leechers`, `trackers` and `seen`.
  - `tz` is minutes east of UTC (±14 h), for `time:`.
  - `explain=1` fills B5c's `explain` block, with M4's priors (below, "Merged with B5c").
  - **Field names follow the UI contract** (`docs/metrics.md` §7), as flat fields of the torrent summary, not a nested object. The contract was merged while this PR was in review.
  - Every hit and the detail view carry `seeders`, `leechers`, `completed`, `trackers_reporting`, `trackers_checked` and `last_scrape_at`, which are `null` together when the torrent was never scraped (§10.12: null ≠ zero). They also carry `seeders_approx`, `swarm_suspect` and `last_seen_at`.
    - They're read from the stored swarm row: one primary-key read per hit. The hot columns don't hold `completed`, `trackers_checked` or the scrape time, and the row is never behind them.
    - `last_seen_at` is the later of the row and the hot column, which moves on every sighting.
  - **`seeders_approx`** (signed off for M4) is true when no tracker reported and the DHT estimate stood in for the counts. The "freshness window" for the max is the last answered round. The DHT is never part of the max: it only stands in.
- **The detail view** adds:
  - `dht_seeds` and `dht_peers`;
  - `swarm_trackers`, the per-tracker rows with URLs and the time of each answer (`at`);
  - `swarm_history`, daily and weekly samples. Each sample's `at` is the start of its UTC day or week, and gaps are `null`;
  - `swarm_trend`, the 7-day trend.

  A first scrape nobody answered reads as never scraped.

- **Interest and Serve counters:** the API reports through an `ApiEvents` hook the binary implements, so the API crate doesn't depend on the pipeline.
  - A detail view, and the top 10 hits of a first page, go to the interest lane.
  - Each search adds to `SEARCHES` (8: with results, none, failed) and `SEARCH_LATENCY` (9: log₂ millisecond buckets) in the rollups.
- **Status and metrics:**
  - `/api/status` gains `swarm`: counts, and per-tracker health with outcomes and last answer. The status page shows it.
  - Prometheus gains `scrape_*`, `tracker_rounds_total`, `tracker_health`, `hot_columns_*` and the DHT's `dht_scrapes_*`.
  - Trackers are labelled by id in metrics: `/metrics` is open, and an HTTP tracker URL can carry a passkey.
- **Operations:** `docs/operations.md` starts with swarm health: what scraping reveals, the config, the schedule and its throughput, tracker states, fake seeding, routing and hot-column recovery.
- **The binary test** runs scraping against a local tracker nothing listens on, never the public list. It checks the metrics, the status key and the hot snapshot written at shutdown.

## Search on hot columns

- **Query language.** `seeders:`, `leechers:`, `trackers:` and `dht:` are count ranges with the usual `>`, `>=`, `<`, `..` and `=` forms. `seen:` is a date field like `discovered:` (`seen:<24h`, `seen:2026-01-01..2026-03-31`). The renderer and compiler now follow the date field instead of assuming `discovered`.
- **`time:` is a new node, `TimeOfDay { from, to }`**, in minutes after midnight, both inclusive.
  - Accepted forms: `22:00..02:00` (wraps past midnight), `9..17` (whole hours, 09:00 to 17:59) and `22:30` (that minute). The canonical text is always `HH:MM..HH:MM`.
  - **The AST has no time zone,** so a saved search means the same in any zone. `SearchIndex::query_at(node, now, utc_offset)` applies the user's offset, and `query(node, now)` is UTC. A fixed offset is approximate across a DST change; the API passes the client's current one.
  - It reads the existing `discovered` fast field, so the index is unchanged.
- **Hot columns in search:**
  - `SearchIndex::with_hot(Arc<HotColumns>)` attaches them. **Without them, swarm clauses match nothing, swarm sorts treat every torrent as never scraped, and ranking stays text-only.** Results are then identical to M3's.
  - **They're never in the index.** A check reads the document's `id` fast column, then the atomic value. Doc order inside a segment isn't id order after merges (§16, M2), so nothing assumes it.
  - **"Never scraped" matches no range** (`seeders:<5` leaves it out). A negation keeps it: `-seeders:0` is "not known to be dead".
- **Where checks run:**
  - **At the top level,** hot-column, `seen:` and `time:` clauses are always collector checks, and **never drive the query.** A filter-only query becomes everything, checked: one id-column read and one atomic load per document.
  - **Negated ones are lifted too,** as a negated check (`-seeders:0`). As a `MustNot` clause they scanned whole segments for what to exclude, which put "word + swarm filter" at 5.4 ms p95 at 200k (about 270 ms at 10M). Checked on the candidates alone, it's 2.0 ms.
  - **Under `|` or `-`,** a small constant-score `CheckQuery` runs the same check over each segment into a bitset, like the file clause's `IdsQuery`.
  - A test checks that both forms select the same torrents.
- **Sorts:** `seeders`, `leechers`, `trackers` and `seen`, ascending or descending.
  - Never-scraped torrents (and never-seen ones, for `seen`) sort after every scraped one, in both directions, like a missing fast value.
  - **The browse cache keys on the hot columns' epoch** whenever the query or the order reads them. Cached tops for hot data therefore last until the epoch moves, at most a minute after the scraper writes (`EPOCH_EVERY`), while the segment cache keeps working for everything else.
  - `time:` adds the zone offset to the key.
- **The re-ranker (§8 phase 2)** runs on the 200-candidate window whenever hot columns are attached, with or without name boosts. The text score (phase 1 plus boosts) is multiplied by:
  - **seeders:** `1 + 0.2 · ln(1 + s)`;
  - **freshness:** `0.75 + 0.25 · ½^(days since last seen / 30)`;
  - **trackers:** `1 + 0.1 · min(t, 5) / 5`;
  - **junk:** `1 − 0.5 · junk / 100`, from the stored M3 score;
  - **a penalty hook:** `SearchIndex::with_penalty(Fn(id) -> 0..=1)`, for the scraper's swarm-consistency check (slice 4). The default is none.
- **Neutral priors for unknowns.** A never-scraped torrent counts as 1 seeder and 1 tracker: above a dead swarm and below a live one, so new torrents aren't buried before their first scrape. An unknown last-seen counts as one half-life old.
- **`RankWeights`** holds every weight, with the defaults above. `with_weights` sets it from the `[ranking]` config section, which also sets the suspect multiplier (default 0.2).
- **Only the window is re-ranked.** Hits past the 200 candidates keep phase-1 order. When a page reaches past the window, its tail is scored for its parts, then put back in phase-1 order. Re-ranking the tail as well would let a hit move between pages as the window shifted, so it could be shown twice or not at all.
- **Explain:** the re-ranker keeps `ScoreParts` per relevance hit (text, each multiplier and the final score) on the page. Sorted pages have no parts. The API shows them through B5c's `explain` block (below).
- **The window path costs little.** Text-only relevance without boosts (phrases, fielded words) now also takes the 200-candidate window when hot columns are attached. That's one id read, one junk read and five atomic loads per candidate.
  - A filter-only relevance query ranks an arbitrary 200 of its matches by the priors. Browsing by swarm health uses the seeders sort, not relevance.
- **Bench:**
  - `synth::swarm(seed, id)` and `synth::last_seen(seed, id)` are stateless SplitMix hashes of `(seed, id)`, so the generator's stream and the size report are unchanged.
  - The swarm mix: 30% never scraped, 40% dead, and Pareto live swarms (α 1.1, median about 2 seeders, a tail to 100k) with 1–20 trackers and a DHT estimate capped at BEP 33's ~7,700.
  - `search-bench` fills hot columns for the stored corpus (`--no-hot` skips them) and adds five classes:
    - **word + swarm filter:** gated at 30 ms;
    - **word by seeders:** 30 ms;
    - **`trackers:>=3` browse:** 50 ms;
    - **browse by seeders (cached):** 50 ms;
    - **by seeders (cold):** not gated.

- **On B5a's searcher leases.** `/search` pages through a `Lease`, so M4's search lives there too:
  - The compiled query carries the hot columns, so counts, cursors and post-filters check swarm clauses like the index does.
  - Hot sorts are a field order over the hot column. Cursors keep the value and the address.
  - Relevance runs two-phase whenever there are boosts or hot columns. Each window is re-ranked with the priors, and its score parts go back as `explain`.
  - `tz` reaches the lease's compile.
  - **Swarm values aren't pinned by a lease.** A torrent scraped between two pages can move across the cursor: one page late or early, never lost from the index.
  - **Every served index gets the same `Ranking`** (hot columns, weights, penalty) through `ServingConfig`, including one a rebuild swaps in.
- **Measured at 10M (the M4 exit, §14).** Setup:
  - 10M synthetic torrents in 8 index segments;
  - 4 cores and 15 GB of RAM, against about 18 GB of index and store;
  - 300 queries per class, top 50, rows loaded from SQLite.

  **The first run, straight after indexing, read cold from disk.** One common word came in at 464 ms p95 and two words at 168 ms, while their p50s were 3.5 and 6.9 ms. The re-run on the same corpus, with a warm page cache, is the table below.

  The run found two M4 paths over budget, both fixed:
  - **An OR of swarm clauses** (`word (seeders:>=5 | trackers:>=3)`) went from 363 ms to 26.6 ms p95.
    - It ran as `CheckQuery`, which reads every document of every segment.
    - An OR or AND made only of hot, `seen:` and `time:` clauses is now one check (`FastRange::Any`/`All`). It's lifted like a plain swarm filter and runs on the word's candidates.
  - **Cold hot-column sorts** went from 81 ms to 61 ms p95.
    - Segments were one per thread, and merged segments are uneven. Cold top-k now splits segments into 512k-document pieces that run in parallel (70 ms).
    - The values are also read 64 documents at a time, so the reads overlap (61 ms).

  - **Every swarm filter and sort meets §3 on the cached path that §3 defines.** The hot epoch keys the cache, so a given page goes cold at most once a minute while the scraper writes.
  - **Cold hot sorts miss 50 ms by about a fifth.**
    - The cost is the same full scan that a cold browse by a plain field pays (54 ms): a cold miss must count every match exactly, which only a scan can do. The hot reads add about 6 ms.
    - Getting under 50 ms cold is therefore a general scan-speed question (or an approximate total, `total_approx`, planned in B5a), not an M4 one. That decision went to the runner with this table.
  - **Not M4's:** word + file clause runs 413 ms p95 at 10M even when warm (p50 86 ms). That's M3's file index, and it was reported separately.

- **Soak (`scrape-soak`, 10 min on the 10M store):**
  - 3 fake UDP trackers and 1 fake HTTP tracker, at a 10 ms interval;
  - 3,000 rounds, 222,000 torrents scraped and written;
  - resident memory at 460.5 MiB throughout, at most 460.7 (+0.0% over the second half; the gate is 5%).
  - The rate (370 torrents/s) is set by the fake trackers' pacing, not by the scheduler.
- **Fuzzing:** the `query` target ran 1.3M runs clean, seeded with the new syntax.
- **Merged with B17 (Adult visibility) and B6 (facets):**
  - M4's hot sorts, swarm filters and re-ranked relevance run the query B17 restricts (`BaseQuery::query()`), so a hidden Adult torrent never surfaces through `sort=-seeders` or `seeders:>N`. A test covers both states.
  - `/facets` takes `tz` as `/search` does, and the lease keeps its offset, so facets compile `time:` in the same zone as the results they count.
  - B6's swarm buckets (`buckets.seeders/trackers`) stay null, listed in `unavailable`. They count hot columns, not index fields, and are left for a follow-up slice rather than this merge.
- **Merged with `main` at `76528ef`** (B5c, B7–B12, B16, B-log1, B-update and others):
  - **One explain shape, B5c's.** M4's per-hit `SearchHit.explain` and its second `explain` request field are gone. B5c's top-level block gets M4's numbers in its `priors` slots (seeders, freshness, trackers, junk, and the suspect penalty as `swarm_consistency`), from the same page the search serves. So `score = (phase1 + phase2.name + phase2.title) × every prior`, a prior the ranking doesn't apply being null. `two_phase` is true whenever phase 2 ran, which with hot columns is every relevance search. B5c's explain treated hot sorts as relevance (it asked whether the sort had an index field); they're field sorts there too, and `ranked_by` names them. Tests: `explain.rs` (`with_hot_columns_the_priors_scale_the_parts_to_the_score`) and `tests/api.rs`.
  - **The result cache keys what hot columns and zones change.** Swarm health changes without a commit, so a generation-only key would serve stale hot sorts, swarm filters and priors. A cached result that reads the hot columns (a swarm clause, a hot sort, or relevance on an index with priors) also carries the columns' epoch, which moves at most once a minute while they change, as the browse cache's tops do. Facet counts carry it only for swarm clauses, since order doesn't change a count. A query with `time:` carries its UTC offset, and the cache takes its leases with `Lease::new_at`, so `tz` reaches cached searches and facets. Skipping the cache for hot queries was the alternative; with priors on every relevance search, that would have turned the cache off for most searches. Tests: `cache/tests.rs` (`time_of_day_is_keyed_by_the_zone`, `results_that_read_hot_columns_last_until_their_epoch_moves`).
  - **Swarm fields everywhere a torrent is listed.** Compare, Related, discover, user items, collections, alerts and `/export` rows all read the swarm summary as `/search` does, so none shows a scraped torrent as never scraped. A test exports a scraped and a never-scraped torrent.
  - **Percolation reads the hot columns.** A saved search with a swarm clause runs it as search does. A torrent percolated right after indexing usually hasn't been scraped, so such a search alerts only on torrents scraped by then. Matching later scrapes is a follow-up.
  - **Logging (B-log1).** The scrape scheduler's four lines are catalogued events in a new `lodestar-pipeline` catalogue: `pipeline.scrape_failed` (Debug, off by default), `pipeline.http_trackers_disabled`, `pipeline.tracker_skipped` and `pipeline.scheduler_store_failed`. Trackers are named by their store id and errors by their kind, never by URL (a passkey) or message (a tracker's own text). `services.rs` stays on B-log1b's list.
  - **Fixture mode (B16)** doesn't start the scraper: a fixture never asks a tracker, and its swarm rows are the manifest's.
  - **Rollups (B11b)** name M4's codes 5–9 in the aggregation table; all five are sums. No migration: B8 took V10 and B11b V11, and M4 needs none.
  - **Suggestions** offer the swarm fields, from the parser's field table.

  - **The cold hot-column sorts at 10M are accepted as they are.** By seeders and by trackers measured 60.8 and 59.5 ms p95 cold, over the 50 ms gate the review asked for. §3's targets (cached browse under 50 ms, keyword under 30 ms) are met, so the runner accepted them. The 200k CI gate on the cold sorts stays, and a faster cold path is a carry-forward.
  - **`time:` in percolation matches in UTC.** A saved search's `time:` clause runs in UTC when new torrents are matched, while `/search` applies the request's `tz`. A saved search doesn't store a zone yet; that's a follow-up.
- **The late-answer path is tested** end to end (`an_answer_after_its_round_closed_is_late_and_writes_nothing`): it's counted as `late`, the tracker's `ok` count goes back down, and nothing from it reaches the swarm row or the hot columns.
- **An unreadable hot-column snapshot is rebuilt from SQLite,** like a missing or corrupt one, and logs `pipeline.hot_snapshot_unreadable`; it never stops startup.
- **`[ranking]` refuses `nan` and `inf`.** TOML accepts them, and clamping passes NaN through, which would make every relevance score NaN.
- **A best-practice self-review, at the owner's request (UX and performance fixes accepted):**
  - **A new torrent is seen when it's stored.** Its hot last-seen was `NEVER` until its first rediscovery, so `seen:`, the seen sort and the freshness prior treated the newest torrents as the stalest, and a clean restart kept it so. The writer now touches it with `discovered_at`, as `torrents.last_seen_at` holds.
  - **A segment's match set is built once per search.** Sorted searches ask for a scorer per 512k-document piece, and the column-check and file-id clauses each rescanned the whole segment into a bitset per scorer (20 times on a 10M-document segment). Each weight now keeps its per-segment sets (`filter::SegmentSets`) for the life of the search; pieces copy the set instead.
  - **Rounds the scheduler skips count on the tracker's status.** A tracker that's backing off, down or busy showed 0 skipped on the status page and in `tracker_rounds_total` while it sat rounds out; only the rollups had them.
  - **Every failed search counts in the Serve rollups,** not only one whose page failed (the cache lookup, the lease and the exact count too). A bad or expired cursor isn't counted as a failed search.
  - **`hot_columns_ids` and `hot_columns_bytes` are exported with scraping off too:** the columns are held either way. `scrape_fresh_skipped_total`'s help names everything it counts.
  - **Tidier ranking API:** the duplicate `with_rank_weights` is gone, and `page()` uses `priors()`.
