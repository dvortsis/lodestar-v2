# B-update decisions, part 4 of 5: the engine and `/updates`

Part 4 makes the update engine check, decide and report (architecture §12.2). It's built on part 3's pieces (`2026-10-23-B-update-r3.md`): the window, the policy, the trust file and the check's fetch. Installing comes in part 5: until then `can_install` is `false`, with `not_built_yet` wherever nothing else stops it. The code is in `crates/lodestar/src/update/{engine,unavailable}.rs` and `crates/api/src/{updates.rs,dto/updates.rs}`.

## When the engine checks
- **On a schedule:** with `mode` notify, auto or image, every 6 hours plus a random minute within the hour. `<data>/bin/updater.json` keeps the last and next check, the pause and the last error, so a restart doesn't check again at once.
- **By hand:** `POST /updates/check` works at any mode, `off` included. It runs at most once a minute (`429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`) and one at a time.
- **Then:** `lodestar_update::check` against `trust.json`, the forward-only write of the next trust state, and the policy over what it found.

## The trust floor in the engine
- **Every check reads every installed overlay's mark.** That covers the statements and the high-water mark, whether or not `trust.json` exists, as part 3's `trust_file::load` expects. Part 3's runner review asked for this, so a stale or damaged file can't drop an overlay's revocation or mark.
- **A mark comes only from a fully verified overlay** (`overlay::verify`): the manifest's signature against the embedded keys, then the binary's SHA-256 and size. A planted directory under `<data>/bin` therefore can't raise a mark and freeze updates (runner, the 4/5 note on #149).
- **The cost:** verifying means a sealed copy and a hash of each installed overlay's binary, once per check (every 6 hours, or by hand at most once a minute). It's paid only while overlays exist, and it's what the supervisor already does before running one.

## Where there's no engine
- **Fixture mode:** no engine and no request. `/updates` reports `unavailable: "fixture"`, and every write is `409 updates_unavailable`.
- **Windows (runner decision; E-M6 owns check, notify and install on Windows):** `/updates` answers `unavailable: "windows_helper"`, with `capability.reason` the same. Checking on Windows needs Windows artifacts in the signed manifest and E-M6's helper, and neither exists yet. The settings section and the window parsing are cross-platform.
  - **What E-M6 reuses:** `update::window`, `update::policy`, `update::check` (the source, the hop allowlist and the request), `trust_file`, `lodestar_update::check`, the DTOs and `UpdatesApi`. The Windows-specific parts are the helper, the install, and the module gates (`#[cfg(unix)]` on `engine`, `check`, `policy`, `trust_file`).
- **`unavailable` is an enum** (`UpdatesUnavailable`: `fixture`, `network`, `windows_helper`), with the OpenAPI example `windows_helper`. `network` means the update client couldn't be built.

## `/updates`
- **Routes:**
  - `GET /updates`;
  - `POST /updates/check`;
  - `POST /updates/skip {version}`, which writes `skipped` with reason `user`, or `404 unknown_version`;
  - `POST /updates/pause {days}`, where `days` is 0, 1, 7 or 30, else `422 invalid_value`;
  - **`POST /updates/history/clear` (§2.19, a new route).**
- **Access:** every route needs a session, never an API key, and the writes are same-origin JSON. No route needs the fresh password: nothing installs that isn't signed.
- **Install and Roll back** come with part 5. `rollback.available` is `false` until then, though `to_version` shows where it would go.
- **`UpdatesApi`** is an `Extension<Arc<dyn UpdatesApi>>` beside `SystemApi`. Without it every route is `503 updates_unavailable`.
- **Release notes** are signed text. The DTO says to render them as plain text or sanitised Markdown, never as HTML.

## The check's error codes
- **A URL the client refuses on the first hop is `no_release_source`,** not `redirect_refused`: that URL is ours, built from `CARGO_PKG_REPOSITORY`. After the first hop it's still `redirect_refused` (runner, cosmetic note on #149).

## The `updates` SSE topic
- **`Topic::Updates` is the 7th of 8 bits,** last, so the existing bits keep their values. The `u8` set stays.
- **`UpdatesEvent {state, version, bytes_done, bytes_total, error}`** carries the brief's states. This part publishes `checking`, `available` and `failed`; the rest come with installing.

## `/system`
- **The Updates row** is `on` unless `mode` is `off`, with `last_checked_at` from `updater.json`. It's null where there's no engine: a fixture, or Windows.

## Logging
- **Two catalogue events:** `update.available` (no fields) and `update.check_failed` (`error`, a stable code).
- **No version, URL or signature text** reaches a log line, because the fields are static labels. A test captures a failed check's log and looks for the URL, the host and a query secret.

## Tests: what 4/5 carries and what 5/5 does
- **4/5, the engine against an in-memory release source:**
  - a good check that advances trust;
  - a replay (older than the mark) and a foreign key, neither of which moves anything;
  - a bad signature;
  - a downgrade, which offers nothing;
  - an expired manifest (`manifest_lapsed`, every release `not_current`);
  - a damaged trust file, which is never overwritten;
  - nothing published;
  - the rate limit;
  - skip, pause and clear;
  - the schedule following the mode;
  - the log capture;
  - the Windows `unavailable` answer.
- **4/5, routes:** through the router, with goldens.
- **5/5:**
  - the binary-level fake-source verify cases that need a download: wrong hash or size, and an artifact for another target;
  - "trust survives a rollback";
  - the install, switch and rollback events.
