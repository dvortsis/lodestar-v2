# F-near decisions (#85)

NEAR words of several tokens in filter rules (architecture §20.2, §20.4, §20.8).

- **The bug:** the filter compiled each NEAR word to every token text the tokenizer gave it, joined. A word that splits gives its whole form and its parts (`1080p` → `1080p`, `1080`, `p`), so it became `1080p1080p`, a token no torrent has, and the cheap check and rule index looked for that too. Such a rule never matched. The reference matcher had the same join, so the property test comparing them couldn't see it.
- **A NEAR word now matches its parts at consecutive positions (a phrase), or one token of a compound or whole form that covers all its parts** (`webdl` for `web-dl`, so a run-together name still matches). A form covering only some parts doesn't stand for the word. A word with no parts (`++`) matches nothing, as before.
  - **A NEAR word and a plain word now differ there:** the plain word `web-dl.x264` matches "Show WEBDL" (a word matches any one of its compound forms), while `web-dl.x264 NEAR/1 show` doesn't. The plain word's over-match is a follow-up, not changed here.
- **Distance runs between the nearest ends of two words' places.** A place of several parts takes up their span; a single token, whole or compound forms included, takes its own position. The window is unchanged: fewer than `words + slop` positions apart, measured from a place of the first word, in any order. So `1080p NEAR/1 x` allows one token between `p` and `x`, as `a NEAR/1 x` allows one between `a` and `x`.
  - **Why not the first token:** a word's own length would then eat into the distance on one side only. The search query language already counts this way: its NEAR runs positions on across the words (`compile.rs`), so a phrase query's offsets skip a word's parts and slop counts only the tokens between. For words in order, both now agree; the filter's NEAR stays unordered, as it was.
  - **A document's compound token sits at its first part's position**, as in search. So `webdl NEAR/1 x264` doesn't match `WEB-DL.a.x264` (`webdl` at 0, `x264` at 3), but `web-dl NEAR/1 x264` does (`web dl` ends at 1).
  - **Overlap counts as 0**, so a word inside another's span (`264` in `x264`) or the same word twice may be the same place, as a repeated single word already was.
- **Hostile names (§6):** a name from the DHT can repeat a word hundreds of times. NEAR looks for the other words only in the window around each place of the first, found by binary search on position (texts whose compounds come after their later parts are sorted first), after at most 8 plain scans per text, which is the cheaper way for an ordinary name. Cost grows with the text, not its square. `1080p NEAR/1 x264` on a 507-token name took 2.15 ms before this change and takes 25–31 µs; on 2,019 tokens, 120 ms before and 89–99 µs (`near_hostile`, release, build VM, tokenizing included). `hostile_names_stay_cheap` holds 2,000-token names under 0.2 s in a debug build.
- **Narrowing and the cheap check** treat each NEAR word as a word: a covering form, or all its parts able to follow each other. The rule index keys a NEAR on its rarest word's keys, as for an AND.
- **Rules affected:**
  - **Core (CS-\*) and pack rules** aren't queries, so none uses NEAR; they're unchanged, and their fixtures pass.
  - **Your rules** with such words now hide, flag or drop what they always should have.
  - **Queries that release or allow now match more, which protects less:** CSX-5 override entries given as queries and their scopes (which bypass the non-floor core), the core's exceptions, _Always allow_, and rules' `unless` clauses. A stored entry with a split NEAR word was previewed while it matched nothing and now releases items without a new preview. Whether stored entries need a re-preview on upgrade is with the owner.
- **Cost** (`near_cost`, release, build VM; 10,000 rules over a 512-torrent video set, the rest unique keywords; before and after measured alternately): single-token NEAR at 10% of rules 417–555 µs per torrent before, 410–525 after; at 1%, 97–112 and 115–138. Multi-token NEAR cost 30 µs before at any share, because it was rejected without matching. Run through the first version of this change it cost 1.0–1.2 ms at 10%, and through the windowed search it costs 0.81–0.83 ms (149–215 µs at 1%), about 4.5 µs per full match against about 1 µs for single-token words. NEAR shapes are outside the held budget (§20.8, "Scope"); the `budget` gate, which has no NEAR rules, is unchanged within the VM's noise.
- **Follow-ups:** prefixes and fuzzy words compile through the same joining step (`x26*` becomes `x26x26`, so it can't match split words), and NEAR doesn't apply CSX-5 _Neutralize term_ as words and phrases do.
