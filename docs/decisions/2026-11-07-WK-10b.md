# WK-10b decisions (the worker runtime core)

The second of WK-10's three PRs (runner, 2026-11-07T19:01Z; see `2026-11-07-WK-10a.md`). This one is the worker's runtime behind `session::Work`: snapshots, the judge, claims and outcomes, the fetch pool and prepare stage with the worker's caps, sealing, results, drain and stop. It also covers the coordinator's midnight pre-send that #592's review asked WK-10b for. WK-10c follows with the worker's sharded DHT, budget and intensity, heartbeat stats, the floor-drop tombstones and the measured RSS breakdown.

## The runtime (`crates/lodestar/src/worker/runtime.rs`)
- **`lodestar worker` runs it** instead of WK-2's idle runtime. Its fetcher is `PeerWireFetcher` with `max_metadata` 4 MiB − 32 KiB (`MAX_METADATA`), so the largest dict's bound fits the pool's 4 MiB byte budget (`HELD_BYTES`, X-fetch-pool-bytes and X-fetch-meta-reserve). It opens 200 sockets at most (`MAX_SOCKETS`, X-fetch-meta-reserve's proposal). WK-10c revisits both numbers with the measured RSS. Until WK-10c's DHT lands, the worker fetches only what it's granted: re-grants, and what it claims through `Finds`.
- **Judge first (WK-8).** Nothing is fetched until a filter snapshot is installed, an `Assign` with fetching is applied, and the dictionary it names is in. A worker that restarted has no wire key until a fresh snapshot arrives, so it can't seal. Grants that come before then wait in the queue into the pool.
- **Snapshots** are assembled in the session's message loop, in order; each chunk is acknowledged. They're decoded and installed on a blocking thread (`spawn_blocking`), never on the async threads (WK-8: up to about 96 MiB at the caps). Installs are numbered, so a slow decode never replaces a newer snapshot.
- **The dictionary is fixed when the stages start.** A later dictionary takes effect at the worker's next start (runner, 2026-11-07T20:34Z). The coordinator keeps old dictionaries, so the blobs still open.
- **`Assign`:** a revision is applied only if it's newer than the one in use (WK-5). `pool_cap` sizes the pool's `max_pending`. The shard, the budget, the intensity and the politeness are WK-10c's.
- **Claims:** keys found here pass the have-filter (below) and are batched with `next_claims` (≤ 512 or 250 ms). Their peers pass `network_policy::allows` before they're sent as hints or dialled. Each grant puts its token into the worker's leases and the key, with its peers, into the pool. A claim that fails is dropped, because the DHT finds the key again.
- **Re-grants and hints** go into the pool too. The session passes on only hints whose token is the lease it holds. The runtime keeps only peers the network policy allows (§18.7: in memory only).
- **Losses** go out as `Outcome::Lost` with the funnel's loss code, through one queue that's never dropped from. Its length is bounded by the leases held, which `pool_cap` bounds (WK-7). They're reported in batches of up to `MAX_OUTCOME_BATCH`, each kept until a session takes it.
- **Results:** sealed records are gathered into `Results` batches of up to `MAX_OPAQUE`, encoded with `encode_with(&[], &sealed)` so nothing goes in clear (WK-9c), and numbered from 1.
  - **Kept until accepted.** A batch stays until the coordinator accepts it. A refusal ("send it again later") retries on the next 5 s tick. A new session sends everything not yet accepted, because batch ids are idempotent (§19.2).
  - **Committed on acceptance.** An accepted batch's leases go to `committed`.
  - **Capped.** A batch's records keep their bytes in the outbound queue's 8 MiB budget until they're accepted, so a link that never accepts pauses fetching instead of growing memory (WK-7).
- **One outbound queue for the runtime's life** (WK-7's note): the session task owns its receiving end across reconnects. WK-11's spool takes over from it.
- **Only `SealedRecord`s reach the outbound queue,** because the queue's type is `ByteSender<SealedRecord>`.

## Sealing across midnight (runner, #592's review B2)
- **"Not ready" is never `Blocked`.** WK-8's `Judging` reported a result it couldn't seal yet (no snapshot, or past its wire key's day) as `Loss::Blocked`. That would throw away every worker's fetched work at each UTC midnight. Once WK-10c tombstones `Blocked` keys, it would also tombstone legitimate keys for good.
  - **The runtime's own `Sealing`** wraps the outbound queue instead. A result that can't be sealed yet waits for the next install, or a minute's tick of the clock, for up to `SEAL_WAIT` (10 minutes, the lease TTL).
  - **The prepare stage waits with it.** That pauses the pool, as a stalled link does, so nothing builds up beyond the existing caps.
  - **Past the wait, the result is released unreported.** Its lease runs out on the coordinator and the key is granted again. The same applies if the day turns between the check and the judge.
  - **`Blocked` is only ever the floor's:** CS-8a and hash-list drops.
  - `Judging` itself is unchanged; only the worker stopped using it.
- **The coordinator pre-sends the next day's key** (`serve.rs`, approved for WK-10b). From 23:00Z (`PRESEND_FROM`), the snapshot is built for the next day's wire key, and the open keys add that day's. Workers then seal at 00:00Z with no gap. Building at 23:00Z doesn't cause a second build at midnight, because the due check compares days by the same key time. Yesterday's key stays open through the window, so a 24 h spool still opens.
- **The worker side of WK-9c's question is settled:**
  - a worker seals only while its clock is on or before the installed key's day;
  - the pre-sent key covers the midnight crossing;
  - a late snapshot is waited for, not dropped.

## The have-filter in front of claims (runner, 2026-11-07T20:34Z, Q6; flagged to the owner under §2.19)
- **Before a filter is complete** (no filter, mid-assembly, or the in-place window above 64 MiB), every sighting is claimed (WK-3's "maybe").
- **Once one is complete:**
  - a "not stored" is claimed;
  - a "maybe stored" is dropped and counted (`Counts::skipped`, the worker's `rediscovered`);
  - except that one "maybe" sighting in `REDISCOVERY_SAMPLE` (16) is claimed all the same.

  The sampled sighting is picked by a per-sighting random draw (SplitMix64, seeded at start; tests fix the seed), never by the key. So a false-positive new key (~1 %, ~2 % above 30M) is claimed on a later sighting, typically within about 16, instead of being lost for good. The coordinator answers those claims from the store and sees only about 1/16 of the rediscovery claims. A rebuild re-keys the filter, which shuffles its false positives too.
- **The over-count:** `rediscovered` on a worker includes its false positives, about 1–2 %.

## Floor drops (runner, 2026-11-07T20:34Z)
- WK-10b reports CS-8a and hash-list drops as `Lost { reason: Blocked }` and writes nothing to the store.
- **The coordinator-side tombstone is WK-10c's,** an approved edit to `crates/pipeline/src/remote.rs` `RemoteSink::outcomes`, under these conditions:
  - its queue is bounded and never blocks;
  - nothing on the sink's path blocks;
  - no blocked key is stored or re-granted after its tombstone;
  - a test covers the coordinator refusing a re-claim of that key.
- **Until WK-10c lands, a blocked key can be re-granted** on a later sighting and dropped again by the next worker. That's acceptable only because workers aren't released before WK-G.

## Drain and stop
- **A drain** (`Work::drain`) takes no new keys, asks the pool to stop, and waits for the pool and the prepare stage up to `DRAIN_DEADLINE` (10 s; the pool's own drain is 5 s, X-fetch-meta-reserve). It then returns the losses not yet reported, for the session to report before `Drained`. Results the session task already sent are committed by the coordinator. Everything else goes back with `Drained` and is granted again.
- **A stop with a stalled session finishes within its deadline** (the brief's gate). If the pool or the prepare stage hasn't handed over by the deadline, because the outbound queue is full and no session takes it, or a fetch never returns, both are aborted. Their keys' leases go back to the coordinator when they end.
- **The drain blocks the session's message loop while it runs** (WK-2's note), so the drain doesn't wait for `Accepted`. It sends and returns.

## Tests
- **End to end** (`a_worker_stores_torrents_end_to_end`): a worker against an in-process coordinator, made of `serve`'s cluster port and tick on a real pipeline over TCP and mutual TLS.
  - It gets its assignment and the snapshots, and claims five finds.
  - It fetches (a stub fetcher), prepares, judges and seals them, then sends `Results`.
  - The coordinator opens them under the day's wire key and stores all five; the worker sees them committed.
  - Found again, they're dropped by the have-filter (its snapshot plus the deltas of those inserts), or claimed as sampled sightings, which the coordinator answers from the store. None is granted again.
  - A drain then stops the worker with `Drained`.
- `nothing_runs_before_a_fresh_filter_snapshot` and `a_named_dictionary_is_waited_for`: the WK-8 gate, and newer-revision-only `Assign`s.
- `a_stop_with_a_stalled_session_finishes_within_its_deadline`: a full outbound queue with no session, and fetches that never return.
- `a_pre_sent_snapshot_seals_across_midnight` and `a_late_snapshot_is_waited_for_not_dropped`: nothing is reported lost, and `Blocked` never comes from "not ready". On the coordinator, `the_next_days_key_goes_out_from_23z` and `the_open_keys_follow_the_day`.
- `maybes_are_dropped_once_a_filter_is_complete_but_sampled`: 16,000 maybe-sightings with a fixed seed, of which 800–1,200 are claimed, every dropped one counted. `a_false_positive_is_claimed_on_a_later_sighting`.
- `hints_pass_the_network_policy`.
- Every wait is on a condition with a deadline of at least 10 s.
- **Not tested on their own:** a refused `Results` batch sent again on the next tick, and a split of a batch over `MAX_OPAQUE` (building sealed records outside the judge needs a prepared torrent). Both paths are short.
