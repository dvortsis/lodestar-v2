# CR-SIM-restart decisions

The follow-up `docs/decisions/2026-10-30-CR-0d.md` named for CR-0d-2a: a CR-SIM scenario that checks restored restrictions end to end, with the real actor and pool against the scripted BEP 51 network (runner brief, 2026-10-30). CR-0d's in-crate wire-level replay stays the unit gate; this adds the end-to-end check and a restart-cost baseline. No production behaviour changes.

- **Two legs in one runtime.** A `Scenario` may carry `restart: Some(Restart { leg1_minutes, gap_s, safe_restart })`. Both legs run in one paused current-thread runtime, so tokio's clock and the simulated wall clock (a fixed base plus elapsed virtual time) carry across the restart without any translation. The network is the same value in both legs: it keeps each node's sample window and interval, and its record of every sample.
  - **Leg 1 sits out the full first-start suppression** (6 h plus the query timeout) before it samples. There is no way around it that keeps the proof on: a `sample_start_delay` override turns the proof off, and an off run leaves `off_run` for the next start. Idle virtual hours are cheap; the whole scenario takes about 10 s of wall time.
  - **Replies in flight at the stop are dropped** (`Net::cut`): the old process's socket is gone. Samples the node sends after the network stopped serving are recorded unanswered (`Net::drain`), so each leg's `samples_sent` equals the network's count exactly.
  - **Leg 2 restores the pool's queue** from leg 1's, as the pipeline does from the store, and runs with a different actor RNG seed, as a new process would. The fetch outcomes keep their seed, so the network's luck is shared.
- **The check is a lower bound, computed independently of the code under test.** Each node's earliest allowed re-sample comes from the network's record of its last leg-1 sample and the sampler's rules: reply arrival plus the interval clamped to the 60 s floor and 6 h ceiling (`sampler::MIN_INTERVAL`, `MAX_INTERVAL`), the ceiling for an error reply, and the send plus the ceiling when no reply arrived before the stop. Every real deadline is at or after it, so a leg-2 sample before it is always a violation, never a modelling artefact. A reply due at the exact stop instant takes the earlier of its two possible deadlines.
- **What the run asserts:**
  - no leg-2 sample before its node's bound;
  - with `safe_restart` on: the reason is `resumed`, the first sample comes within a minute, `restart_obligations_restored` = `pending` + `honoured`, and `restored` is at least the restrictions the record says were live at the restart;
  - with it off (the negative control, a plain test rather than a second golden, to keep the suite fast): the reason is `off`, nothing is restored, and leg 2 sends no sample inside the suppression window;
  - both legs pass the existing conservation checks.
- **The golden `restart-honoured.txt`** reports leg 1's summary, leg 2's per-minute rows and totals, and a restart section. In it, the proof restored 1,991 obligations, exactly the 1,991 the record predicts. 943 expired during leg 2's hour and were all honoured; each node was sampled again a median of 11 s after expiry (316 s at most). The 1,048 still pending are the 6 h cooldowns from errors, timeouts and unsupported nodes. The restart cost: 1,742 stored in the first hour after a safe restart, against 0 in the first hour of a fresh start.
  - The three existing goldens are byte-identical. The shared run loop moved into a `Leg` type without changing the order of any spawn, read or stop.
- **One test-only seam in `crates/dht`:** `node::testing::stats(&NodeHandle)`, behind the `testing` feature. It reads the counters after `Node::shutdown`, which consumes the node, so a leg's totals include samples sent while stopping.
- **README:** the conservation paragraph no longer says the actor drops closed-caller requests without counting them. Since #232 they are `peer_queued_dropped`. The remaining bound is there only because of the 1 s gauge lag.
