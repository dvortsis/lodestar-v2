# Theme palettes: 24 families × light/dark

This is the source table for the 24 theme families (48 themes) in §10.18 of `docs/architecture.md`. Each family below has one fenced YAML block in Riverstone's schema; the theme files in `web/themes/*.json` are generated from these blocks, and the theme compiler derives every role token (inks, muted text, borders, tints, status colours, data variants) from them.

## Format

Each family is exactly one ```` ```yaml ```` block:

```text
id: <kebab-case id; use as the file name, e.g. web/themes/<id>.json>
name: "<display name>"
group: official | accessibility | community | original
light:
  background: "#RRGGBB"   # the page (daisyUI base-200)
  surface: "#RRGGBB"      # panels, rows, drawer (base-100)
  text: "#RRGGBB"         # base-content
  primary: "#RRGGBB"
  accent: "#RRGGBB"
  categorical: ["#RRGGBB", ×6]
dark:
  ... same keys ...
```

- Every value is a quoted, upper-case 6-digit hex (quoted because `#` starts a YAML comment).
- **`categorical` is in Riverstone order** (design-tokens.md §3): blue, sage, ochre, terracotta, slate, plum. The compiler maps `categorical[i]` to data slot `[1, 5, 2, 3, 4, 6][i]`, so the roles land on 1 Video (blue), 5 Images (sage), 2 Audio (ochre), 3 Software & executables (terracotta), 4 Documents (slate) and 6 Archives & disc images (plum). Where a palette has no slate-like hue, the slate role takes its next most distinct hue (cyan, orange, …). Each family's notes list its categorical picks in this order.
- `accent` is green wherever the source palette has a usable green, because the compiler derives `success` from `accent-ink`.
- Categorical colours are per mode. Values under 3:1 on the surface are allowed: the compiler derives a data variant by shifting OKLCH lightness only (§10.18), as it does for Riverstone light.

> **Family count.** The §10.18 table names 23 families; **Rosewood** (Originals) is the 24th, approved by the owner, giving 24 families × light/dark = 48 themes.

**ΔE_ok ≥ 5 warning.** Every theme clears 5 under normal vision. The weakest is Riverstone at 5.1 (sage–slate), which §10.18 grandfathers anyway. Under simulated colour-vision deficiency, most community palettes put blue and plum, or sage and ochre, close together. That's inherent to those palettes and stays a warning; the composition-bar mitigations in §10.18 (gaps, fixed order, text, patterns) cover it. The Accessibility and Originals families were tuned (lightness only) to keep CVD pairs ≥ 5 under both models where possible. Three fall just short:
- Okabe-Ito light: 4.9, ochre–terracotta, deutan (Machado 2009), after the derived orange shift;
- Sandstone light: 4.9, terracotta–slate, deutan (Machado 2009); and 4.9, blue–plum, protan (Viénot 1999);
- Moss light: 4.9, ochre–terracotta, deutan (Machado 2009).

`themes_check.py` checks only the raw values, not derived tokens. Several Originals light modes have raw categorical values at exactly 3.00–3.02:1. They pass as written; if the compiler rounds differently, it will simply derive a variant one step darker.

**Riverstone under CVD (correction to §10.18).** Riverstone's weakest categorical pair is:

| Mode | Deutan (Machado 2009) | Protan (Machado 2009) | Deutan (Viénot 1999) | Protan (Viénot 1999) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| light (data variants) | 3.4 sage–terracotta | 4.5 sage–terracotta | 3.5 sage–terracotta | 4.6 sage–slate |
| dark | 3.3 sage–terracotta | 4.5 sage–slate | 3.6 sage–terracotta | 4.6 sage–slate |

§10.18 says the plum swap keeps every pair at ΔE_ok ≥ 5.5 under deuteranopia and protanopia. **That doesn't hold under either model.** The plum fix itself still holds (plum is never in the weakest pair). The normal-vision figures and the data variants match §10.18 exactly. The script also measures the old sage–seafoam pair at 4.4, not ≈ 1.9. The runner logs the correction in §16.

### Okabe-Ito

```yaml
id: okabe-ito
name: "Okabe-Ito"
group: accessibility
light:
  background: "#F4F4F2"
  surface: "#FFFFFF"
  text: "#1A1A1A"
  primary: "#0072B2"
  accent: "#009E73"
  categorical: ["#0072B2", "#009E73", "#E69F00", "#D55E00", "#56B4E9", "#CC79A7"]
dark:
  background: "#121417"
  surface: "#1E2226"
  text: "#EDEDED"
  primary: "#56B4E9"
  accent: "#009E73"
  categorical: ["#56B4E9", "#009E73", "#E69F00", "#D55E00", "#F0E442", "#CC79A7"]
```

Text contrast: **light** 15.80:1 on background, 17.40:1 on surface; **dark** 15.76:1 on background, 13.67:1 on surface.

Categorical slots are the canonical Okabe-Ito set, mapped by hue to the roles (blue, sage, ochre, terracotta, slate, plum): blue `#0072B2`, bluish green `#009E73`, orange `#E69F00`, vermillion `#D55E00`, sky blue `#56B4E9`, reddish purple `#CC79A7`. In dark mode the blue role uses sky blue and the slate role uses yellow `#F0E442` (blue `#0072B2` is only 3.09:1 on the dark surface and too close to it in lightness to read well; yellow fails on white but is ideal on dark). Neutrals are plain greys so the categorical set carries all the colour. The light mode keeps the canonical values and lets the compiler derive data variants for orange and sky blue (both under 3:1 on white); after that shift orange vs vermillion is 4.9 ΔE_ok under deuteranopia (warning only).

Source: Masataka Okabe and Kei Ito, *Color Universal Design (CUD): how to make figures and presentations that are friendly to colorblind people* (2002, rev. 2008), <https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/>. The colour values are a published recommendation, free to use; credit the authors.
