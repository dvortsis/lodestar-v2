# Lodestar DHT crawler survey and scheduler review — 2026-11-16

> Review status: the [counter-review](2026-11-16-antigravity-throughput-assessment-review.md) qualifies the severity and scope of the scheduler counterexamples, the comparator evidence and the queue/throughput claims below. Consult it and the [subsequent research](2026-11-16-throughput-research-extension.md) for the current recommendations; this survey preserves the assessed record.

**Verdict: do not integrate the pasted scheduler yet.** It does not compile unchanged. Ten deterministic counterexamples now cover restart clock handling, same-file ownership, failed completion persistence, foreign permits, expired `hot_heap` ledger wedging, stale hot classification after failed `ExplicitUnsupported` completion, six-hour RAM poisoning on failed WAL reservation, 128-op SQLite batch rollback contamination, unbounded multi-restart drift within the 600 s wall-jump window, and AS112/AMT IPv4 bogon acceptance (plus detached WAL writer thread ownership). The most promising throughput work is controlling admission and preserving fresh peer evidence across the pipeline under the `p95 <= 10 min` latency gate; increasing the sampling table alone cannot establish 100 newly verified torrents/minute. The [strategy follow-up](2026-11-16-dht-throughput-strategy-review.md) adds previously omitted local performance evidence (including `soak-r3`, `soak-r5`, `CR-4`, `cr-ab-comp-1`, and `CR-1a-H` guard skips), Little's Law queue-residency constraints, production `Sampler` retention gaps, and frozen [CR-SPEC](../decisions/2026-11-02-CR-SPEC.md) alignment.

**What the protocol and research actually establish.** BEP 51 permits intervals 0–21,600 s, says not to sample again during the advertised period, distinguishes support by the **presence** of `samples` even when empty, and explicitly offers six-hour sweeps as a way to avoid interval bookkeeping. Changing `target` changes returned routing contacts, not which hashes the node samples. A local 60 s minimum is conservative policy, not a protocol requirement. A missing interval/non-support six-hour restriction is likewise your conservative policy. [B51]

Do not confuse random lookup targets with rotating sender identities. Bitmagnet rotates the sought target every ten seconds; its client sends its configured node ID separately. mlDHT partitions traversal work over RPC servers; “16” in the sampling call is a maximum task/server count, not evidence of 16 virtual identities per public IP. [BM10][BM11][ML2] shiyanhui's `target[:15] + localID[15:]` really does change the sender ID near the target. BEP 42 ties valid ID prefixes to external IP; its eight prefix choices do not authorize unlimited identities or load. Multiple ports do not create independent destination-IP rate budgets. [SH2][B42] The risks are filtering, routing-table damage and concentrated traffic; none of the reviewed sources establishes a universally safe query rate or ban threshold for a shared commercial VPN exit. [B51][B42][P1]

No time-bucketed interval wheel or hard per-sweep node-memory ceiling was verified in these inspected crawler paths. Bitmagnet uses node-record timestamps; mlDHT uses traversal-local sets and a coarse sweep cadence; magneticod does not enforce individual advertised intervals. The indexed ledger/wheel recommendation below is my design proposal, not an attributed implementation detail. [BM2][ML1][ML3][MG1]

Relevant research, with limits on what can be transferred:

- **Grunthal, Efficient Indexing of the BitTorrent Distributed Hash Table (2010), §§2, 3.6, 4.** Ordered traversal, shared lookup cache, adaptive timeouts and ARC/Blue duplicate suppression; deliberately deemphasizes popular repeats. Reports **>330 new torrents/10 min (33/min)** after one week, and up to **870/10 min (87/min)** with adjacent tracker-provided hashes. Hardware: 2 GHz quad-core AMD Opteron, 4 GB RAM, Linux/Java; **64 IPv4 +65 IPv6 addresses**, 192 lookups, 2,500–3,000 packets/s, ~4.8–5 Mb/s. Its **587,000/week** is an accumulated count, not a steady 100/min result. It predates BEP 51 and is not a single-IP benchmark. [P1]
- **Wolchok & Halderman, Crawling BitTorrent DHTs for Fun and Profit (WOOT 2010), §§5–7, Tables 1–3.** Vuze, 4,000 Sybils/hop, quad-core Xeon/12 GB RAM; “one million indexed” chiefly means torrent-description records. Mean full-torrent recovery **56,590/249,371 ≈22.6%** when both description and peer list were available; pooled recovery **26.1%**. These are torrent-level retrieval coverage, not success per peer attempt. Table 2's 30 min retrieval stage and 153 min full pipeline are one representative run, not a sustained unique/minute benchmark. Link capacity is not established by its SATA disk's 3 Gb/s specification. [P2]
- **Wang & Kangasharju, Real-World Sybil Attacks in BitTorrent Mainline DHT.** Primary measurement evidence for deployed ID manipulation, routing pollution and monitoring; not evidence that such behavior is polite or needed for indexing. BEP 51 cites it as motivation. [P3][B51]
- **Juenemann et al., Towards a Basic DHT Service (2011), measurement methodology and connectivity results.** Useful on churn and partial connectivity; its historical NAT/firewall estimates do not predict this VPN's metadata failure rate. **No modern BEP 51 adoption, source-share or BEP 9 success-rate measurement for your exact setup was verified.** The older papers above predate BEP 51. [P4][B51]

**Candidate changes.** These are engineering proposals informed by sources, not measured speedup promises. The original ordering below is superseded by the [strategy follow-up](2026-11-16-dht-throughput-strategy-review.md): measure the current baseline and coordinated admission first, then test freshness and hints together with attribution. Existing local screens do not justify ranking hints alone first.

1. **Preserve the sampling responder and obtain peers immediately; start fetching on the first usable peer batch.** BEP 51 samples keys the responder stores, so one bounded direct `get_peers` there is better grounded than starting every hash with a fresh full lookup. Bitmagnet and magneticod retain this association; mlDHT ranks endpoints by reporting-source corroboration. Preserve fallback when the responder has no usable values. [B51][BM3][MG1][ML5] Lodestar already implements 60 s responder hints and streaming delivery behind opt-ins; review their actual runtime enablement and measurements before duplicating them. [L1][L2] **Risk:** direct queries can concentrate on one responder; keep its per-IP guard, aggregate budgets and one-attempt fallback. Never dial the DHT UDP responder as though it were a torrent peer.

   A fresh announcement supplies a candidate torrent endpoint using its announced port (or UDP source port when `implied_port` is set); it still does not prove that peer has complete metadata or is reachable. An observed `get_peers` sender's UDP port is not a reliable torrent TCP endpoint. Use returned peer values or a validated announced endpoint. Connecting for the actual metadata handshake supplies the useful open-port evidence; a separate speculative port probe adds traffic and delay. [B5][B9]

2. **Replace one giant aging backlog with a small fresh ready set plus a bounded deferred store (aligned with planned `CR-2`).** Prioritize fresh validated announces and fresh peer-bearing responses (`T1`), preserve `T0` interactive interest with bounded burst, and age-bound `T3` cold background work so `p95 discovery-to-searchable <= 10 min (600 s)` holds; keep `T2` independent corroboration deferred until a `>=64-bit` keyed reporter-tag contract with bounded channel evidence is defined, preserving the ban on actor/pool shared mutable maps, bare/XOR Bloom mixing, or counting self-generated lookup results as corroboration ([PLAN §8][PLAN]). Also account for `Actor::seen` (`1M–2M` rotating Bloom hashes, ~5.6–11.2 h at `49.7/s`), which marks every sampled hash seen upstream before admission so the `99.47%` of keys evicted unattempted from `Pool::entries` (`CR-0`) cannot trigger fresh `CR-1a-H` hint recapture while retained in `SeenFilter`. Source precedents: mlDHT's priority/initial/failed states, shuffled prefetch and age-dependent purging; magneticod's bounded drop-on-saturation sink; Grunthal's suppression of popular duplicates. [ML8][ML9][MG3][P1] **Proposal:** reserve most work for recent peer evidence and age out its dialing usefulness on the scale of measured peer lifetimes; keep older hashes cheaply for later re-observation. Numeric TTLs/shares need A/B measurement. **Risk:** low; reduced load, with explicit coverage/fairness tradeoffs. LIFO alone can starve old keys; FIFO alone can waste fresh peers.

3. **Decouple cooldown protection, active candidates and productive-node retention.** Keep exact unexpired endpoint restrictions independently of routing/frontier eviction. An indexed tree/heap or timing wheel with upward-rounded deadlines can schedule a small ready pool, while a durable ledger holds protection for all admitted samples. Reject unknown work if protection cannot be recorded; never evict live restrictions just to admit another sample. Separately demote repeatedly unreachable “hot” nodes. Existing Lodestar already separates a 20k frontier from a 200k state map, but `next()` stops when that map is full, `Sampler::prune` discards expired productive entries instead of re-offering them to `frontier`, and `sample_tick` pushes rate-throttled popped candidates to the back of `frontier` (`push_back`) behind up to 20k entries. Retaining a 1-bit `productive` marker in `Sampler` to re-queue expired productive nodes (inside `CR-0d`) and preserving front priority on throttles captures the main benefit without a separate SQLite scheduler. [L3][L8] **Alternative:** mlDHT's seven-hour sweep design avoids frequent node revisits, but its reachable-server filter and restart/revisit policy need adaptation for an outbound-only crawler. [ML1][ML2][ML3] **Risk:** low if intervals survive eviction/restart; high if “drop unsupported” means forget and immediately rediscover/reprobe.

4. **Measure the conversion pipeline, then allocate capacity to the saturated stage.** Split unique discovery → admitted lookup → peers found → connected → extension negotiated → complete validated metadata → first successful durable insert. Export live queue age, accepted/running/terminal lookups, wire request rate, failure stage, occupancy seconds, per-source yield and duplicate-success rate. mlDHT distinguishes sockets from established connections; Bitmagnet distinguishes new torrent persistence from rediscovery. [ML4][BM9] Lodestar's checked-in older soak even observed large logical lookup counts with almost no active metadata fetches; that historical result is not evidence of today's run, but makes the denominator check essential. [L4] **Risk:** negligible network effect; metrics can show whether more sockets would do anything.

5. **Shorten wasted connection occupancy and remember failure evidence at the right scope.** Use separate connect, handshake and useful-progress deadlines within a total attempt budget. magneticod uses a 1 s connect timeout; Bitmagnet uses 6 s total per peer; mlDHT uses an adaptive connect timeout and rotating filters for failed connections/no-extension support. [MG4][BM7][ML5][ML10] **Proposal:** test ~2–4 s connect and ~6–10 s total against observed VPN RTTs; do not assume 1 s fits your path. Cache endpoint-level connect failures across hashes, but keep “does not have this torrent” or metadata reject scoped to that hash. A fresh valid announcement should permit reconsideration. **Risk:** shorter timeouts can waste retries or exclude distant peers; avoid aggressive retry loops and permanent IP bans on one failure.

6. **Use a small, budgeted peer race for promising hashes; cancel losers after verified success.** mlDHT connects to multiple candidates per hash, while Bitmagnet/magneticod try sequentially. [ML5][BM6][MG3] **Proposal:** try two peers from distinct IPs for fresh promising hashes; keep the global socket and per-IP limits, add a handshake-start rate cap, and stop on the first valid complete info dictionary. This can improve latency and avoid one dead first peer; it does not make dead swarms live. **Risk:** medium: more simultaneous connections; naive racing every queued hash increases load and can reduce useful attempts/sec.

7. **Audit BEP 9/10 interoperability, then A/B transport diversity.** Verify the extension bit, remote extension ID for outgoing messages, local ID for incoming messages, zero meaning disabled, keepalives/unrelated messages, piece bounds/duplicates/rejects, final raw-info hash validation and memory budgets. [B9][B10] `CR-4` already wired shared-port `dht.utp` (`crates/utp`, `crates/dht`, `crates/lodestar/src/services.rs`) and `dht.route_backoff` (both off by default) alongside Lodestar's MSE/plaintext fallback, bounded metadata memory, and four-piece initial window, and `docs/build-plan.md` §0.5 queues `CR-AB-4` directly behind `CR-AB-ADM` because `CR-0` final recorded **288,665 (74.1%) `timed_out`**, **57,364 (14.7%) `unreachable`**, and **43,481 (11.2%) `refused`** among 389,644 failed metadata fetches. [CR4][CR0F][L5][L2] **Proposal:** keep plain/MSE compatibility and run the frozen `CR-SPEC` ABBA/BAAB evaluation of `dht.utp` before inventing another transport path. **Risk:** medium if fallback doubles every failed attempt or starves the shared UDP actor loop. Encryption is interoperability, not a remedy for NAT or a guarantee against VPN throttling.

8. **Keep one stable, appropriate identity and make traversal diverse within the aggregate budget.** Use random targets or a cursor/prefix walk, collect returned nodes from sampling replies and filter bad/unreachable nodes. Use BEP 42 identity construction for the VPN's actual public egress where applicable; honor BEP 43 if actually uncontactable. [B51][B42][B43][ML1] Separate sample budget from peer-finding/maintenance shares under one aggregate limiter so discovery cannot starve conversion. **Risk:** low for target diversity; high for target-spoofed identities, routing pollution or multiplying same-IP workers without shared guards. This recommendation preserves politeness rather than offering a guaranteed rate increase.

9. **Treat inbound reachability as an architectural constraint; put holepunching last.** Lack of inbound does not prevent outbound TCP to a reachable peer or solicited DHT replies through a permitted NAT mapping. It does remove unsolicited peer connections and much of the passive-discovery advantage; two unreachable peers cannot normally complete a direct TCP connection. BEP 43 expressly supports uncontactable clients doing outgoing queries, and BEP 5 says such nodes need periodic routing refresh. [B43][B5] BEP 55 requires an existing connected rendezvous peer that is also connected to the target, both endpoints' support and simultaneous uTP attempts; it cannot turn an arbitrary DHT address into a reachable peer. [B55] **Inference:** prioritize active sampling/fresh outbound peers; measure actual UDP return paths and VPN drops. mlDHT's current TaskBuilder reachable-server filter is a specific integration blind spot behind this VPN. **Risk:** low for correct reachability handling; holepunch implementation complexity is high and its payoff here is unverified.

**Capacity arithmetic for the target.** This is derived from your supplied observations, not an external benchmark.

At 4% success, 100 successes/min needs 2,500 completed attempts/min, or **41.7/s**, even if every success is new. With mean occupied socket time 8 s, that requires roughly **334 continuously occupied metadata sockets**. If success rises to 20% at the same service time, the requirement drops to about **67**. More generally:

`new_verified_per_min ≈ 60 × average_active_metadata_sockets × per_attempt_success × fraction_of_successes_that_are_new / mean_socket_occupancy_seconds`.

This is steady-state accounting, not an independence assumption or a promise that enough good peers exist. A saturated **logical infohash lookup** counter is not a saturated metadata-socket counter. Lodestar's library defaults are 400 sockets /256 logical lookups /200k pending /32 peers per key /4 lookup attempts /120 s initial retry backoff; runtime presets and options can change effective behavior. [L6][L2]

For cooldowns, steady-state occupancy is approximately **new endpoint admissions/s × mean retained cooldown seconds**, plus queued/in-flight/hot retention. A 200k exact ledger holding six-hour restrictions supports only **9.26 new endpoints/s** continuously. Filling 200k in 35 min suggests roughly **95 endpoint admissions/s** while it fills; if all retained for six hours, that requires ~**2.06m records**, before headroom. The proposal's 4m default and 25/s sample cap change the capacity regime, but do not prove its heap/memory/SQLite behavior or metadata conversion. Endpoint admission rate and retention distribution must be measured separately from duplicate candidates.

**Detailed assessment of the pasted scheduler.** Locations below are line numbers in the extracted Rust code block ([original module snapshot](dht-survey-evidence/original-scheduler.rs.txt)); attachment lines are those numbers +23. - **Blocker, verified compilation:** E0499 at module lines 1278, 1285, 1530: a mutable ledger-record borrow overlaps writes to other fields through the mutex guard. The original does not compile with Rust 1.99.1 and the locked harness dependencies. The previous audit did not catch these remaining blockers.
- **Critical, verified restart counterexample:** lines 717–769, especially 758, and writer lines 1714–1739. After A gets a six-hour cooldown, advance monotonic time 10 s, jump wall time 24 h, commit B, restart one wall second later. `.max(current_wall_sec)` admits A early. The supplied RG6 only jumps after the last commit. Use a defined trusted timebase/uncertainty state; merely removing one `max` is not a complete restart-clock design, since forward jumps within the trusted 600 s window remain indistinguishable from downtime.
- **High, conditional on multiple owners, verified:** `open_with_config` permits two schedulers on one file; `next_op_seq` is local to each loaded snapshot. A writes X at sequences 1/2; B writes Y at 1/2, then X at 3, overwriting A's live restriction. SQLite serialization and a monotonic row condition do not enforce scheduler ownership. Acquire a process/file ownership lock before loading state, or move admission/sequence allocation into shared transactional state. If integration already guarantees one owner, document/test that boundary rather than calling this an unconditional single-instance failure.
- **High integration concern:** new SQLite cooldown state is not automatically compatible with Lodestar's existing `CR-0d` restart proof ([CR-0d][CR0D]), `WK-10e` same-IP politeness scaling ([WK-10e][WK10E]), actor transaction reservations, IPv6/normalized endpoints, and IP guards. Meanwhile the proposal accepts only `SocketAddrV4` (the current sampler accepts `SocketAddr`) and supplies no adapter, migration, `WK-10e`/`CR-0d` integration, all-query budget wiring, or benchmark result. [L3][L7][CR0D][WK10E]
- **Medium persistence/resource issue:** lines 718–724 discard every metadata-read error via `.ok()`; range conversions also fall back to current wall time. Distinguish missing rows from corruption/read failures and reject invalid timebase values. Do **not** claim `.ok()` alone proves premature dispatch: a later insert/error may fail closed.
- **Medium persistence/resource issue:** lines 954–1021 remove memory records while cleanup `try_send` errors are ignored. Stale disk rows can accumulate. Add drop metrics, bounded retry/reconciliation, disk-row/WAL-size limits and shutdown flushing/ownership release. The newer sequence checks reduce the earlier stale-upsert risk under one owner; dropped deletion is not independently proof that dispatch-before-persistence recurs.
- **Medium liveness/performance:** productive-node timeouts preserve support and prior novelty (lines 1510–1520), retaining hot classification; no consecutive-failure retirement policy is demonstrated. Heap rebuilds run under the core mutex, and expired stale entries need not count against the reclaim-item budget. - **Missing crash/admission contract:** reservation horizon starts before awaiting WAL and before the caller actually sends UDP (lines 1267–1330). A permit can be held arbitrarily long. A strict crash guarantee needs a bounded valid send window and coverage of the response/commit uncertainty, or a conservative restart suppression protocol. The abort test commits then aborts before real network I/O; it does not test “remote replied with long interval, local completion lost.”
- **High, verified live completion failure (Finding 7):** the preceding concern also causes an early dispatch without a crash or clock jump. After removing the trigger, `acquire_next_dispatch` returns a permit at t=21600, ten seconds before the received interval ends. Cancellation/error reconciliation retains the original reservation deadline. Preserve the received deadline in memory on failure and define durable coverage of the send/response/commit uncertainty; an in-memory repair alone does not solve restart safety. It creates a fresh temporary crate, checks Rust/Cargo 1.99.1, captures original compilation errors, applies only the mechanical borrow repairs, verifies that all ten supplied tests ran, runs Clippy/format check, then runs both regression groups. Offline mode requires the dependencies to be cached. [Recorded verification](dht-survey-evidence/verification.md)

**Follow-up review improvements.** The original evidence depended on a local attachment, merely documented the compiler version, and left personal temporary paths in captured evidence logs. The fixture, version/count checks, and path-scrubbing in `verify-proposal.ps1` address those reproducibility and hygiene gaps. Behavioral coverage should still extend the clock test from candidate admission to actual dispatch and the packet boundary, and add delayed-send, group-commit failure-isolation, and repeated-restart cases. The revised strategy prioritizes measuring existing switches under frozen `CR-SPEC`, fixes the `CR-1a-H` routing-damage and tick-latency blind spots, accounts for the `CR-ADM` FIFO wait vs 60 s hint TTL interaction, and favors incremental changes to the existing indexed sampler. The local baselines (`CR-0`, `soak-r3`, `soak-r5`, `cr-ab-comp-1`) and `CR-SIM` screens in the [strategy assessment](2026-11-16-dht-throughput-strategy-review.md) materially qualify the original ranking.

**Remaining unknowns:** no verified modern per-project BEP51/passive source percentages, comparable per-peer BEP9 success rates, single-IP/no-port-forward 100/min strict BEP 51 compliant searchable demonstration (note `cr-ab-comp-1` reached 317.8 stored/min on this host without port forwarding while clamping >300 s novel-hash intervals to 60 s and measuring `stored`, not strict-interval `searchable_new_total`), VPN-specific block thresholds, or quantified live transport/encryption/holepunch lift. Project source and the comparator establish host headroom, but those missing numbers require the controlled `CR-SPEC` runs on this network. The next implementation should address the scheduler defects (if any disk-backed scheduler is revisited) and run controlled conversion experiments while keeping all advertised intervals and existing protection gates.
