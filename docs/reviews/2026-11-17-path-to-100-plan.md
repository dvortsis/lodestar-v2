# Highest-probability path to more than 100 searchable torrents/minute

Research and planning review, 2026-11-17 UTC. Source baseline: review branch `fd93da234fe6d2b12ae51390e6a91cca123f3421`; local main `63a2c6dfd64495d6cc3a1178e82c2361c47ba824`. This is a recommendation, not a change to the runner's experiment order or the frozen measurement specification.

**Prioritize useful work reaching metadata retrieval: validate coordinated admission, preserve fresh opportunities through the queues, and remove measured lookup/dispatch stalls. Then improve the limiting conversion stage.** This has a stronger case than beginning with a new persistent sampling scheduler, more raw hashes, or broad concurrency increases. “Highest probability” is an engineering ranking by local evidence, relevance to the limiting resource, implementation readiness and test cost; the available evidence does not support numerical probabilities of success.

The strategy can plausibly produce a substantial improvement. It does **not yet establish that this crawler can exceed 100/min**. Historical output is roughly 11–17 stored/min, and the strongest local isolated screen is around 30% under production pool defaults. Closing that gap requires a much larger change in useful service rate or conversion, demonstrated across the whole pipeline. Do not multiply unrelated paper and simulator percentages to manufacture that result.

This plan consolidates the [Antigravity counter-review](2026-11-16-antigravity-throughput-assessment-review.md) and [subsequent research](2026-11-16-throughput-research-extension.md). Their correctness findings remain applicable; this document supplies the execution priorities and decision rules.

## 1. Define the result before optimizing

Use [CR-SPEC](../decisions/2026-11-02-CR-SPEC.md): one node, one public IP, the specified experiment host and Balanced preset, a fresh data directory for the combined run, and new **network-origin, verified, searchable** torrents. Raw hashes, peer responses, metadata successes, imports, recovery and duplicate stores are not substitutes.

The frozen acceptance gate is a valid 24-hour net mean **at least 100/min**, every measured hour at least 70/min, discovery-to-searchable p95 at most 600 seconds, and latency coverage at least 98%, with dropped samples handled as specified. Report strict **greater than 100/min** separately to answer the user's stronger wording. Neither a burst above 100 nor a 100.0 mean establishes strict exceedance.

Use **120/min as a proposed engineering capacity target**, providing 20% headroom over 100. This is a planning assumption, not a new acceptance threshold, forecast or guarantee against hourly variation. Preserve first-sighting age across queue changes; do not improve reported latency by restarting its clock. Report the six-hour first-start sampling suppression and warmup separately, as CR-SPEC requires.

## 3. Make the capacity argument measurable

For a consistently attributed lookup-fed path, define:

- `L`: maximum concurrently occupied logical lookup permits; `W`: **mean complete permit residence**, including failed/cancelled attempts and any waiting while holding a permit.
- `y`: distinct new searchable torrents attributable to that path per admitted logical lookup attempt. Retries count in the denominator; each output is credited once, using a declared attribution rule.
- `q`: mean wire DHT queries per admitted lookup, including speculative/retried queries; `Q`: queries/second available to those lookups after sampling, routing maintenance and other DHT work.
- `A`: eligible lookup admissions/second sustainable from the discovery/queue path, excluding duplicate offers that cannot produce additional work.

Then a steady-state capacity bound for that path is:

`R_lookup <= min(60*A*y, 60*L*y/W, 60*Q*y/q)`.

These are average resource bounds, not predictions or p95 estimates. Use actual occupied permit-seconds and all terminal outcomes, not latency among successful lookups alone. Attribute direct-peer work separately, avoid double credit when paths overlap, and apply the global metadata/store/index limits to their combined output.

For metadata, let `S` be concurrent logical fetch slots, `T` mean occupied slot-seconds per logical fetch task, and `p_new` new searchable outputs per such task. Its analogous bound is `60*S*p_new/T`, also limited by available eligible peers, per-IP caps, memory and output backpressure. **Keep the unit consistent:** uTP-to-TCP fallback can increment multiple transport-attempt counters inside one logical fetch task. Do not divide logical slot occupancy by an incompatible transport-attempt count. Verification, duplicate exclusion and index loss are included in `p_new`; report their individual conversions too.

Illustrative budgets, **not measured operating points**:

| Assumptions | Implication |
|---|---|
| `L=32`, `y=0.20`, no direct-peer contribution | Strictly exceeding 100/min requires `W < 3.84 s`, plus sufficient packet, supply and downstream capacity. |
| `Q=150/s`, `q=20`, `y=0.20` | Packet capacity is only 90/min, regardless of how quickly permits return. |
| Proposed 120/min case: `y=0.25`, `L=32`, `W=4 s`, `q=15` | Requires 8 eligible lookup attempts/s and 120 lookup queries/s. With a 200/s aggregate DHT budget, 80/s remains for other DHT work. This operates at the modeled lookup bound; it is not a robust guarantee. |
| Same 120/min case: 20 logical metadata tasks/s, `p_new=0.10`, `T=8 s` | Requires 160 occupied fetch slots on average and sustainable store/index service above 2 records/s. It is internally compatible with 2.5 metadata tasks per lookup, but none of these yields is established. |
| Keep historical raw success near 4.38% | 100 raw successes/min needs about 38.1 attempts/s; 120 needs about 45.7/s, versus historical 4.71/s. New-searchable output can need more. With consistent 8-second logical tasks the latter would occupy about 365 slots, leaving little margin under a nominal 400 cap. |

Do not prescribe raising 32 lookup permits or 400 fetch slots from this arithmetic. Those are configured limits, not evidence of sustained utilization. In CR-0, the sampled peer-request, pool-lookup and outstanding-RPC gauges were not uniformly full. Low occupancy can result from starvation, cooldown, memory pressure or scheduling; raising a ceiling does not fix those causes.

## 4. Execution order and decisions

### Step 0 — Establish a usable baseline and bottleneck ledger

Use the latest valid raw collection if it exists. Otherwise prepare the already-defined CR-ADM comparison with its off arm as baseline. Add only missing diagnostics, identically in both arms, before freezing the build/configuration. A diagnostic-only replay or short run can select experiments; it cannot replace CR-SPEC evidence.

| Boundary | Required observation | Existing source / missing work to scope |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery to admission | Distinct eligible offers, admission wait and age, expiry/eviction by state, failed delivery followed by suppression | Actor `harvest_hash`, `SeenFilter`, sampler and pool. Existing harvest/eviction/admission counters are useful; matched bounded cohorts and stage-age distributions need an implementation audit. |
| Lookup service | Granted/returned permits, occupied seconds, queued versus executing time, queries by purpose, useful peers and terminal reason | Existing coordinated-admission identities and peer/RPC gauges; add missing duration/query attribution without hash/IP metric labels. |
| Metadata service | Eligible-peer backlog, task/transport attempts, occupied seconds, connection/MSE/extension/piece/reservation durations and outcomes | Existing fetch transport/family counters and `fetch_held_bytes`, `fetch_reserved_bytes`, `fetch_held_full_total`, `fetch_reserve_timeouts_total`; detailed phase timers need audit/addition. |
| Verification to search | Unique verified, duplicate/invalid exclusions, new searchable, index backlog/lag, latency coverage | Existing gate counters and histograms in [metrics](../metrics.md); reconcile deltas under CR-SPEC rather than using UI totals. |

Preserve low metric cardinality and bounded sampling. Never export infohashes or endpoints as metric labels. Explain unresolved samples, output at the window boundaries, and measurement overhead. Rank candidate changes by expected **absolute new-searchable gain at the limiting budget**, with uncertainty; a large percentage in a tiny stage need not matter.

### Step 1 — Finish the implemented admission and transport experiments

Preserve the recorded order in [runner/slices.yaml](../../runner/slices.yaml): **CR-AB-ADM → CR-AB-4 → CR-CP1**. Admission has the best local screen and an implemented switch. uTP is an implemented compatibility experiment with substantially weaker causal evidence about the historical failures; its position also reflects the existing experiment contract and low additional implementation cost.

Run each treatment alone under CR-SPEC, holding the other crawler opt-ins off except its specified safe-restart constant. A mechanism check must show it acted: no-op uTP is void, and route backoff is skipped with a recorded reason when the baseline has no IPv6 no-route opportunity. Do not call 74% timeout share evidence that uTP fixes 74% of failures.

The [fetch implementation](../../crates/peer-wire/src/fetch.rs) makes this distinction material: one overall deadline covers transport fallback, MSE, transfer and reservation waits. `Encryption::Prefer` retries plaintext after `Error::Mse` while time remains; it does not provide an independent plaintext budget after every overall timeout. Inspect phase measurements before changing encryption preference or timeout allocation. Preserve compatibility and the user's encryption requirements. First prove a fallback/deadline opportunity with controlled peers; this is a diagnostic hypothesis, not an externally proven new gain.

At CR-CP1, report the specified additive and multiplicative projections with uncertainty and their assumptions. Neither is a combined measurement. If these tests fall far short, proceed to the measured limiting resource below; do not spend weeks testing every small routing idea in a fixed list.

### Step 2 — Repair useful-work starvation, if the baseline shows it

This is the leading new implementation track when lookup/fetch capacity is available but fresh, useful work is lost or delayed. Implement independently switchable slices so attribution survives:

1. **Freshness and bounded fairness:** apply the existing [CR-2 plan](../plan/dht-crawler-implementation-plan-2026-10-25.md) at retention, lookup admission and eligible socket dispatch. Bounded T0 interactive bursts, T1 fresh hint/announce opportunities, and a non-starving T3 cold queue must agree across those boundaries. Keep T2 corroboration deferred until its reporter-identity/evidence contract is defined. Measure age at service, yield per occupied second and cold-work service, not only average queue depth. An admission timestamp is not a new discovery timestamp.
2. **Dispatch when eligible:** wake queued work on admission/capacity/token availability, with bounded work per actor turn. Re-drive eligible farther candidates promptly when an initial batch was entirely locally skipped. Preserve per-IP hint restrictions and all rate/capacity limits. Existing maintenance already processes lookup continuations before sampling; prove the actual scheduling gap rather than assuming sampling always runs first. Test sample/maintenance fairness under sustained lookup demand.
3. **Bounded rediscovery recovery:** account for a hash remembered before delivery and later discarded without useful service. Allow a bounded reoffer only after an explicit eligible state transition or fresh qualifying evidence, respecting deduplication, retry budgets and cooldowns. Do not disable SeenFilter globally, count repeated sightings as independent corroboration, or create actor/pool shared mutable maps.

Screen these at production queue/backoff defaults, including overload, cancellation, drain/restart, duplicate sightings and all-local-skip cases. Use paired seeds and report all scenarios. The simulator cannot validate NAT or live peer freshness. Advance only candidates that improve the relevant resource efficiency with intact invariants; a faster first peer alone is insufficient.

### Step 3 — Choose one next experiment from the limiting resource

| Observation after the earlier steps | Next candidate | Keep/reject test |
|---|---|---|
| Permits mostly occupied; large time spent awaiting unresponsive contacts | Bounded soft-timeout progress/fresh-candidate speculation; separately evaluate recently responsive, low-RTT routing candidates [S1] | Increase attributable output per permit-second **and** sustain the packet budget. Track progress-blocking requests separately from real outstanding transactions; retain owners, hard deadlines and exactly-once settlement. First-values latency alone does not decide. |
| DHT packet budget binding | Reduce queries per useful output through validated routing quality, enlarged useful buckets [S1], or within-bucket identifier diversity [S2] | Measure total maintenance plus lookup packets and novelty-adjusted yield. No blanket fan-out increase. Identifier diversity is different from subnet caps; neither a KAD/MDHT comparison nor a maintenance bundle isolates every proposed patch. |
| Eligible peer supply healthy, metadata slots occupied, low conversion | Use phase evidence to test the specific reachability, handshake or deadline problem; retain successful CR-4 outcomes | Improve new searchable/task-second at bounded total connection attempts and memory. Reject a faster timeout that discards useful slow peers without net output benefit. Reservation waits require a downstream/budget investigation, not a network explanation. |
| Fresh eligible work is scarce even though service capacity is available | Retain productive BEP 51 responders across cooldown expiry, with decaying yield/recency scores and an exploration share | Measure distinct, ultimately useful new hashes per sampling packet. Keep cooldown/proof state separate from candidate desirability. Honor [BEP 51 intervals][S3]; target choice affects returned nodes, not the sampled hashes. |
| Confirmed lossy live contacts; spare query budget | Selective bounded DHT retransmission [S4] | Compare against querying another eligible contact, at the same aggregate budget. Do not retransmit sample RPCs on the assumption that a lost response means no interval was established. |
| Store/index backlog grows or reservation waits dominate | Profile writer/index service and backpressure; tune the demonstrated limiting service | Preserve verification, durable semantics and gate accounting. No storage rewrite or arbitrary batch-size change without a measured bottleneck. |
| A particular CPU service/actor is saturated | Profile-guided release compilation [S5], or a narrowly profiled hot-path change | Require improved output and CPU/record on held-out representative workloads. Low process-wide CPU can conceal one busy actor; PGO cannot remove network/cooldown waits. |

Select only the row supported by evidence. Recompute the capacity ledger after each material change because the bottleneck can move. Bounded speculation and retransmission compete for the same resources; test separately before proposing an interaction. RTT preference must retain coverage/exploration and cannot route solely to the fastest endpoints.

## 5. Evidence strength and additional-method research

The post-review search already identified two additional candidates not found as implemented/tested in the inspected project record: **selective DHT RPC retransmission and PGO**. The [research extension](2026-11-16-throughput-research-extension.md) records novelty searches, exclusions and exact applicability limits. This plan rechecked their primary sources; it does not count them as new discoveries again. Unrecorded or inaccessible experiments remain unknown.

- **[S1] Jimenez, Osmani and Knutsson, IEEE P2P 2011:** live Mainline measurements support testing routing quality. RTT preference changes the standard median from 510 to 459 ms while worsening the reported tail; the aggressive variant improves 284 to 185 ms. Enlarged buckets further improve those variants to 286 and 164 ms respectively. The NICE result includes maintenance/quarantine changes. These are conditional latency results, not additive throughput multipliers.
- **[S2] Salah, Roos and Strufe, 2014 technical report:** within-bucket identifier diversity has modeled/simulated evidence and live KAD mean hop counts of 3.39862 versus 3.23172, a calculated reduction of about 4.9%. The authors' conservative CI-adjusted comparison is about 4.08%. Credible primary evidence, but not a live Mainline searchable-output result or proof of a multi-bit-zone redesign.
- **[S3] BEP 51:** authoritative protocol constraints, not performance evidence. Respect the responder's interval; changing the request target does not select the samples.
- **[S4] Liu et al., 2012:** live KAD search success rises from 91.19% to 95.97% with retransmission, about 5.24% relative improvement. Extra traffic can erase this gain under a fixed packet budget; KAD results do not establish Mainline metadata benefit.
- **[S5] Rust compiler team, 2020:** reports 10–16% shorter compilation times with PGO in its benchmark setup on one machine. This establishes a CPU optimization technique, not a 10–16% crawler gain. Train and optimize Lodestar itself, not merely its compiler.

Deprioritize the reviewed persistent scheduler replacement because of its unresolved lifecycle/persistence defects and weak evidence that sampling dispatch is the current limiting stage. Hint/streaming combinations remain possible experiments, but their earlier screens do not justify assigning a large standalone gain. Do not relabel already implemented mimalloc, metadata pipelining, TCP_NODELAY or Tokio cooperation as new methods. Keep unverified reference-code details out of quantitative claims.

## 6. Validate the actual combination and stop at the right point

Use the existing two-block ABBA + BAAB minimum, phase offset, validity rules and regression/decision procedure. A treatment win requires the prescribed positive lower 95% confidence bound and point improvement of at least 5%; inconclusive results follow the predefined extension, not repeated ad hoc peeking. Proposed interactions need a recorded design extension **before** affected data collection because ordinary CR-SPEC arms hold other treatments off.

Two minimum blocks cost 48 measured hours per treatment, plus eight one-hour warmups and two six-hour initial suppressions: approximately **68 host-hours before switching, phase scheduling and retries**. Do not schedule both admission and uTP as a two-day acceptance effort. Reuse valid existing completed runs if available. Cheap deterministic/simulator screens should prevent obviously ineffective or incorrect candidates from consuming those host windows.

Run CR-COMB from a fresh directory with the selected configuration only after lifecycle checks and the existing dependencies, including a CR-5 tuning decision or reasoned skip, are complete. Publish the code/configuration identity, raw data, analyzer output, net mean, every hourly rate, latency coverage/p95 and guardrails. Also publish new verified versus new searchable, carried-in contribution and remaining queues. Treat memory/FD growth or impaired fairness as a failed engineering result even if a brief output burst looks good.

If the valid combined run exceeds 100/min and passes the other gates, the throughput objective is demonstrated **for that run and environment**. If it does not, record the measured deficit and the remaining service constraint. Choose another experiment only if its supported improvement could materially close that deficit at the fixed budgets. If no such candidate remains, report that the present design/constraints have not demonstrated a route to the target and prepare an explicit architecture/options decision. Additional nodes, public IPs or imports cannot silently satisfy this single-node goal. More literature searching alone is not evidence that the gap has closed.

## 7. Self-review and disposition

I challenged the plan first against the source and existing contracts, then against its numerical and causal claims. The final checks and corrections are:

| Challenge | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Does the plan promise a six-to-ninefold gain from small isolated results? | No. Large gain remains a hypothesis; the 120/min arithmetic states necessary compatible budgets rather than a forecast. |
| Could faster lookups exhaust another resource? | Yes. The packet, supply, metadata, memory and indexing bounds must all hold; mean bounds do not establish p95 or worst-hour performance. |
| Are configured capacity and observed utilization confused? | No. Measure occupied seconds, blocked reasons and eligible supply before raising caps. |
| Are raw metadata attempts a stable denominator with fallback? | Not automatically. Separate logical tasks from transport attempts and credit each new searchable output once. |
| Are all timeout failures network failures? | No. Include MSE, transfer and reservation waits; reuse existing reservation counters before adding diagnostics. |
| Does the hint “Skipped” proposal repair routing damage? | No. The reviewed local failure does not mark the routing table bad. Keep the guard; only measured scheduling idle time motivates a throughput change. |
| Can queue trimming manufacture a latency win? | Preserve first-sighting timestamps, publish losses/evictions, and require searchable output and fairness alongside latency. |
| Are primary-source results transferred across outcomes/networks? | Each remains labeled latency, hop count, search success or CPU time. None proves Lodestar's throughput. |
| Does the recommendation change the frozen protocol or pretend current run status is known? | No. Reconcile new evidence first; preserve existing order and gates, with prospective decisions for extensions. |
| Is “agree with every part” treated as certainty about future performance? | No. I agree with these conditional recommendations and stated limitations. Their performance hypotheses remain falsifiable; only the live measurement resolves them. |
