## Blocking

### B1. Only a probabilistic test covers the gate's BEP 27 and remote-unverified refusal
`crates/pipeline/src/identify.rs:273`, `crates/pipeline/tests/identify.rs:545-618` (`each_refusal_has_its_reason`) and `:450-540` (the proptest).
- **The code is right.** `PRIVACY` = `PRIVATE | PRIVATE_SOURCE | REMOTE_UNVERIFIED`.
- **The gap:** the only deterministic gate test of privacy uses `flags::PRIVATE_SOURCE`. `PRIVATE` (BEP 27) and `REMOTE_UNVERIFIED` reach the gate only through the proptest. That test draws `bep27` at weight 0.2, behind eight other 0.2-weighted refusals and the 0.8-weighted plugin-wide switches, over 32 cases with a random seed.
- **What I measured:** with M1 applied (exactly the PRIVATE_SOURCE-only mask a sibling slice just shipped), the suite passed 2 runs of 3.
- **Failure scenario:** a later edit narrows the mask to `PRIVATE_SOURCE`. CI goes green about two times in three. BEP 27 private torrents' titles are then sent to Wikidata or TMDB, which is a Q5 and §18.7 violation.
- **This is new behaviour on the slice's core safety property, so it is blocking under standards §5 ("a missing test for new behaviour").**
- **Fix (a few lines):** in `each_refusal_has_its_reason`, store one torrent with `flags::PRIVATE` and one with `flags::REMOTE_UNVERIFIED`, and assert `Ineligible::PrivateSource` for each. Optionally also feed one through the running runtime in `only_the_parsed_title_and_year_are_sent`.

## Important

### I1. No test exercises the "an ambiguous name sends nothing" guard
`crates/pipeline/src/identify.rs:330-333`.
- **What happens now:** M4 (dropping `named.title.ambiguous ||`) passes every test. The test's `unnamed` item (`"New Folder"`, one `.mkv`) is refused earlier, at `:324`, for its kind.
- **What I checked:** `classify("New Folder", files)` gives `Video` and `ambiguous=true`, and the name-only parse still yields the title `"New Folder"`.
- **The decisions file relies on this guard** ("A name that's ambiguous … sends nothing", `Ineligible::NoQuery`). The runner's option-(a) reasoning (via_plugin is dormant because Q5 sends only names that parse to a title) depends on it too.
- **Failure scenario:** the guard is lost in a refactor. An ambiguous TV or Movie item, such as `New Folder` holding `Show.S01E01.mkv` (type Tv) or `a8f3x.zip.part` over a film, then sends `"New Folder"` or `"a8f3x"` to the plugin. It gets back a confident wrong match, and if via_plugin ever fires, a wrong effective title.
- **Fix:** add an item whose file tree classifies it Movie or Tv under an uninformative name (for example a folder name with an `SxxEyy` file), and assert `NoQuery`.

## Nits
- **N1. The tests depend on the wall-clock UTC day.** See `identify.rs:535`, `:650` (`crate::unix_now()`), and `tests/identify.rs:741`, `:790`.
  - `Budget` counts per UTC day from the real clock. A run of `interest_comes_before_the_capped_backfill` or `a_restart_resumes_the_backfill` that straddles 00:00Z resets the count, and `looked_up == 5` / `["Item2 Plain Film"]` then fail.
  - It's rare, since each test takes under a second. An injectable clock in `Config`, or a test-only `now` hook, would make them deterministic.
- **N2. No per-lookup timeout.** At `identify.rs:703-707`, `plugin.lookup` is raced only against shutdown.
  - A plugin future that never resolves stalls the whole runtime, user interest included, until restart.
  - The client's own timeouts probably bound it, but a `tokio::time::timeout` (for example 60 s, counted as `failed`) costs nothing.
- **N3. A failing plugin spends its whole day.** At `identify.rs:723`, an `Err` is `Step::Asked`, is not cached, and keeps its budget.
  - A plugin that fails persistently (for example a revoked key returning 401) retries the walk at its interval, up to 100k failed requests a day.
  - It's bounded by the cap, but consider a short pause after N consecutive failures.
- **N4. `IdentifyStats::ineligible` also counts store errors and gate panics** (`identify.rs:686-693`). Those aren't refusals, so a status page would misread them. `unreadable` already exists for them.
- **N5. For later slices (not this PR's problem):** `claims_kib` (`unverified.rs`) now charges every details section with a 12 KiB worst-case verified name, from any source.
  - A full section is about 0.6 MB, so about 13 fit in one 8 MiB writer step.
  - That's fine at plugin rates (≤10/s). A future feed that sends v3 sections in bulk (a Torznab with details) would ingest slowly. ENR-11 and the feed slices should know.

## Things checked and found correct
- **What is sent (Q5):** `Query` holds only the title (≤256 B, control characters stripped), the year (1–9999) and the kind. The title comes from a name-only parse (`classify_corrected(name, &[], default)`). The wire test shows no hash (either case), no quality tags and no file extension. `Debug` prints lengths only, and nothing logs.
- **The gate:**
  - The plugin-wide conditions (kill switch, enabled, consent through `consent::need_in`, including the version) are read first, with nothing read about the item while one fails.
  - Then:
    - `stored` (not unverified, not `FILES_PARTIAL`);
    - `PRIVACY` (all three bits);
    - held, through `held_among` plus `blocked_hash`;
    - `HIDDEN`;
    - a drop, quarantine or hide verdict now;
    - adult by stored type, an Is adult correction, an always-adult finder (unless your Not adult correction), an Is adult rule or detection now;
    - pending reclassification;
    - the kind against the plugin's kinds.
  - The gate runs right before every lookup, user and background alike, after the cache check and before the budget.
- **Ingest:**
  - `is_identification` covers every `Claims` field.
  - The match path refuses a private source, a missing torrent or a `PRIVACY` torrent, and `put_claims` refuses a held one.
  - No source bit, no `make_public`, no unverified item.
  - Judged by `judge_in` with the verified name; a core hit holds every swarm hash.
- **Untrusted answers:**
  - `into_claims` goes through `Claims::clamp`: the closed id schemes, alt titles, genres, years and confidence ≤100 are all bounded.
  - The proptest shows the claims are bounded and idempotent under a second clamp.
  - `via_plugin` strips control characters, collapses whitespace and caps confidence at 95.
  - No unwrap, expect or indexing on plugin data. The budget's `used + 1` is guarded by `left > 0`.
  - The `i64_at`/`u32_at` decoders are `get`-based.
- **The plugin can't touch the runtime's own state:** `Guarded` covers all four `PluginState` methods, and the plugin gets only `Kit{state: Guarded, http}` and the `Query`. It can't choose the key it answers for.
- **Concurrency:**
  - The interest queue is bounded (64, `try_send`), the background queue is ≤ one page (1024), and the emitter applies backpressure, raced against shutdown.
  - Store access is a dedicated `Arc<Mutex<Store>>` inside `spawn_blocking`, with poisoning tolerated. There's no lock nesting.
  - One run at a time holds the interest receiver.
  - Every wait races shutdown.
- **Restart and resume:** the cursor never passes a queued item, the budget fails closed, a clock stepped back can't hold the count, and the cache's worst case is 256 × 64 KiB (16 MiB), inside the state quota.
- **The runner calls:**
  - `ResolvedVia::Plugin` stays out of `ALL` and `from_name`, and is read by `from_stored` in `record.rs`.
  - `with_identified` re-titles only ambiguous names and keeps the type, kind and junk score.
  - The cap is shared by both lookup kinds.
  - The four "never a find" tests and the consent-version test are present and meaningful.
- **Tests:** no fixed sleeps. `until` polls every 5 ms with a 10 s deadline, and the only other sleeps pace work.
