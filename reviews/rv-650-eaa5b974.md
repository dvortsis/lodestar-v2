## Findings

### 1. LOW (correctness, lock/lease). The 25-minute lease check doesn't bound deliver-pia: the job can run up to its 30-minute timeout
- **Where:**
  - `infra/pia-bridge/deliver.py:75` (`LEASE_NEEDED = timedelta(minutes=25)`) and `:418`;
  - `.github/workflows/fleet.yml:208` (`timeout-minutes: 30`);
  - the claim in `docs/decisions/2026-11-09-OP-2b.md:25`: "That's its worst case… So a delivery can't outlive the lock it runs under, whatever the runners' backlog."
- **Failure path.** The check runs once, at the start. Nothing inside the job is tied to the lease. Adding up the job's own bounds:
  - **SSH:** a 255 near the end of the 120 s retry window, then one session of `SSH_TIMEOUT` = 870 s, gives about 990 s. A lost-session clean poll adds 200 s, plus a last 60 s attempt. That's about 21 minutes.
  - **Before SSH:** about 31 timeout-bounded calls, about 30 s each:
    - OIDC, then STS, S3 and Secrets Manager ×2, each with botocore's 3 attempts × (10 + 30) s;
    - checkip and `/meta`;
    - per firewall: list, get, any sweep lookups, list and put.
  - **The close:** about 6 more such calls.

  With slow or failing APIs, the job passes 25 minutes and is cut only at 30. If it starts with 25 to 30 minutes of lease left, the lease ends while it is still SSHing or closing its rule.
- **What then happens.** Another `/fleet` command can take the lock: a `down`, or a second `renew-vpn` chain. The two deliver-pia jobs' whole-rule-set read-modify-writes can then interleave. One job's `/32` is dropped, so its SSH fails, or a closed rule comes back until the next sweep. A `down` can also destroy the targets mid-delivery. Each fails closed (an availability problem, or a rule left to the hourly sweep), but it contradicts the decisions file.
- **Fix (two lines and a test).**
  - Set `LEASE_NEEDED` to at least deliver-pia's `timeout-minutes` plus a margin, for example `timedelta(minutes=31)`.
  - Add a `test_workflow` assertion that `LEASE_NEEDED >= jobs["deliver-pia"]["timeout-minutes"]`, so the two can't drift.
  - It's still feasible: 90 - 50 (act's worst case) - 4 (pia-fence) leaves 36 minutes before queue time. Alternatively, clamp each SSH timeout and the clean poll to `lease_end - now - close_margin`. Either way, correct line 25.

### 2. LOW (correctness, secret hygiene). After a session dropped with exit 255, the `/run` clean check can pass before the orphaned bootstrap has unpacked the login
- **Where:**
  - `infra/pia-bridge/deliver.py:74` (`CLEAN_WAIT_SECONDS = FLOCK_WAIT + 20`, "the orphan's own bound");
  - `:331-336` and `:344-353`;
  - the bootstrap's order at `:100-116`;
  - the claim in `docs/decisions/2026-11-09-OP-2b.md:78`.
- **Failure path.**
  1. ServerAlive drops the session (15 s × 4) while the remote bootstrap is still in `cloud-init status --wait` (up to 480 s) or waiting on the flock (up to 180 s). The payload is about 10 KB and was sent at session start, so it already sits in the pipe buffer, and sshd closing the channel only adds EOF after it.
  2. **The poll passes too soon.** `/run/lodestar-pia` doesn't exist yet, so the first clean check returns 0 and the poll stops. Clean means rc 0, not "the orphan is gone". The report says "/run clean".
  3. **The orphan runs anyway.** It finishes its wait, takes the flock, unpacks `login.json` into `/run/lodestar-pia`, and runs the step. The login sits there for up to 150 s after the job declared the VM clean, and its traps then delete it. If the 255 came inside the 120 s retry window, the retry's successful session runs `_clean(wait=False)` with the same false positive. The orphan then registers a second key and replaces `wg0.conf`.
- **Exposure.** It's bounded: a root-only tmpfs directory on the fleet's own VM, cleaned by the trap, and OP-3's leak test checks again. But the "past the orphan's own bound" premise is wrong for 255. The orphan's bound runs from its own start: up to 480 + 180 + 150 s, not 200 s after the drop. The 256 case (a session timeout at 870 s, after that bound) is sound.
- **Fix, either of these:**
  - **(a) Make "clean" mean no delivery is in flight.**
    - Take the per-VM flock first in the bootstrap, before the cloud-init wait. An orphan then holds it from its first instant.
    - The clean check becomes `sudo -n flock -n /run/lodestar-pia.lock test ! -e /run/lodestar-pia`: busy or present reads as not clean.
    - After a 255, poll until the lost attempt's start plus the orphan's full bound. Include that in the lease budget (finding 1).
  - **(b) Keep the code and state the residual accurately in the decisions file.** Treat "/run clean" after a 255 as provisional, and leave the real check to OP-3's leak test.

### 3. LOW (tests). A guard that blocks root command injection on the VM, and several other guards, have no test
- **Where:** `infra/pia-bridge/vm/pia-wireguard.sh:80` and `:63`.
- **Why it matters.** At `:80`, `$server_port =~ ^[0-9]{1,5}$` short-circuits before `$server_port -ge 1`. Bash's `[[ -ge ]]` evaluates its operand arithmetically, so a value like `a[$(cmd)]` runs `cmd`. I confirmed this locally: without the regex, `p='a[$(touch pwned)]'; [[ $p -ge 1 ]]` creates the file. The step runs as root.
- **Failure path.** A refactor that drops or reorders the regex passes every test (the mutant `vm_no_portcheck` survived). A hostile or compromised `addKey` answer then gets root command execution, or a `PostUp` line in `wg0.conf`.
  - The answer comes over TLS pinned to PIA's CA, so this needs PIA's side compromised. That's why it's LOW, but the guard should be pinned.
  - The `wg_cn` check (`:63`, mutant `vm_no_cn_check`) is untested too. `--cacert` is the real defense there.
- **Other surviving mutants, all benign today:**
  - **The bootstrap's secrets-directory check** (`deliver.py:105`).
  - **The step's temp-file cleanup** (`pia-wireguard.sh:24`).
  - **`_ours`** (`lock.py:170-171`): dropping either `kind == "run"` or the expiry condition survives. It's unreachable today, since the takers' run IDs are disjoint, but S1's spec names "a live run holder".
  - **`expires_at`'s expiry check** (`lock.py:185`). deliver-pia's lease check also refuses a past lease.
  - **pia-fence with a tombstone** (`fleet.py:477`, the `fleet is None` arm). deliver-pia refuses "no fleet".
  - **pia-release's attempt check** (`fleet.py:497`). A re-run's ID releases nothing, and the job's `if:` gates it too.
- **Fix:**
  - Add cases to `test_bad_inputs_fail_closed`, each asserting exit 16 or 13, no side effect and the work directory gone:
    - `server_port` set to `"a[$(touch <tmp>/pwned)]"`, to `"1337\nPostUp = x"`, to `0` and to `70000`;
    - `cn` set to `"evil.example:443/x"`.
  - Optionally add one case each for `_ours` with a reaping marker and an expired own holder, pia-fence on a tombstone, and the bootstrap with the secrets directory missing (exit 20).

### 4. LOW (correctness). Flipping `FLEET_LIVE` mid-chain strands the live lock, contrary to the decisions file
- **Where:**
  - `.github/workflows/fleet.yml:267` (pia-release's env `FLEET_LIVE: ${{ vars.FLEET_LIVE }}`) and `:263`;
  - `infra/fleet/fleet.py:497-501`;
  - the claim in `docs/decisions/2026-11-09-OP-2b.md:26`: "changing one mid-chain … can't strand the lock".
- **Failure path.** act runs live and keeps the S3 lock. The owner sets `FLEET_LIVE=false` before pia-release starts, for example as a stop. Then:
  - pia-fence and deliver-pia take their dry-run paths;
  - pia-release prints "would release this run's fleet lock" and never deletes the S3 lock;
  - the lock stays until its 90-minute expiry, and a re-enabled operator gets "busy" until then.

  It fails closed, and the test only checks the `if:` for `vars.`.
- **Fix.** Take the mode from act rather than a variable. act writes `live=true|false` beside `deliver=true`, and pia-fence and pia-release use `FLEET_LIVE: ${{ needs.act.outputs.live }}`. deliver-pia can keep the variable, since dry-run there is the safe direction. Or narrow the decisions sentence to the `if:`.

## Nits (no action needed)
- **N1. `_ours`'s docstring** says "(or a failed read) isn't" ours (`lock.py:168`). In fact `S3Store.get` raises `RuntimeError`, and `acquire` (outside act's `try`) propagates it. That's the same as the first `get` already did, so it's no regression.
- **N2. The end-to-end bootstrap test runs the host's real `cloud-init status --wait`** whenever `cloud-init` is on PATH (`test_deliver.py:472-474`; the bootstrap at `deliver.py:100`). A `cloud-init` stub in `stub/bin` that exits 0 would make it hermetic. CI is green today.
- **N3. One target's unexpected exception loses the other target's report.** In `Delivery.run`, an exception such as `OSError` from one target's `to_target` aborts `pool.map`, so the other target's delivered-or-clean line is never reported. Only the close line and "unexpected error" are. Consider catching per target and returning a fixed "unexpected error" line for it.
- **N4. Masking short region IDs hides those strings everywhere in the job's log.** Masking `PIA_SERVERS` values follows the brief, but a region ID that happens to be a short common string would be hidden all through the log. It's cosmetic.
