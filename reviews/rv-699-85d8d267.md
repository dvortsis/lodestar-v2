# Review: PR #699 (WK-11, the worker spool) at 85d8d267

**Verdict: CHANGES.** There is one blocking finding, and it's small to fix. The format, the decoder, durability, replay idempotency and the Q7 refusal are sound, and the mutation tests back them.

Scope (runner-approved) and size weren't judged. Sealing and crypto were left to the security reviewer, apart from what's noted below.

## Blocking

**B1. With a session up, a full spool pauses the pool, but claiming and DHT sampling carry on.**
- Where: `crates/lodestar/src/worker/runtime.rs:1107` (`want_dht`), `:1714` (the claim loop's gate) and `:1834` (the `check` gate).
- What's wrong: `full` only feeds `refresh_pause`, which pauses the pool. `want_dht` returns `Desired::Run` whenever a session is up and it isn't fresh. The claim gates check only `sealing_down || !fetching()`.
- The brief's gate says "caps hold (... fetching pauses, DHT keeps routing)". Plan §WK-11 says "full means fetching pauses and the DHT keeps only routing".
- The decisions file (line 44) says this "already holds". That's true only while no session is up.
- Failure scenario:
  1. A worker reconnects with a spool at the cap. This is the one case where `full` is set while a session is up.
  2. The DHT runs at full intensity, and `claim` keeps getting grants up to `pool_cap`, which is 50,000 leases.
  3. Those keys sit in a paused pool. The coordinator sees them as held, and the worker's heartbeats renew them.
  4. No one fetches them until the replay brings the spool under half its cap. Under writer backpressure that can take many minutes.
  5. This also makes WK-11's lease-drift counter noisy.
- Fix:
  - Treat `sp.is_full()` like `!fetching()` in both claim gates (held back and counted).
  - Return `Desired::Pause` from `want_dht` while full.
  - Call `want_dht` wherever `refresh_pause` is called after a removal.
  - Add a test: full with a session up means no claims are sent, and the DHT is paused or only routing.
  - Correct line 44 of the decisions file.

## Important

**I1. Nothing tests that a segment survives a "refused for now" answer.**
- Where: `crates/lodestar/src/worker/spool.rs:564`.
- The code is correct: only `ok` reaches `replay.accepted`, at `runtime.rs` around line 2090.
- A mutation that also removes the segment on `accepted: false` passes every test. In `batches_spooled_offline_are_replayed_once_then_removed`, the retried record is accepted later and the end state is the same.
- Real-world effect of such a bug: the segment is deleted while a record is only refused for now. If the session then drops, `flight.retain(|f| f.from.is_none())` discards that record, so it's lost.
- Fix: after the `accepted: false` answer, wait for the retry to arrive, then assert the segment file is still there (`sp.bytes() > 0`) before the final accept.

**I2. The wire-key-day reasoning has a gap: a record's age runs from when it was spooled, not when it was sealed.**
- Where: `worker/spool.rs:246` sets `at: unix_now()` at `put`. Decisions file, line 61.
- The decision says "a record sealed on day N is therefore replayed on day N or N + 1", and that clock skew is the only edge.
- But a batch sealed on day N can wait in memory before it's spooled:
  - in flight across midnight;
  - refused for now on repeat;
  - or kept in memory because the spool was full. Offline, that's unbounded in time: `spool_live` never ages these.
- Such a batch is stamped day N + 1 and can be replayed on day N + 2. It then ends as `unopened`, which means `Invalid`.
- It is counted, and its lease expired long ago, so no key is lost silently, and the decision's outcome still holds. Its stated proof doesn't.
- Fix, either:
  - carry the batch's creation or seal time in `InFlight` and use it as `at`. That also measures the 24 h from the fetch, which is stricter for content safety;
  - or amend the decision text to name this edge.

**I3. Damage is counted but not logged, and the follow-up isn't queued.**
- The brief says "counted, logged by name only".
- The decisions file (line 12) defers the log event to "a slice that owns those files". No `slices.yaml` entry exists for it.
- The runner should queue it, or this is a silent gap against the brief.

**I4. The 24 h cap uses the wall clock only.**
- Where: `worker/spool.rs:408`, `pipeline/src/spool.rs:942` and `:987`.
- **Clock set backwards** (by NTP, or a user): records keep their old `at`, so they sit on disk past 24 h by the size of the jump. That breaks the owner's wkq1 cap on how long a sealed name may stay on disk.
- **Clock jumps forward** (a Pi with no clock chip starts at its last saved time, then NTP corrects it): everything spooled before the jump is dropped as old, and counted.
- `at > now` is never questioned.
- Suggestions:
  - treat a record or segment stamped more than a minute in the future as suspect, and age it from the file's mtime or from first sight in this process (monotonic);
  - or at least say in the decision that a clock set back extends the cap.

## Nit

- **N1.** A length field damaged mid-segment so that it points past the end of the file reads as `Torn`, not `Corrupt` (`pipeline/src/spool.rs:387-389`, confirmed with a crafted input). The frames after it are dropped either way, but they're counted as one torn write rather than as damage.
- **N2.** A failed sync can spool a batch twice.
  - `append` returns `Err` after the frame was written if the cadence sync fails (`spool.rs:845-846`).
  - `put` then returns false, the batch stays in memory and is spooled again, so there are two copies on disk.
  - On replay the coordinator answers the second copy as stale, but the worker's `committed` counter counts it twice.
  - The spool also keeps appending to a file after an fsync failure. Consider closing the segment on a sync error.
- **N3.** The `MAX_FRAME` check in `decode_segment` has no test, and its mutation survives. That's harmless for memory: frames are slices of a capped read, and the seal and record caps back it.
  - The `MAX_PLAIN` check in `Record::decode` is redundant with the lease, payload and trailing-bytes checks. Its mutation also survives.
- **N4.** `a_torn_final_record_is_dropped_not_half_used` still passes if a torn frame is handed to the opener, because AEAD rejects it. Only the proptest `a_torn_segment_yields_only_whole_frames` catches that mutation. Assert the frame count, or that `damage` has no `seal` entry.
- **N5.** Windows: an NTFS rename isn't guaranteed durable when it returns, because the log is flushed lazily (`spool.rs:636`, decisions line 25).
  - A power cut right after `start_segment` can leave the frames in `<seq>.tmp`, which is removed at open.
  - Only records the coordinator never acknowledged are lost.
  - Use `MOVEFILE_WRITE_THROUGH` or reword the claim.
- **N6.** `Spool::open` fails as a whole, leaving the worker without a spool, when one leftover `.tmp` or foreign segment can't be removed (`spool.rs:720`, `:731`). Antivirus holding the file on Windows is an example. Skip that file and count it, as `read` and `remove` do with `forget`.
- **N7.** `docs/metrics.md:969`:
  - `damaged` counts events, not records: the frames after a corrupt one aren't counted, yet the table's header says "Records dropped".
  - `WorkerSpool::errors` and `unavailable` have no rows.
  - `drop_expired` can count frames as old that were already counted as torn or damaged.
- **N8.** `sp.next()` makes a `spawn_blocking` call and takes the mutex on every pass of the session loop while the spool is empty (`runtime.rs:2034`). Skip it when `bytes() == 0` and no segment is active.
- **N9.** `remote.rs:613`: a refused worker's current-epoch records that don't open still end their leases as `Invalid`, while the ones that do open are requeued. That's inconsistent with the intent of the `requeue_back` refinement.
- **N10.** Replay holds a whole segment in memory: about 9 MiB read, plus about 8 MiB of decoded records, plus a copy of each payload as it's sent. That isn't in WK-10's RSS breakdown under Q6.
- **N11.** The refusal gate test covers only the current epoch. The earlier-epoch case is WK-9's `remote_intake` test, which still passes, so it's covered. Just noting it.

## Checks against the brief

1. **The decoder:**
   - Bounded: lengths are checked against the bytes left and the caps before any allocation. `Vec::with_capacity(n)` comes only after `n <= MAX_LEASES` and `n * 28` bytes are present. The segment read is capped at about 9 MiB.
   - A torn final frame is dropped. Mid-segment damage stops the segment and is counted (but see I3 and N1).
   - The header is versioned: magic, version, three zero bytes and a checksum.
2. **Durability:**
   - A new segment is written as a temp file, synced, renamed, and the folder synced on Unix.
   - Appends are synced at the end of each `spool_live` round, plus a 1 s cadence inside `append`.
   - Crash at each step:
     - During creation: a leftover `.tmp`, removed at open.
     - Mid-append: a torn tail, dropped.
     - During removal: zeroing, fsync, unlink, then the folder sync. A crash after the zero-and-fsync leaves zeros, so the header is invalid and the file is removed at open. A crash mid-overwrite can at worst replay records that were already accepted, which the coordinator answers as stale.
   - No acknowledged record is lost on the worker side. Note that "accepted" means queued at the coordinator (WK-9).
   - Windows: no file is renamed over an open one. Rust's default share modes allow it. Durability caveat in N5.
3. **Caps:**
   - `Caps::new` clamps to 1 GB and 24 h. `unclamped` is `#[doc(hidden)]` and used in tests only. There's no setting.
   - The pause behaviour is B1.
   - Records too old are dropped and counted, never replayed. The clock is I4.
4. **Replay:**
   - In order: oldest segment first, and batch ids rising within one.
   - Idempotent: frame positions are remembered across sessions, and the coordinator dedupes by token (stale) or by `prior.remove` for late results.
   - A segment is deleted only after every frame is `done`. Done means accepted, too old or damaged.
   - Backpressure retries on the tick. Heartbeats run on the driver's own timer.
   - Q7: `refused` is checked before the epoch split, and purge checks are on both paths.
5. **Wire key:** no key is kept longer than needed, and no key is lost silently. The reasoning gap is I2.
6. **No peer addresses:** the record holds only keys, tokens, the batch id, `at` and the wire payload. The test opens the record and re-encodes it in clear, and a mutation that leaks the peer is killed.
7. **`--rotate-key` reseals the spool before the retire.**
   - A crash mid-reseal: each segment's rewrite is atomic, and the retire comes only after `needs_reseal` is false. A crash leaves both keys, so nothing is unopenable, and a re-run finishes the job.
   - Unjoined (`worker` is `None`): nothing is resealed, then the old key is retired. Only an orphan spool from an earlier membership could be affected, and it would be dropped as foreign anyway.
8. **Concurrency:**
   - Generation-based reset: an `Accepted` from an old session for a replayed batch finds no entry and is ignored, and that record is resent and answered stale. Batch ids are never reused.
   - No lock is held across an await: the std mutex is taken only inside `spawn_blocking`.
   - No unbounded queues are added. `Replay.accepted`, `counted` and `old` are bounded by the segments.
   - Duplication only through N2.
9. **Metrics:** `spool_bytes` is in the heartbeat stats. The doc rows mostly match (N7).
10. **Tests:**
    - Waits use `until` and `timeout` with deadlines of 15 to 20 s. No fixed sleeps; the only sleep is a 10 ms poll inside a deadline.
    - `cargo tree -e features -p lodestar` shows no tokio `test-util`, and no dht, sources or companions `testing` feature. This is CI's pattern; the `lodestar-update` and `tracker-scrape` `testing` features were already on main.
    - Every gate has a test, except the "DHT keeps routing" part (B1) and the backpressure branch (I1).

## Flagged refinements (for the runner to flag to the owner)

- **`requeue_back` (WK-9 refinement): sound.**
  - `take_back` releases only under the exact token. An `in_flight`/`held` race is harmless.
  - The key waits at the back. No loss is counted and nothing is harvested again, so the funnel still reconciles. The test checks the key is pending and not held.
  - It depends on a refused worker never getting the key back. Quarantine has to pause it; that's WK-13's "seed the pause set" note. Revoked workers can't connect.
  - Small inconsistency: N9.
- **Offline behaviour (§19.6 refinement): sound, but tell the owner what it costs.**
  - The reasons hold: WK-5's re-plan of per-IP shares, and the 10-minute lease TTL, which means most fetches made offline would come back stale.
  - What the owner should hear: offline, the spool only collects what was in flight when the session went. That's at most the 8 MiB outbound budget plus the fetches still finishing.
  - So §19.6's "keeps harvesting ... into a 1 GB / 24 h spool" doesn't happen in v1. The 1 GB cap is a backstop, and 24 h is the cap that actually applies.
  - It also leaves B1 as the only path to a full spool with a session up.
