Reviewer: a fresh reviewer, delta only. Release builds on the shared 4-core container. Times are best of 15–30 runs, interleaved with the baseline, re-measured whenever load spiked (load at times went over 3, which doubled single samples).

**Verdict: MERGE.** No blocking findings. B3 is closed, B2 stays closed and I3 is relieved. A few nits follow.

## 1. Does the cost model bound parse+fold time? Yes

The previous review's shapes are refused in microseconds: `[\x{80}-\x{FFFF}]`×7 and ×19, the 8-deep `[[[[[[[[\x{41}-\x{1E943}]a]…]`×2, and `[[[[\x{41}-\x{1E943}]a]a]a]`.

I ran three scratch searches; none of the probes are committed:
- **Unit fill.** I generated about 7,000 random units: nested brackets up to 4 deep with negation, ranges on boundary points (0x41, 0x1E943, the Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Deseret, Adlam and Garay blocks, surrogates), properties, Perl and ASCII items, `--`/`&&`/`~~` operands, and `(?i:)`/`(?-i:)` wrappers. I binary-searched each one to its largest accepted repeat and timed it.
- **Mixed fill.** 4,000 patterns, each built by greedily appending random units, with `|` mixed in, up to the length, node and fold limits.
- **ns/unit ranking.** I added a scratch hook to read the fold cost, then ranked translate time per unit.

Results:
- **Worst ratio.** Re-timed quietly, every candidate came in at 1.0–1.1× the baseline. The "16–19 ms" outliers in the single samples all fell back to about 10.5 ms when re-timed.
- **Per-unit rate.** About 2.3–2.5 ns a unit for full-table and outside-table walks alike, so the 10:1 in-table weighting holds.
- **50 ms.** Nothing accepted came near it. The worst case is about 12 ms parse, so roughly 24 ms end to end with RX-1's re-fold.

## 2. Is the model sound against regex-syntax 0.8.11? Yes

I read `hir/translate.rs`, `hir/interval.rs`, `hir/mod.rs` (range fold) and `unicode.rs` (`SimpleCaseFolder::mapping`/`overlaps`). Folding happens at these call sites, and the model covers each one:
- `hir_unicode_class`: fold then negate. Charged by `unicode_item` on the un-negated set, exactly. `is_negated()` covers `\P`, `\p{^X}` and `!=`.
- `hir_perl_unicode_class`: never folds. Correct for `(?i)` too.
- Bracket post, top-level and nested: `unicode_fold_and_negate` on the whole set. Charged by `bracketed`.
- Set-op post: both operands folded. Charged in `class_set`.
- `hir_ascii_unicode_class`: folds the ASCII item itself. **Not charged** (nit N1).
- `case_fold_char` (top-level literals): microseconds, not charged.

Containment holds. The translator's real set is always contained in the model set unioned with `cased()`:
- folding only adds cased images, and those are keys, which the unit test proves are in `cased()`;
- negating a folded set gives a subset of the negated unfolded set;
- `&&`, `--` and `~~` of folded operands stay inside the model's result unioned with `cased`.

Every real range lies inside a widened range, so the walk cost is monotone. The only slack is the 1-unit per-range charge, against about 20 ns real for `overlaps` plus the folder. It's bounded by range counts and the weight limit: `\p{Cn}`×68 runs in under 3 ms. The `folded` flag (`IntervalSet::folded`) only ever makes the real work cheaper (`[[:^alpha:]]` is about 0 ms real and fully charged), so it over-charges, never under-charges.

The `(?i)` toggles are ignored by the model, which always charges. `(?-i)` therefore over-charges, which is safe. Flags other than `i` are refused, so `(?-u)` byte classes can't arise.

## 3. B2 stays closed

The charges:
- `[\D--a]` costs 1,990,113 units: the `\D∪cased` operand fold plus the bracket's fold of the result. Real is 2.0–2.9 ms each, so it's charged at or above the real cost. ×2 is accepted (4–6 ms) and ×3 is refused.
- `[\S]` ×2 is accepted (about 11 ms) and ×3 refused.
- `[^\D]` and `[\D]` ×4 are accepted (about 8 ms).
- `[[^a]b]` and `[b[^a]]` ×1 are accepted.
- `[[:^alpha:]]` ×2 is accepted (really about 0 ms).
- Nested negations such as `[^[\S]\W]` and `[^\s[^\S.]\S]` are at the baseline.

I3's relief comes from the model computing the real sets. There's no blanket exemption, so it reopens nothing.

## 4. Arithmetic

- **Bounds.** Fold and weight use `saturating_add`. `fold_cost` subtractions are guarded:
  - `inside ≤ width` through `.min(width)`;
  - `b ≥ a` because table ranges past the partition point overlap `[start, end]`;
  - `end.min(hi).saturating_sub(…)`.
  
  The totals are at most about 2.3e7 a range, far from u64 overflow.
- **Panic loop.** 30,000 random token patterns (brackets, ops, properties, flags, repetitions, up to ×30) ran in a **debug** build with overflow checks on. No panics: 3,988 accepted and 26,012 refused, with the largest fold cost accepted 4,597,156, which is ≤ 4.6M.

## Nits (non-blocking)

- **N1.** The ASCII item's own fold (`hir_ascii_unicode_class` folds `[:alpha:]` before negating) isn't charged. It costs at most about 1.5k units per item and about 75k per pattern (about 1.6% of the budget), so it's harmless. But the docs say every fold is charged. Either charge `fold_cost` of the un-negated ASCII set in `class_item`, or mention it in the docs.
- **N2.** The timing claims disagree slightly:
  - `MAX_FOLD_COST` says "about 7–9 ms to translate" and "units of at most about 2 ns"; I measured about 2.3–2.5 ns a unit here;
  - the test comment says 7–12 ms;
  - the decisions file says 9–11 ms.
  
  `[\w.-]`×51 measured 11–12 ms here, the slowest I found. Suggest "about 9–12 ms" in one place, naming `[\w.-]`×51.
- **N3 (optional usability).** `fold_cost_folded` widens by all of `cased()` even for a bracket or operand whose items are only literals, ranges and Perl classes, none of them folded beforehand. Plain `fold_cost` is exact there. Narrowing the widening would lift the 60-bracket cap on `[^/]`-style patterns. It's not needed for safety.
- **N4.** `fold_cost` treats codepoints below U+0041 as cheap (1 unit), but the folder binary-searches them too (`Err(0)`). That's at most 65×9 units per range, negligible. Using `lo = 0` would make the in-table span exact.
- **N5.** The docs say "Perl classes … are never folded, standing alone or in brackets". The item itself isn't folded, but the enclosing bracket's whole-set fold does walk it, and the model charges that. Suggested wording: "never folded on their own; a bracket around them folds them with its set". In the module doc, the "limit), and node count, …" sentence and the over-long wrapped line are cosmetic.

VERDICT: MERGE
