### N2: `Config::lookup_timeout`, 60 s by default. VERIFIED
- **The code (`identify.rs:715-724`):** `tokio::time::timeout(lookup_timeout, plugin.lookup(..))` runs inside the shutdown `select!`.
  - On `Elapsed` it bumps `failed` and returns `Step::Asked`, so the next lookup waits the interval.
  - The day's count was taken once, by `Budget::take` before the lookup. There's no second charge and no refund.
  - The test asserts the stored `used == 2` for one timeout plus one miss. **It isn't double-charged or left uncharged.**
- **Cancellation:**
  - `timeout` drops the inner future on expiry, and the shutdown arm drops the whole timeout future.
  - The plugin future borrows `kit` and `query` and isn't spawned, so nothing outlives it.
  - On the client path, `HostLimiter::acquire` is a loop over `try_acquire` (a sync lock, never held across an await) plus `sleep_until`, so it's cancel-safe. A token taken before the drop is spent, which errs on the strict side.
  - `http.rs` has no semaphore or permit to leak. A plugin's state write that is already in a `spawn_blocking` finishes on its own, which is harmless.
  - **No task or permit leaks.**
- **The test `a_hung_lookup_times_out`:**
  - It has no fixed sleeps. `until` polls every 5 ms with `DEADLINE` = 10 s.
  - **Mutation M5** (timeout forced to 3600 s): **it failed** with "timed out waiting for the timeout, then the next lookup" after 10.1 s.
  - Unmutated: 3 of 3 passed, each in 2.1 s.
- **Flakiness, worst case on slow CI:**
  - The test limiter is `per_second(1000)`, burst 1, floored to `floors::HOST_INTERVAL` (1 s).
  - The first request takes the free token at t0, then the plugin hangs, and the timeout fires at about t0+2 s.
  - The second lookup starts after the 100 ms interval floor, at t0+2.1 s or later. Its token has been free since t0+1 s, so `acquire` doesn't wait. The fetch is in-memory.
  - So the second lookup uses microseconds of its own 2 s budget. That leaves about 7.9 s of the 10 s deadline as margin.
  - The only failure path is the first `send` taking more than 2 s, so the timeout fires before `hang_first.swap` and the second lookup hangs instead. That needs a scheduler stall of more than 2 s on an in-memory fetch whose token is free.
  - **Not a realistic flake.** Even if the floor were hit, `acquire` would wait at most 1 s, which is less than 2 s.
- **Decisions file:** the new bullet ("A lookup that takes longer than 60 s … counts as failed, already charged to the day's count") matches the code.

### Nits
- **N6. `lookup_timeout` isn't floored in `IdentifySource::new`** (`identify.rs:494-501`).
  - `per_day`, `interval` and `idle` are each clamped "whatever the host sets"; `lookup_timeout` is passed through.
  - **Failure scenario:** a host passes `Duration::ZERO` or a few ms. Then every lookup times out at once, and each spends one of the day's count. That's up to the cap of wasted requests, with no answers.
  - A `.max(Duration::from_secs(1))`, or at least `MIN_TIMEOUT`, would match the other fields.
  - It's dormant: no production caller builds a `Config` other than `Default`.
- **N7. A lookup cut off mid-fetch never reaches `HostLimiter::record`.**
  - `send` calls `record` only after `fetch` returns (`sources/src/http.rs:1223-1228`).
  - The default egress timeout is 30 s, under the 60 s lookup timeout, so normally the egress error is recorded first.
  - **Failure scenario:** a user sets the egress timeout above 60 s (`MAX_TIMEOUT` is 120 s). A host that hangs is then never backed off, and is retried at the plugin's interval within the day's cap.
  - This joins N3's backoff follow-up. Mention it there when that's done.
