## Blocking

**B1. The cap brings back the rejected Karn regression once the round trip rises above 4× the RTO, and the decisions file says the opposite.**

Where:
- `crates/utp/src/ledbat.rs:26` and `:186-190`
- `crates/utp/src/conn.rs:444`
- `docs/decisions/2026-11-12-X-utp-backoff.md:5`
- `crates/utp/src/conn/tests.rs:732-759`

Mechanism: once `backoff >= PROGRESS_BACKOFF_CAP`, a one-packet message works like this:
1. It times out at R = 4·base, where base is MIN_RTO, 500 ms, on a path that had a low srtt.
2. The resend arms 2R.
3. The ACK of the original arrives at the round trip T, with R < T < 2R. That is progress but no sample (transmissions = 2), so `progressed = true`.
4. The next message's timeout is again "after progress", and the backoff stays put.

No timeout without progress between them ever happens, so the backoff never grows past R. No first transmission is ever acknowledged before its timer fires, so no sample ever resets it. The same trap closes at whatever level k has R_k < T < 2·R_k, so it catches every round trip from about 2 s up to MAX_RTO.

The decisions file's line "A bigger jump grows the backoff through consecutive timeouts, which the cap doesn't touch" is wrong for this case. The Sim bound only tests a 1.8 s round trip, below the threshold, and its own comment says it "passes without the cap too". So nothing tests what the cap risks.

Probe: Sim seed 13, 20 one-packet messages of 200 B each, after the round trip jumps from 20 ms. Retransmissions per run:

| round trip | base (bd860547) | this head (24376ffa) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.8 s | 2 | 2 |
| 2.5 s | 3 (2,1,0,…) | 21 (2,1,1,1,…,1) |
| 3 s | 3 | 21 |
| 5 s | 5 | 23 |
| 10 s | 6 | 24 |

On main the timer recovers after two messages. Here every message after the first is resent forever, and LEDBAT takes a spurious `on_timeout` each time. That is the reset-on-progress failure that `2026-11-08-runner-followups-3.md` §2 rejected, moved from "round trip above 1× the RTO" to "above 4×". The workload it hits is the crawler's own: BEP 9/10 requests are small one-packet messages, sent over bufferbloated or mobile paths.

Fix: pick one, and add a Sim test that bounds retransmissions after jumps to 2.5 s, 5 s and 10 s. It should fail on 24376ffa and pass on the fix, for example ≤ 6 resends per 20 messages.
- **(a) Spurious-timeout detection (Eifel-style, RFC 3522) using uTP's echoed timestamp.**
  - What the ACK tells us: `h.timestamp_us.wrapping_sub(h.timestamp_diff_us)` is about our stamp on the packet that triggered the peer's ACK.
  - What to store: keep the micros stamp of each `Sent`'s first and latest transmission.
  - The rule: on an ACK of a retransmitted front whose echoed stamp is before its latest transmission (compare wrap-aware), the timeout was spurious. Take `now - first_sent_at` as a sample, or at least let the next timeout grow past the cap.
  - Why it's safe against a forged echo: `now - first_sent_at` ≥ `now - latest_sent_at`, so a forgery can only raise the RTO (bounded by MAX_RTO), never shrink it.
  - It keeps the lossy-link behaviour: an honest peer acking the resend echoes the resend's stamp, so the cap still applies.
- **(b) Drop the cap.** Keep main's Karn behaviour and land only the other two decisions (fast retransmit of lost resends, and the `MAX_TIMEOUTS` fix). Record that lossy-link compounding for one-packet flows stays as a known limit. #196's flake is already fixed in the test's loss model.
- **(c) Another rule the author prefers,** as long as the new Sim test shows the backoff escapes any R < T < 2R trap.

Then correct the decisions file's first bullet and the comment on `PROGRESS_BACKOFF_CAP` at `ledbat.rs:21-25`.

## Important

None beyond B1. Each of the other checks the brief asked for holds:
- **Doubling and cap.** `back_off(false)` doubles per consecutive timeout. `rto()` shifts by `backoff.min(16)` with `saturating_mul`, then `.min(MAX_RTO)`. `backoff` uses `saturating_add`. No overflow is possible, and `next_order: u64` cannot wrap in practice.
- **Reset only on real progress.** Only `Rtt::sample` resets the backoff, from a first transmission's ACK (Karn), as before.
  - `progressed` is set only when an ACK newly marks a packet. A duplicate ACK, or a selective ACK of packets already acknowledged, marks nothing and sets nothing.
  - An optimistic ACK is real progress from our side. Through `progressed` it can only slow the backoff's growth; it can never shrink the timer.
- **Bounded give-up.** A silent peer, or one that talks with an open window, now counts every timeout. `MAX_TIMEOUTS = 6` closes it after at most about 6 min (backoff ≤ 60 s). The only exemption is a heard peer advertising a window smaller than our oldest unacknowledged packet. It is strictly tighter than main's `!heard` rule, and it is TCP persist behaviour, bounded in memory by the send buffer and in time by the stream's owner and `Config::linger`.
- **Fast retransmit of lost resends.**
  - Repeated or forged selective ACKs can't re-trigger it cheaply. After a resend, `front.order` is the newest order, so only packets sent later count. That bounds a forging peer to one front resend per three new packets of ours.
  - `!front.lost` stops a double count before the resend goes out.
  - Byte accounting (`flight`, `unacked_bytes`) is unchanged and consistent.
- **Timers.**
  - `heard` resets on `arm()`.
  - The timer is re-armed on progress and on the first emit after a timeout.
  - `on_timeout` clears the timer and marks all in flight as lost, and `poll_transmit` resends even into a zero window (`flight == 0`).
  - No lost wakeup and no busy loop: each timeout either closes, or leaves lost packets that `poll_transmit` sends and re-arms on.
- **Tests.**
  - `a_peer_that_talks_but_never_acknowledges_is_given_up_on` fails on main, as the brief's check needs.
  - The fast-retransmit test and the closed-window test exercise the new paths.
  - All are deterministic.

## The three decisions
1. **Cap growth across progress at 4×.** The intent is a runner call: an internal performance tuning, not an owner-set number (§2.26). It loosens no owner or security decision. As recorded, though, it is unsound, and its stated justification is false (B1).
2. **Fast retransmit of lost resends from selective ACKs.** Sound, and a runner call. A forging peer is bounded as described above.
3. **`MAX_TIMEOUTS` counts unless the peer is heard with a window smaller than our oldest packet.** Sound, and a runner call. It tightens the security posture rather than loosening it: a talking but non-acknowledging peer can no longer pin the connection.

None of the three changes an owner-set number, a wire format or a setting.
